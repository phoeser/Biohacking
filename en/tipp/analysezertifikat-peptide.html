Certificate of analysis for peptides: what matters

What a good lab report contains, how to verify it, where you can have a sample tested yourself – and what a test cannot do.

6 min read · 7 sources, checked November 19, 2026

In short

An independent lab test is the only way to know with some confidence what is in a grey-market peptide vial. A useful report states identity, purity, content in milligrams and the batch number – and can be verified with the lab itself. Why this matters is shown by a study of semaglutide bought online: purity was far below the advertised 99 percent, and endotoxin was found in every sample. A good report does not make a product approved or safe, though – it only tells you what was in that one sample.

What a certificate of analysis should contain

  • Identity: Is it the right substance at all? This is checked by mass spectrometry (MS or LC-MS): the measured molecular weight must match the peptide.
  • Purity: What percentage of the material is the intended substance? Measured by HPLC, with a chromatogram attached. One large peak is good; many small side peaks point to impurities or fragments.
  • Content: How many milligrams are actually in the vial? This differs from purity: a vial can be very pure and still contain only half the stated amount – or much more.
  • Endotoxin: Fragments of bacterial walls that can cause fever and inflammation when injected. Measured by LAL test in EU (endotoxin units). Purity says nothing about it.
  • Sterility and microbes: Whether live bacteria or fungi are present. Usually takes longest and costs extra.
  • Sample details: Batch number, date, lab name, a photo of the vial and a code to retrieve the report from the lab.

How to check a certificate

  • Verify with the lab. Reputable labs offer a lookup with task number and key, or a QR code. A PDF that cannot be found there is worthless – edited or borrowed reports are common in the grey market.
  • Compare the batch. The batch number in the report must be on your vial. A report on some batch from the same seller says nothing about your vial.
  • Check the date. A two-year-old report is no evidence for today's goods.
  • Look for content in milligrams. "99% purity" alone is not enough. Without an amount you do not know how much is in the vial.
  • Do not forget endotoxin. For anything injected, this is the most important extra test. Most basic panels do not include it.
  • Your own test beats the seller's. A report commissioned by the seller shows their best sample. A vial you send in yourself shows what actually reaches you.

Why this is not overcautious

In 2024, a research team led by Amir Reza Ashraf ordered semaglutide from online sellers without a prescription. Of six orders, three prefilled pens never arrived. The three vials that did arrive were analysed: advertised purity was 99 percent, measured purity 7.7 to 14.4 percent. The amount was 29 to 39 percent above the label, and endotoxin was found in all three samples. No live microbes were found. Approved medicines from a pharmacy are a different matter – but in 2023 counterfeit Ozempic pens also turned up in European wholesale, and the European Medicines Agency issued a warning.

Where you can have a sample tested

Most tests for private individuals go through a few specialised labs that accept samples by post. They usually do not hold an ISO 17025 accreditation for peptide testing, as official testing labs do – Janoshik not at all, Vanguard only for other methods. What they offer are verifiable reports. What a test cannot do

  • It applies only to the sample sent in – not to the next vial and not to the whole batch.
  • It says nothing about whether a substance works or is safe in humans. That is what the topic pages with the BK-Score are for.
  • It does not make an unapproved product legal and does not replace medical supervision.
  • What happens after the test – storage, reconstitution, drawing up – can degrade quality again.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a lab test for a peptide cost?

A basic test covering identity, purity and content costs about 215 to 360 US dollars at the well-known labs. Endotoxin costs about 120 to 210 dollars extra depending on the lab, sterility about 150 to 290 dollars. Shipping comes on top.

Is the seller's certificate of analysis enough?

Only if it can be verified with the lab and the batch number matches your vial. Even then it shows a sample the seller chose. A test you send in yourself is more meaningful.

What is the difference between purity and content?

Purity is the share of the intended substance in the material, in percent. Content is the actual amount in milligrams. A vial can be 99 percent pure and still contain much less or more than stated.

Why does endotoxin matter?

Endotoxins are bacterial fragments that can cause fever and inflammation when injected. A high purity value does not rule them out, which is why they are measured separately.
