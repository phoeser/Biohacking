Air quality and particulate matter

Particulate matter tops the Global Burden of Disease analysis 2021 with 8 percent of all healthy life years lost, ahead of high blood pressure and smoking, and there is no harmless threshold. The strongest source in your own home is the stove. HEPA filters lower particulate matter and blood pressure; no one has measured hard endpoints.

The largest risk factor in the current global health balance ranks ahead of high blood pressure and smoking and cannot be put into a capsule: particulate matter. How much of it reaches your home is something you can influence, and what a filter device achieves in this respect is well studied.

In short

Particulate matter tops the Global Burden of Disease analysis 2021 with 8 percent of all healthy life years lost, ahead of high blood pressure and smoking. There is no threshold below which it becomes harmless: in the US Medicare analysis, the effect was even steeper in the cleanest regions. Indoors, where we spend around 87 percent of our time, the strongest source is your own stove. HEPA devices clearly lower the concentration and lower systolic blood pressure by about 2 to 4 millimeters of mercury. Whether they prevent heart attacks or deaths, no one has measured.

Why particulate matter ranks at the very top

The Global Burden of Disease analysis 2021 in the Lancet compared 88 risk factors. At the very top is particulate matter, with 8 percent of all healthy life years lost worldwide, followed by high blood pressure with 7.8 and smoking with 5.7 percent. The WHO estimates 4.2 million premature deaths in 2019 from outdoor air alone, 89 percent of them in poorer countries.

That is a global figure. Does it also apply at Central European levels? On this there is an analysis in the New England Journal of Medicine from 2017: the entire American Medicare population, just under 61 million people over 65, over 13 years. For every 10 micrograms more particulate matter per cubic meter in the annual mean, all-cause mortality rose by 7.3 percent.

The second part of the result is the decisive one. In the regions below 12 micrograms, that is, with clean air by American standards, the effect did not disappear. It was steeper: 13.6 percent per 10 micrograms. According to these data, there is no threshold below which particulate matter becomes harmless.

Where Germany stands

In 2021 the WHO halved its guideline value for the annual mean to 5 micrograms; the EU is lowering its limit value from 25 to 10 from 2030. The mean exposure in the urban background was most recently 9 micrograms, according to the German Environment Agency (Umweltbundesamt); the EU limit value is met everywhere.

The WHO guideline value of 5 micrograms, on the other hand, was exceeded in 2024 by 97 percent of all monitoring stations, that is, practically all of them. We live precisely in the range in which the Medicare curve is steepest.

What the dust does in the body

PM 2.5 refers to particles smaller than 2.5 micrometers. They are small enough for the alveoli, and the smallest pass from there into the blood. The lung is not the main victim: according to the WHO breakdown, 68 percent of deaths are due to heart attack and stroke and only 4 percent to lung cancer. It is a vascular disease.

The strongest source is in the kitchen

According to a large American activity study, we spend around 87 percent of our time in buildings. Indoor air is the outdoor air that comes in, plus what we produce ourselves. The strongest source in a normal home is cooking. A measurement study from Seattle with standardized searing found peaks of 200 to 1,400 micrograms per cubic meter in the kitchen and living room. Depending on ventilation, it takes 1 to 10 hours for this to subside.

With a gas stove, nitrogen dioxide is added. A Stanford group measured in 32 homes: without a range hood, a small kitchen can exceed the American one-hour limit within minutes. Candles are the third source; a Düsseldorf study with 55 volunteers found an indication of a small decrease in lung function after 2 hours of candle smoke. The street outside the window is mainly an ultrafine particle problem: ultrafine particles halve within the first 150 meters.

What a filter device has to achieve

The key figure is the Clean Air Delivery Rate, or CADR: clean air per hour. The US Environmental Protection Agency calculates around 110 cubic meters per hour for 9 square meters. A bedroom of 20 square meters therefore needs about 240. Devices from about 150 euros often manage this, but only on the high setting, and that is loud. On the night setting, only a fraction of it remains.

A particle filter cannot remove gases, neither nitrogen dioxide from the stove nor carbon dioxide. On this there is a blinded Harvard study with 24 office workers over 6 days in a climate chamber: for every 400 parts per million more carbon dioxide, performance in a decision-making test fell by 21 percent; the worst condition was 1,400, the best around 500. It was funded by the air-conditioning group United Technologies; the effects are so large that they should be read with caution. The remedy costs nothing: open the window.

What is well supported

Best supported is the risk factor itself. The Medicare analysis of just under 61 million people over 13 years is one of the largest data bases in environmental medicine; it shows no threshold, and Germany is above the WHO guideline value almost everywhere. Secondly, it is established that HEPA devices lower the concentration: in Shanghai by 57 percent, in Detroit from 15 to 7 micrograms. And thirdly, blood pressure moves with it, consistently in several randomized studies and two meta-analyses, by 2 to 4 millimeters of mercury systolic.

What the studies show

Medicare cohort study, New England Journal of Medicine 2017

Harvard analysis of the entire American Medicare population, just under 61 million people over 65, over 13 years. For every 10 micrograms more particulate matter per cubic meter in the annual mean, all-cause mortality rose by 7.3 percent. In the regions below 12 micrograms, the association was not weaker but steeper, namely 13.6 percent per 10 micrograms.

Shanghai 2015, randomized and double-blind

Device versus dummy in dormitory rooms, 35 students. 48 hours of filter operation lowered particulate matter by 57 percent, from 96 to 41 micrograms per cubic meter. Several inflammatory markers in the blood fell markedly, systolic blood pressure by just under 3 percent.

Detroit 2018, double-blind at low exposure

40 older women and men in a social housing complex, 3 days per condition. Baseline exposure was already low at 15 micrograms per cubic meter and fell to 7 with a HEPA filter. Systolic blood pressure fell by 3.2 millimeters of mercury.

Two meta-analyses on blood pressure

A systematic review from 2020 with 10 studies and 604 participants found an average of minus 4 millimeters of mercury systolic. A second meta-analysis of 14 crossover studies arrived at minus 2.3 millimeters and explicitly rates the certainty of the evidence as very low.

Ulaanbaatar, largest study on a hard endpoint

540 pregnant women, HEPA device versus no intervention, target outcome birth weight. Particulate matter fell by 29 percent, considerably less than the 57 percent in the dormitory trial. Birth weight did not change.

Where the data stop

For hard endpoints there is nothing. No randomized study shows that an air purifier lowers heart attacks, strokes or mortality. What is available is a surrogate: blood pressure and inflammatory markers over days instead of events over years, in a few hundred people. The second meta-analysis itself rates this evidence as very uncertain and warns against advertising claims. The largest study with a hard endpoint, 540 pregnant women in Ulaanbaatar, showed no effect on birth weight.

How to do it

Start at the source: switch on the range hood and open the window when frying. In the Seattle measurement, standardized searing reached peaks of 200 to 1,400 micrograms per cubic meter, and depending on ventilation it took 1 to 10 hours for this to subside. If you have a gas stove, nitrogen dioxide is added, which pushes a small kitchen without a hood above the American one-hour limit within minutes.

You size a HEPA device by its clean air delivery rate, CADR: the US Environmental Protection Agency calculates around 110 cubic meters per hour per 9 square meters, so about 240 for a bedroom of 20 square meters, which devices from around 150 euros often only manage on the loudest setting. In the studies, the devices ran continuously in the living room and bedroom. What is established is the reduction in concentration, in Shanghai by 57 percent, in Detroit from 15 to 7 micrograms, and a systolic blood pressure lower by about 2 to 4 millimeters of mercury. It is not established that this prevents heart attacks or deaths: the largest study, 540 pregnant women in Ulaanbaatar, lowered particulate matter by 29 percent without changing birth weight. Devices that generate ozone, that is, ionizers, plasma and some UV devices, are excluded, and against carbon dioxide no filter helps, only the window.

Safety

Pure filter devices bring hardly any risks of their own. The exception is devices that generate ozone, that is, ionizers, plasma devices and some UV devices: ozone is a lung irritant, and the US Environmental Protection Agency explicitly advises against them. The second risk is false security, for example a device in the living room while frying takes place in the kitchen without a hood. In the studies, the devices ran continuously in the bedroom and living room, for between 2 days and an entire pregnancy, without reported harm.

Frequently asked questions about air quality and particulate matter

Is particulate matter really the largest risk factor?

In the Global Burden of Disease analysis 2021, with 88 risk factors compared, particulate matter ranks first with 8 percent of all healthy life years lost, ahead of high blood pressure with 7.8 and smoking with 5.7 percent. That is a global figure, and 89 percent of the estimated deaths from outdoor air occur in poorer countries. For Central Europe, the Medicare analysis is more relevant; it finds an association with mortality even at low exposure.

Is there a level of exposure below which it becomes harmless?

Not according to the available data. In the Medicare analysis, the association in the regions below 12 micrograms per cubic meter was not weaker but, at 13.6 percent per 10 micrograms, steeper than in the cohort as a whole. Germany is at about 9 micrograms in the urban background, that is, right in the middle of this range.

What is the largest source of particulate matter in your own home?

The stove. A measurement study from Seattle found peaks of 200 to 1,400 micrograms per cubic meter in the kitchen and living room during standardized searing, which, depending on ventilation, take 1 to 10 hours to subside. Gas stoves additionally emit nitrogen dioxide; candles are the third relevant source.

How large does an air purifier need to be for my room?

The decisive factor is the clean air delivery rate, CADR. The US Environmental Protection Agency calculates around 110 cubic meters per hour for 9 square meters, so a bedroom of 20 square meters needs about 240. Devices from around 150 euros often only manage this on the highest and therefore loudest setting.

Does an air purifier demonstrably lower blood pressure?

In several randomized studies, yes, in the order of 2 to 4 millimeters of mercury systolic. In Detroit it was 3.2 millimeters, a review of 10 studies with 604 participants arrived at 4 millimeters, a second meta-analysis of 14 studies at 2.3 millimeters. The authors of this second paper, however, rate the certainty of the evidence as very low.

Does such a device prevent heart attacks?

No one has shown that. There is no randomized study with hard endpoints such as heart attack, stroke or mortality, and the largest study, with 540 pregnant women in Ulaanbaatar, found no effect on birth weight. What is established is a lower particle concentration and somewhat lower blood pressure over days.
