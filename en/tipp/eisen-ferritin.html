Optimizing iron levels & hemoglobin

Without iron, no oxygen transport capacity.

Without iron, no oxygen transport capacity — that is undisputed. What is disputed is the point at which a value is too low: the commonly used cutoffs differ by a factor of three. Here you can see who set which mark and how to read your ferritin value.

In short

From age 5, the WHO defines iron deficiency as a serum ferritin below 15 µg/l, or below 70 µg/l with infection or inflammation. Physiologically, the body starts to counteract earlier: in young women, iron absorption rose below a ferritin of 51.1 µg/l. Sports medicine uses below 20 µg/l as absolute deficiency. At the upper end, the WHO warns of iron overload above 150 µg/l for women and above 200 µg/l for men. In deficiency without anemia, what is established is mainly an improvement in fatigue, not in performance. And ferritin is an acute-phase protein — without an inflammation marker alongside it, you are not measuring the stores.

Why there are so many cutoffs

For apparently healthy people aged 5 and over, the WHO guideline gives a serum ferritin below 15 µg/l as iron deficiency; with infection or inflammation, the cutoff is raised to below 70 µg/l. At the upper end, the same table gives a risk of iron overload above 150 µg/l for females and above 200 µg/l for males, and above 500 µg/l in people who are not healthy.

Alongside this there is a physiologically derived value. In a pooled analysis of isotope studies in 1,058 healthy women, iron absorption began to rise below a ferritin value of 51.1 µg/l. This value is not set by expert opinion but defined by a measured response of the body. In sports medicine, in turn, a ferritin below 20 µg/l counts as absolute deficiency.

What ferritin does not measure

Ferritin is an acute-phase protein. Inflammation drives hepcidin up and makes the value useless as a storage marker — that is why the WHO raises the deficiency cutoff with inflammation from below 15 to below 70 µg/l. A ferritin of 60 µg/l can then already mean deficiency, while the same value without inflammation is unremarkable.

The second point concerns the popular target ranges: in the literature researched, no ferritin target range was found for people without deficiency. The often-cited desired values of up to 150 for men lie exactly at the WHO warning threshold for women — that is not optimization but the start of the range that is warned about.

How iron is absorbed

Absorption is controlled by the liver hormone hepcidin: it blocks the iron exporter ferroportin and throttles absorption from the gut. When stores fall, hepcidin falls and absorption rises. This leads to the most important point about the intake rhythm: an oral iron dose raises hepcidin for about 24 hours, but not for 48. Daily intake therefore lowers absorption from the following dose.

This shows in the result. In a double-blind study with 150 young women, median serum ferritin at the end was practically identical with the same total dose — 43.8 µg/l versus 44.8 µg/l (p = 0.98) — but the complaints were not: the prevalence ratio for gastrointestinal side effects was 1.56 to the disadvantage of daily intake.

What is well supported

Three findings are firm. First: iron deficiency is common, especially in sport. A meta-analysis of 17,519 athletes found a ferritin below 50 µg/l in about 53.9 % and absolute iron deficiency below 20 µg/l in 23.0 %. Second: in iron deficiency without anemia, iron improves fatigue. A systematic review with 18 studies and 1,170 participants found a standardized mean difference of −0.38 (95 % CI −0.52 to −0.23), plus the expected lab changes (hemoglobin +4.01 g/l). Third: in a double-blind study with 150 women, intake every other day achieved the same ferritin value with the same total dose and fewer gastrointestinal complaints.

What the studies show

The physiological threshold (Galetti et al. 2021)

Pooled analysis of iron isotope studies from 2006 to 2019 in 1,058 healthy women aged 18 to 50 (mean ferritin 33.7 ± 27.1 µg/l). The turning point was at 51.1 µg/l ferritin (95 % CI 49.1–53.1) and 3.09 nmol/l hepcidin: below that, absorption began to rise; above it, it remained stable. That is well above the WHO cutoff.

Fatigue yes, performance no (Houston et al. 2018)

Systematic review with meta-analysis; from 11,580 hits, 18 studies with 1,170 patients were included. Iron administration was associated with less self-reported fatigue (SMD −0.38; 95 % CI −0.52 to −0.23; 4 studies; 714 participants), but not with better objective measures of performance, including maximal oxygen uptake (SMD 0.11; 9 studies; 235 participants).

Every other day instead of daily (von Siebenthal et al. 2023)

Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study with 150 young Swiss women with a serum ferritin of at most 30 µg/l, 75 per group. One group took the dose daily for 90 days and then placebo for 90 days, the other the same daily dose and placebo on alternate days for 180 days. Result: median serum ferritin 43.8 µg/l versus 44.8 µg/l (p = 0.98), prevalence ratio for gastrointestinal side effects 1.56 to the disadvantage of daily intake.

What the measurement does not show

On objective performance, the meta-analyses contradict each other. In non-anemic iron deficiency, the largest review found no effect on VO2max (SMD 0.11), a meta-analysis in endurance athletes with 17 studies a clear one (Hedges' g = 0.610; 95 % CI 0.399–0.821) and a newer one of 13 studies only a trend (SMD 0.49; p = 0.086). What is established is the effect on fatigue and lab values, not on performance.

A ferritin target range for healthy people without deficiency is not defined in any of the papers researched. Vitamin C also helps less than often claimed: in an isotope study in 19 children, fractional absorption rose from 5.65 ± 0.54 % to 7.14 ± 0.90 %, and in 36 women ascorbic acid did not significantly change absorption — body iron stores were what mattered.

How to do it

Measure instead of guessing, specifically serum ferritin together with an inflammation marker. The reference values: below 15 µg/l iron deficiency from age 5, below 70 µg/l with inflammation, below 51.1 µg/l the point from which young women upregulate absorption, below 20 µg/l the sports-medicine definition of absolute deficiency, above 150 µg/l in women and above 200 µg/l in men the overload risk.

If a deficiency is confirmed, the best-supported schedule is intake every other day as a single dose in the morning: in the study with 150 women, the result was identical with the same total dose and tolerability was better. In two randomized trials with a combined 482 pregnant women, black stools occurred in 8 % with iron bisglycinate, 22 % with fumarate and 31 % with sulfate.

Safety

Measure, don’t guess — that applies in both directions. Iron overload is a real diagnosis; the WHO already names above 150 µg/l in women and above 200 µg/l in men as an indication of risk. The most common hereditary form is based on C282Y homozygosity in the HFE gene; in the largest North American cohort, with 306 people followed for a mean of 11.6 years, end-organ damage occurred in 18.3 %, liver disease in 5.8 %. Anyone with this predisposition must not supplement iron and often does not know about it. Without medical evaluation, iron is also not for people with a family history, chronic inflammation or inflammatory bowel disease, and not for children. In men and postmenopausal women with new iron deficiency, the search for the cause comes before replacement. No diagnosis, no treatment recommendation.

Frequently asked questions about Optimizing iron levels & hemoglobin

At what ferritin level do I have iron deficiency?

For people aged 5 and over, the WHO sets the cutoff at below 15 µg/l, or below 70 µg/l with an existing infection or inflammation. Physiologically, however, the body starts to counteract considerably earlier: in young women, iron absorption rose below a ferritin of 51.1 µg/l. Between these two figures lies the range in which it makes sense for your doctor to decide.

Is my ferritin too high if it is above 300?

According to WHO criteria, the risk of iron overload in apparently healthy people already begins above 150 µg/l for women and above 200 µg/l for men, and above 500 µg/l in people who are not healthy. A high value can also indicate inflammation, because ferritin is an acute-phase protein. That is why measuring it together with an inflammation marker makes sense.

Take it daily or every other day?

The best-controlled study on this shows the same result with the same total dose, but fewer side effects. 150 women, double-blind, six months: median ferritin 43.8 versus 44.8 µg/l with p = 0.98, and the prevalence ratio for gastrointestinal complaints was higher by a factor of 1.56 with daily intake. A newer pilot study found advantages for daily intake in some blood count parameters after 14 days, so the question is not entirely settled.

Does iron improve performance?

In iron deficiency without anemia, it is mainly self-reported fatigue that improves, with a standardized mean difference of minus 0.38, but not maximal oxygen uptake. In endurance athletes with iron deficiency, by contrast, one meta-analysis of 17 studies found an effect on VO2max, a newer one of 13 studies only a trend. The effect on fatigue is reliable, the effect on performance is not.

Do I need vitamin C with it?

It helps, but less than often claimed. In an isotope study in 19 children, fractional absorption from a fortified drink rose from 5.65 ± 0.54 % to 7.14 ± 0.90 %. In a study in 36 women, however, ascorbic acid did not significantly change fractional iron absorption; body iron stores were what mattered. The intake schedule is the bigger lever.

How common is iron deficiency in athletes?

Very common. A meta-analysis of 17,519 college athletes from 23 countries and 62 sports found a ferritin below 50 µg/l in 53.9 % and absolute iron deficiency below 20 µg/l in 23.0 %. The authors conclude that routine screening would be justified in this group.
