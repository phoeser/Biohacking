Glossary

The terms behind the fact check

This glossary does not explain active substances – that is what the topic pages are for. Here you will find the terms we use to read studies: what a primary endpoint is, why a marker is not an outcome, and what “for research purposes only” means legally. Each entry gives examples from the fact checks.

Methodology

How studies are built — and how to tell whether a number holds up.

  • BK-ScoreThe BK-Score is the rating Biohacking Kompakt gives every substance and every practice: five axes from 0 to 10 that describe the state of knowledge.
  • Case report and case seriesA case report describes what happened to a single person; a case series summarizes several such cases without a comparison group.
  • Cohort studyA cohort study follows a group of people for years and compares who, with which characteristics or habits, later develops which disease.
  • Confidence intervalThe confidence interval is the range in which the true value of a study result plausibly lies, usually given as a 95 percent interval.
  • Double-blindDouble-blind means that neither the participants nor the investigators know who receives the active ingredient and who receives the placebo.
  • Effect sizeEffect size describes how large a difference is — not just whether it exists.
  • Hazard ratioThe hazard ratio (HR) states how often an event occurs in one group relative to the other, over time.
  • Mendelian randomizationMendelian randomization uses randomly inherited gene variants as a natural experiment to test whether a risk factor really is a cause.
  • Meta-analysisA meta-analysis combines the results of several studies on the same question into one overall result by calculation.
  • NoceboNocebo is the counterpart of placebo: symptoms that arise because people expect side effects — even under a sham treatment.
  • Number needed to treat (NNT)The number needed to treat (NNT) states how many people must receive a treatment for one of them to benefit who would not have done so without it.
  • Observational studyAn observational study does not intervene but watches: it records what people do anyway and compares how they fare later.
  • PlaceboA placebo is a sham treatment without an active ingredient that looks exactly like the real one.
  • Primary endpointThe primary endpoint is the one question that a study registers in advance as its main question.
  • Randomized controlled trial (RCT)In a randomized controlled trial, chance decides who receives the treatment and who goes into the comparison group.
  • Relative and absolute effectThe same study yields two numbers: the relative change sounds big, the absolute one says what actually happened.
  • Statistical significance (p-value)A result is called statistically significant if it would be unlikely under the assumption of “no effect”, usually at a p-value below 0.05.
  • Surrogate markerA surrogate marker is a measured value that stands in for what you are actually interested in — a lab value instead of the course of a disease.
  • Systematic reviewA systematic review searches for all studies on a question according to a plan set in advance, appraises their quality and summarizes them.

Regulation

Approval, law and distribution channels.

  • Compounded medicine (pharmacy preparation)A compounded medicine (in German: Rezepturarzneimittel) is prepared by the pharmacy itself for an individual patient on a doctor’s prescription.
  • Gray marketGray market means: the substance is sold, but neither as a medicinal product nor as a food — usually declared as “for research purposes only”.
  • Health claimA health claim is an advertising statement that a food or nutrient benefits health, such as “contributes to the normal function of the immune system”.
  • § 3a HWG (German Medicinal Products Advertising Act)Section 3a of the German Medicinal Products Advertising Act (HWG) prohibits advertising for medicines that require authorization but are not authorized.
  • Marketing authorization (EMA, FDA, BfArM)A marketing authorization is the official permission to place a medicine on the market for a specific indication.
  • Novel foodIn the EU, a novel food is a food or ingredient that was not consumed to a significant degree before 15 May 1997.
  • Off-label useOff-label means an authorized medicine is used outside its marketing authorization — for a different disease, a different age group or in a different form.
  • Trial phases (phase 1, 2, 3)Before a medicine is authorized, it goes through clinical trials in three successive phases.
  • WADA list (Prohibited List)The WADA list is the Prohibited List of the World Anti-Doping Agency: it sets out which substances and methods are banned in sport.

Pharmacology

How active substances are absorbed, act and are cleared again.

  • Agonist and antagonistAn agonist binds to a receptor and triggers a signal there; an antagonist occupies the receptor and blocks the signal.
  • BioavailabilityBioavailability indicates what proportion of an ingested substance reaches the bloodstream unchanged.
  • Half-lifeThe half-life is the time after which half of a substance in the blood has been broken down or excreted.
  • PeptideA peptide is a short chain of amino acids, the building blocks of proteins — usually between two and around fifty.
  • SARMSARM stands for selective androgen receptor modulator: a substance that binds to the androgen receptor like testosterone but mainly targets muscle and bone.

Biology

Measured values and mechanisms that come up again and again in the fact checks.

  • AutophagyAutophagy is the cell’s recycling programme: it packages damaged or surplus components and breaks them down into their building blocks.
  • BiomarkerA biomarker is a measurable characteristic that reveals something about a condition in the body — a blood value, an imaging finding, a reading from a wearable.
  • Epigenetic clockAn epigenetic clock estimates a biological age from chemical marks on the genome, the methylations.
  • HbA1c (long-term blood sugar)HbA1c indicates what proportion of the red blood pigment is bound to sugar.
  • HormesisHormesis means that a short, moderate stress prompts the body to adapt in ways that make it more resilient, while more of it does harm.
  • LDL and ApoBLDL cholesterol measures how much cholesterol is transported through the blood in LDL particles; ApoB counts the particles themselves.
  • MitochondriaMitochondria are the power plants of the cell: they produce the energy carrier ATP from nutrients and oxygen.
  • Visceral fatVisceral fat lies deep in the abdominal cavity between the organs — unlike subcutaneous fat, which you can pinch.
