Glossary

The terms behind the fact check

This glossary does not explain active substances – that is what the topic pages are for. Here you will find the terms we use to read studies: what a primary endpoint is, why a marker is not an outcome, and what “for research purposes only” means legally. Each entry gives examples from the fact checks.

Methodology

How studies are built — and how to tell whether a number holds up.

  • Primary endpointThe primary endpoint is the one question that a study registers in advance as its main question.
  • Surrogate markerA surrogate marker is a measured value that stands in for what you are actually interested in — a lab value instead of the course of a disease.
  • Relative and absolute effectThe same study yields two numbers: the relative change sounds big, the absolute one says what actually happened.

Regulation

Approval, law and distribution channels.

  • Gray marketGray market means: the substance is sold, but neither as a medicinal product nor as a food — usually declared as “for research purposes only”.

Biology

Measured values and mechanisms that come up again and again in the fact checks.

  • Visceral fatVisceral fat lies deep in the abdominal cavity between the organs — unlike subcutaneous fat, which you can pinch.

Study database

All studies and official sources on which the fact checks are based – searchable and filterable by study type.

  • Study databaseAround 2,500 studies with original title, year, study type and the pages on which we use them.
