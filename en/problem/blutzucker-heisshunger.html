Question & Context

Cravings and blood sugar swings – what helps?

In brief

Cravings two to three hours after a meal really are linked to the course of blood sugar, though only moderately. Fiber-rich meals with enough protein, a short walk after eating and enough sleep smooth the curve and the appetite most reliably. Vinegar and certain food supplements have measurable but small effects. A glucose sensor can satisfy curiosity, but brings no proven benefit to people with a healthy metabolism. With strong thirst, frequent urination or trembling with sweating, blood sugar should be checked by a doctor.

What matters first

Fiber as the foundation

Fiber from whole grains, legumes, vegetables and fruit slows the uptake of sugar into the blood and keeps you full for longer. The large analysis in the Lancet of 185 observational studies and 58 randomized trials found the greatest risk reduction for type 2 diabetes, heart disease and colorectal cancer at 25 to 29 grams of fiber per day. In Germany, average intake is 18 grams for women and 19 grams for men, so clearly below that.

Protein in every meal

Protein is filling and slows the rise in blood sugar when eaten before or with carbohydrate-rich foods. In a study in young women, a high-protein breakfast made them fuller in the following three hours and reduced the desire to eat. Worth knowing: by the end of the day, the women did not eat less overall. Protein is therefore a tool against cravings, not a weight-loss trick.

Walk after eating

A short walk after a meal lets the muscles take up sugar directly from the blood. In 41 people with type 2 diabetes, ten minutes of walking after each main meal lowered the rise in blood sugar more than 30 minutes of walking in one go at any time of day, most clearly after dinner. Several meta-analyses confirm the principle, albeit with low certainty of evidence.

Do not underestimate sleep

Tiredness makes you hungry. In a randomized trial, people with overweight who habitually slept less than 6.5 hours extended their sleep by a good hour per night after counseling. Afterwards, they took in on average 270 kilocalories less per day, without consciously controlling it.

When you should have this checked by a doctor

Type 2 diabetes often develops over years and for a long time causes no symptoms or only unspecific ones. Have your blood sugar measured by a doctor if you are constantly thirsty, have to urinate often, lose weight for no reason, see poorly, wounds heal poorly or you get frequent infections, and likewise if it runs in your family or you are severely overweight. Trembling, sweating, a racing heart and confusion a few hours after eating can point to low blood sugar and should also be checked, all the more so if you take blood-sugar-lowering medication. Cravings with loss of control that weigh heavily on you are a good topic for your family doctor or for nutrition and behavioral counseling.

What lies behind the cravings

A large study with 1,070 participants from the United Kingdom and the USA measured the link more precisely. All of them wore a glucose sensor and ate standardized test meals. What mattered was not the peak directly after eating, but the dip two to three hours later: those who then fell below their baseline were hungrier, ate again sooner and took in more energy over the following 24 hours.

The link was clearly measurable, but not overpowering. Hunger also depends on sleep, stress, habits and what is on the plate. In practice this means: meals that enter the blood slowly make the curve calmer, and with it the afternoon. That is exactly what fiber, protein, healthy fats and movement after eating achieve.

To measure or not to measure?

Glucose sensors worn on the upper arm are now freely available and popular in biohacking. They can help you recognize your own patterns, such as the effect of a late dinner. A meta-analysis of 23 studies with 1,074 people without diabetes, however, found a better average blood sugar only in prediabetes, not in people with a healthy metabolism.

On top of that come two measurement problems. In a laboratory study, sensor readings in healthy people, fasting and after meals, were on average 0.9 millimoles per liter above the reference values. And the same meal often produced very different curves in the same person on two days. If you measure, you should therefore watch trends and not use a single curve to judge a food.

What the database says

Sorted by strength of evidence. The BK-Score rates the state of knowledge, not the substance.

  • Fiber – the best-supported component of food Evidence 9/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedOne of the best-supported dietary measures of all: in large analyses, more fiber goes along with less type 2 diabetes, less heart disease and lower mortality.
  • 10-minute walk after meals Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsShort walks after eating flatten the blood sugar peak, most clearly after dinner. Simple, free and well studied.
  • 30 g of protein at breakfast Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsProtein at breakfast keeps you measurably fuller for longer. That breakfast in particular is decisive, on the other hand, is not proven; evenly distributed meals matter more.
  • Intermittent fasting (16:8) Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsEating within a time window improves weight and insulin sensitivity about as well as an equally large calorie reduction. For some people it is the easier way to get there.
  • Myo-inositol Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsIn women with PCOS, myo-inositol improves fasting blood sugar and insulin in many studies. For people with a healthy metabolism, the data are much thinner.
  • Berberine Evidence 6/10 · Supported, with caveatsLowers blood sugar and blood lipids in several meta-analyses, mostly from small studies from one region. The European food safety authority was unable to derive a safe intake level in its draft opinion; we explicitly make no purchase recommendation here.
  • Erythritol and xylitol Evidence 5/10 · Thin human evidenceSugar alcohols barely raise blood sugar and help to cut down on sweets. For erythritol, however, there is an open debate about effects on blood platelets.
  • Apple cider vinegar before meals Evidence 4/10 · Thin human evidenceLiquid vinegar with a carbohydrate-rich meal lowers the rise in blood sugar in small studies. Vinegar tablets did not work, and tooth enamel suffers.
  • Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) Evidence 3/10 · Long used, barely studiedA glucose sensor shows you live how you respond to food, exercise and sleep. In prediabetes there is a benefit, in people with a healthy metabolism so far there is not, and in them the sensors tend to read too high.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I get cravings after lunch?

Often blood sugar drops below its baseline two to three hours after a quickly digested meal, and that goes along with more hunger. More fiber, enough protein and a short walk after eating make the course calmer. Lack of sleep and stress also increase appetite.

Are cravings for sweets a sign of diabetes?

Not necessarily; cravings alone are very unspecific. Typical signs of diabetes are rather strong thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, vision problems and wounds that heal poorly. Often, however, type 2 diabetes causes no symptoms at all for a long time, which is why a blood sugar test at a check-up is worthwhile.

How long should I walk after eating?

In studies, even ten minutes after each main meal measurably lowered the rise in blood sugar. It helps most after the largest and most carbohydrate-rich meal, which for many people is dinner. Regularity matters more than pace.

Do I need a glucose sensor if I am healthy?

It is not necessary. In people with a healthy metabolism, studies have so far shown no benefit, and in them the sensors tend to read too high. If you are curious, you can observe patterns for two weeks, but should not overrate individual curves.

Does apple cider vinegar help against blood sugar spikes?

In small studies, liquid vinegar with a carbohydrate-rich meal lowered the rise in blood sugar and insulin. With a low-glycemic-index meal there was no effect, and vinegar tablets did not work. Because vinegar is acidic, it attacks tooth enamel in laboratory tests.

Can lack of sleep trigger cravings?

Yes, this is well studied. In a randomized trial, people who extended their sleep by a good hour ate on average 270 kilocalories less per day. Getting enough sleep is therefore one of the simplest means against cravings.
