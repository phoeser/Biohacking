Question & Context

I'm always tired – what helps?

In short

Persistent tiredness almost always has several causes, and the strongest levers are unspectacular: enough and regular sleep, exercise and daylight in the morning. Before you buy supplements, it is worth looking at your blood, because iron deficiency is one of the few causes where a remedy demonstrably reduces exhaustion – even when there is no anemia yet. Vitamin B12 works just as reliably, but only in a true deficiency. Caffeine helps in the short term, rhodiola has small positive studies. If the tiredness lasts for weeks, it should be checked by a doctor.

What matters first

Check your sleep honestly

The American sleep medicine societies recommend that adults regularly sleep 7 or more hours per night. If you get clearly less than that in everyday life, you often already have a large part of the answer. Regularity matters just as much: the same wake-up and bedtimes, even on weekends, stabilize the internal clock.

Have your blood values checked

A blood count with ferritin, plus, depending on the situation, vitamin B12, the thyroid value TSH and blood sugar. Ferritin shows iron stores long before hemoglobin drops. Without a measured value, any intake lacks a basis: iron without a deficiency brings no documented benefit, but it accumulates in the body.

Move, even if it feels wrong

Regular training measurably reduces the feeling of tiredness. A meta-analysis of 81 randomized trials with 7,050 participants found less exhaustion and more energy, typically already with training of moderate intensity. Brisk walking counts.

Light in the morning, caffeine before noon

Daylight shortly after getting up sets the internal clock, one of the best-documented effects in chronobiology. Caffeine works acutely but costs nighttime sleep: in a meta-analysis of 24 studies it shortened total sleep time by 45 minutes on average. The authors advise drinking coffee at least 8.8 hours before bedtime.

When you should have it checked by a doctor

Have tiredness checked by a doctor if it lasts for weeks without a clear reason you know of, or if warning signs come on top: unintended weight loss, fever or night sweats, shortness of breath or heart palpitations with light exertion, striking paleness, unusually heavy periods or blood in the stool, strong thirst with frequent urination, persistent low mood or loss of joy. Loud snoring with observed pauses in breathing and nodding off during the day, for example at the wheel, points to sleep apnea, which is very treatable. Medications can also make you tired. Your primary care practice is the right first place to go, and most causes can be narrowed down there with a conversation, an examination and a few blood values.

Why tiredness rarely has just one cause

Tiredness is not a finding but a signal. Too little or restless sleep, lack of exercise, chronic stress, infections, nutrient gaps and metabolic issues such as the thyroid or blood sugar produce the same feeling, often several of them at once. That is why a single supplement rarely brings the solution as long as the basics are not right.

An honest log over a few weeks helps: when do you go to bed, when do you get up, how much coffee and alcohol, how much exercise, when is the low point strongest? Often a pattern shows up that you can change. For your primary care practice, such a log is also more valuable than any description from memory.

Iron: the case where a remedy really works

Iron deficiency is common in menstruating women and endurance athletes and is easily overlooked, because hemoglobin stays normal for a long time. A systematic review of 18 randomized trials with 1,170 patients found a noticeable decrease in self-reported exhaustion in iron deficiency without anemia, with an effect size of −0.38. The picture is not entirely uniform: a study of 154 female blood donors saw no clinical effect after 4 weeks, even though iron levels rose.

Absorption can be influenced. In an isotope study of 34 iron-deficient women, coffee taken with the dose reduced the amount absorbed by 54 percent, while vitamin C increased it by 30 percent. Intake itself should be tied to a measured value, because the body barely excretes excess iron, and in hereditary iron overload disease it can damage organs.

Adaptogens and energy supplements: where the data stop

Rhodiola is the adaptogen with the most studies on exhaustion. In a placebo-controlled study of 161 cadets, a single dose clearly improved a fatigue index, and a study of 60 people with fatigue syndrome narrowly met its primary endpoint. The European Medicines Agency lists roseroot as a traditional herbal medicinal product for relief of stress symptoms such as fatigue – it is not considered proven effective, because the studies are small and have rarely been independently replicated.

Vitamin B12, coenzyme Q10 and B-complex products are often sold as energy boosters. For B12 the situation is clearly split in two: in a deficiency it works reliably; with good status, a meta-analysis of 16 randomized trials with 6,276 participants found no effect on cognition or depressive symptoms. If you are vegan or take metformin or stomach acid blockers, you should know your level, because deficiency is more common here.

What the database says

Sorted by strength of data. The BK-Score rates the state of knowledge, not the substance.

  • Iron Evidence 9/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedThe best-documented approach against exhaustion when stores are empty: in a randomized trial of 198 women with low ferritin and normal hemoglobin, the fatigue score fell by 47.7 percent on iron and by 28.8 percent on placebo. Without a deficiency, there is no evidence for it.
  • Caffeine Evidence 9/10 · Well supportedWorks reliably against acute tiredness; the blockade of adenosine receptors is well measured in humans. The price is nighttime sleep if the last cup comes too late.
  • CPAP for sleep apnea Evidence 9/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedWhen nighttime pauses in breathing are behind daytime tiredness, the breathing mask clearly improves daytime sleepiness, quality of life and mood. In the largest trial, however, it did not prevent heart attacks and strokes.
  • Vitamin B12 Evidence 8/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedIn a documented deficiency, one of the best-founded substances of all, as a tablet just as much as an injection. With good status, there is no effect on energy, mood or cognition.
  • 10 min of sunlight in the morning Evidence 8/10 · Well supportedBright light shortly after getting up shifts the internal clock earlier and stabilizes the day-night rhythm. Free and well documented.
  • 6,000 to 10,000 steps a day Evidence 8/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedEveryday movement is the foundation on which training and energy build, and in large cohorts it is clearly linked to better health.
  • Sleep & sleep hygiene Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsThe overview of sleep duration, regularity and sleep hygiene. For a true sleep disorder, cognitive behavioral therapy is the best-documented approach.
  • Rhodiola rosea Evidence 6/10 · Supported, with caveatsThe best-known adaptogen against exhaustion, registered in Europe as a traditional herbal medicinal product. Several small studies show positive effects on mental fatigue; independent replications are still missing.

Frequently asked questions

Which deficiency most often causes tiredness?

The usual candidates are iron, vitamin B12 and an underactive thyroid. The link is best documented for iron, where even empty stores without anemia can worsen exhaustion. Only a blood test shows which value is abnormal in your case.

At what ferritin level does iron help against tiredness?

The cutoff is set by your doctor, partly because ferritin can look normal during inflammation despite empty stores. In the best-known study on tiredness, the women had a ferritin below 50 micrograms per liter with normal hemoglobin. Taking iron on a hunch is not a good idea.

How long does it take for iron to work against tiredness?

In this study, measurements were taken after 12 weeks, and by then the difference from placebo was clear. How quickly stores fill up depends on the starting level and on absorption. That is why progress is monitored through blood values.

Does coffee help with constant tiredness?

Coffee makes you more alert and more capable in the short term, which is well documented. Against constant tiredness it helps only to a limited extent, because late cups shorten nighttime sleep and can thus increase tiredness the next day. If you regularly drink a lot, you also partly get used to the performance-enhancing effect.

Are adaptogens like rhodiola safe?

In the studies, rhodiola was well tolerated, and adverse effects were rarely reported. One problem is the quality of commercial products, as several analyses found products without the declared marker compounds. If you take medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, discuss taking it beforehand.

Can tiredness also come from the mind?

Yes, persistent exhaustion is a typical companion of depression and chronic stress. If loss of joy, rumination or sleep problems come on top, talking to your primary care practice or a psychotherapist is the right step. Both are very treatable.
