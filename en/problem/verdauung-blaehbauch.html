Question & Context

Bloating and digestive problems – what helps?

In brief

A bloated belly is usually harmless, but can be very annoying. If it keeps coming back, irritable bowel syndrome or an intolerance is often behind it, and there are well-studied aids for that. Psyllium husks, a time-limited low-FODMAP diet, more exercise and selected probiotics have helped in studies, as have peppermint oil and gut-directed hypnosis or behavioral therapy. Commercial microbiome tests, on the other hand, do not provide a reliable basis for diet plans. Weight loss, blood in the stool, fever or pallor should always be checked by a doctor.

What matters first

Keep a symptom diary

For two weeks, note what you eat, how you sleep, how stressed you are and when your belly acts up. Patterns often emerge, for example after dairy products, onions, legumes, sugar alcohols in sugar-free products or large, late meals. The diary also helps at the doctor’s appointment.

Choose fiber wisely

Not all fiber is the same. In a new meta-analysis of 30 studies with 1,904 people with irritable bowel syndrome, 52 percent responded to fiber, compared with 44 percent in the control groups. The benefit was clearest for psyllium husks, that is, soluble fiber. Wheat bran, on the other hand, showed no benefit in an earlier meta-analysis. If you eat more fiber, it is best to increase it slowly.

Move more

Exercise gets the gut going. In a Swedish study with 102 people with irritable bowel syndrome, symptoms improved markedly when the participants became more physically active under the guidance of a physiotherapist. In the control group, symptoms worsened more often. Even a walk after eating is a good start.

Treat the mind as well

The gut is closely connected to the brain, and acute as well as chronic psychological stress can be involved in the development of irritable bowel syndrome. The German guideline therefore recommends measures for coping with stress and, if needed, psychotherapeutic methods as a complement. That is not an excuse but one of the best-supported aids.

When you should have this checked by a doctor

See a doctor if the abdominal complaints are accompanied by marked weight loss, blood in the stool, fever or pallor, because this can indicate another bowel disease. Before irritable bowel syndrome is established, colorectal cancer, chronic inflammatory bowel diseases and intolerances should be ruled out, especially if your digestion changes anew for no apparent reason or such diseases run in your family. The assessment often includes a blood test for celiac disease, and this should be done while you are still eating gluten normally, because a gluten-free diet can distort the result. You should attend colorectal cancer screening regardless of symptoms.

When your belly keeps rebelling

Irritable bowel syndrome affects an estimated 10 to 20 in 100 people, women about twice as often as men. Typical are abdominal pain, bloating and changed bowel movements, without any other cause being found in the examinations. That does not mean the complaints are imaginary: among the factors discussed are an impaired intestinal barrier, an altered microbiome, an overactive enteric nervous system and disturbed communication between gut and brain.

The best-studied approach is the low-FODMAP diet. Poorly absorbed, readily fermentable sugars such as fructans from wheat and onions, lactose or sugar alcohols are reduced for a few weeks and then reintroduced step by step. In a network meta-analysis of 13 studies with 944 people, it ranked first for all symptoms studied, including bloating. The German guideline describes three phases: elimination, testing tolerance and a diet that is as broad as possible over the long term. It explicitly does not recommend far-reaching restrictions without proof of efficacy; a dietitian is a great help here.

What else works

In a meta-analysis of ten studies with 1,030 people, peppermint oil was more effective than placebo, both for overall symptoms and for abdominal pain. Side effects occurred more often, and the quality of the studies was very low. The German guideline recommends considering it for pain and bloating, at least for a short time. Anti-foaming agents such as simethicone received no recommendation there for lack of convincing studies.

The data for methods that work through the mind are surprisingly strong. A network meta-analysis of 41 studies with 4,072 participants found that cognitive behavioral therapy, also in self-help form, and gut-directed hypnosis improved symptoms markedly more often than the control conditions. Because many studies had weaknesses, the effects are probably somewhat overestimated, but the direction is clear.

With probiotics, the strain matters. In a recent analysis, some single strains worked on the core symptoms, others showed no effect. If you try it, it is best to choose a product with a strain that has been tested in irritable bowel syndrome.

Frequently asked questions

What helps quickly against a bloated belly?

In irritable bowel syndrome, peppermint oil has improved pain and symptoms in studies, and many people find exercise such as a walk relieving. Anti-foaming agents, on the other hand, are poorly supported. If the bloating keeps coming back, it is worth looking at your diet and having it checked by a doctor.

What is the low-FODMAP diet?

Hard-to-digest, readily fermentable types of sugar from foods such as wheat, onions, garlic, legumes, some fruits and dairy products are reduced for a few weeks. They are then reintroduced step by step to find your personal triggers. The method is well studied in irritable bowel syndrome and works best with dietary advice.

Should I cut out fiber because of bloating?

No, but the type is crucial. Soluble fiber such as psyllium husks has helped in irritable bowel syndrome, but wheat bran has not. Increasing slowly makes the change easier.

Is a microbiome test worth it?

For treating digestive complaints, rather not. The results are hardly comparable between different laboratories, and the German irritable bowel syndrome guideline rates commercial stool tests negatively. A symptom diary and a medical check-up usually bring more.

Which probiotics help with irritable bowel syndrome?

Only certain strains that have been tested in studies are effective, not probiotics in general. The German guideline recommends using selected strains and bases the choice on the main symptoms. A trial over a few weeks shows whether it helps you.

Can stress trigger digestive problems?

Yes, gut and brain are closely connected, and stress can increase the sensitivity of the gut. That is why behavioral therapy and gut-directed hypnosis, which were effective in many studies, also help in irritable bowel syndrome. Relaxation exercises and enough sleep are good complements.
