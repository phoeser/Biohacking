Question & Context

Constant stress and inner restlessness – what helps?

In brief

Against constant stress, nothing is as broadly supported as regular exercise; it noticeably reduces anxiety and stress symptoms in a great many studies. Close behind come techniques you can use right away: slow breathing with an emphasized exhale and mindfulness meditation. Sleep is both cause and consequence here. Herbal remedies such as ashwagandha show less perceived stress in small studies, but bring their own risks; L-theanine and rhodiola have weaker data. If restlessness, anxiety or exhaustion dominate your everyday life for weeks, professional help is the right way.

What matters first

Exercise as a stress buffer

A review of 97 meta-analyses with 1,039 studies and 128,119 participants found moderate effects of physical activity on anxiety and psychological distress, in healthy adults just as in people with illnesses. More intensive activity tended to bring more. It does not have to be competitive sport, but it should be regular.

Breathing with a long exhale

In a randomized trial, 5 minutes of structured breathing exercises a day over one month improved mood more than the same time spent on mindfulness meditation. The effect was clearest with cyclic sighing: inhale twice, then exhale long. The exercise is free and immediately available in acute moments.

Protect your sleep

Stress robs you of sleep, and lack of sleep makes you more susceptible to stress. Fixed wake-up times, caffeine only early in the day and an evening without work emails break this cycle more easily than any supplement.

Name the sources of stress

Not every burden can be trained away. Writing down exactly what is putting you under pressure, and checking what can be changed, delegated or ended, is unspectacular, but often the biggest lever.

When you should have this checked by a doctor

Get medical or psychotherapeutic advice if inner restlessness, anxiety, brooding or exhaustion persist for weeks and limit you in everyday life, at work or in relationships, if panic attacks occur, or if you increasingly need alcohol, sedatives or sleeping pills to wind down. A racing heart, trembling, sweating and weight loss can also have physical causes such as an overactive thyroid. Chest pain, shortness of breath or heart palpitations should always be checked by a doctor. If you have thoughts of not wanting to live any more, contact the emergency number, an emergency department or the Telefonseelsorge telephone helpline immediately.

What constant stress does in the body

Acute stress is useful: pulse, blood pressure and attention rise, and the body provides energy. It becomes a problem when the recovery phases are missing and the system remains on alert permanently. Then restlessness, irritability, muscle tension, digestive complaints and above all poor sleep appear.

That is why the supported strategies work at both ends: they lower the baseline load, for example through exercise and sleep, and they give you tools for the acute moment, such as breathing exercises. Both complement each other, and both cost nothing.

Meditation and breathing exercises: what the studies show

A meta-analysis of 47 randomized trials with 3,515 participants found moderate evidence for mindfulness programs reducing anxiety, with an effect size of 0.38 after 8 weeks, and reducing depressive symptoms. Meditation was not better than other active treatments such as exercise or medication. It is therefore a good option among several, not a special path.

Breathing exercises are the fastest variant. Slow breathing with a prolonged exhale lowers the breathing rate and has a noticeably calming effect. In the study mentioned, the resting breathing rate fell more with the breathing exercises, especially with cyclic sighing, than with meditation.

Adaptogens: what holds up and where the data stop

Ashwagandha is the best-studied adaptogen against stress. A meta-analysis of 15 randomized trials with 873 adults found lower scores on the perceived stress scale and lower cortisol levels after 8 weeks; quality of life did not change. The extracts are inconsistent, and data beyond 8 weeks are lacking. The German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment points to cases of liver damage and advises against it for pregnant and breastfeeding women, children and people with liver disease.

L-theanine has a rather gentle effect: in the largest meta-analysis, the stress effect was small and relied mainly on studies with a high risk of bias. Rhodiola is registered in Europe as a traditional herbal medicinal product for stress symptoms, but its efficacy is not considered proven. Magnesium is often advertised against stress: a systematic review of 18 studies found indications of less anxiety in particularly susceptible groups, but with weak study quality, and no study had measured stress with a validated questionnaire. No adaptogen replaces sleep, exercise and breaks.

What the database says

Sorted by strength of evidence. The BK-Score rates the state of knowledge, not the substance.

  • 6,000 to 10,000 steps a day Evidence 8/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedRegular exercise is the most broadly supported stress buffer; even everyday movement counts. In large cohorts, it is clearly linked to better health.
  • L-theanine Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsThe amino acid from tea calms without making you tired. The stress effect is small and comes mainly from studies with a high risk of bias; the effects on attention are better supported.
  • Sleep & sleep hygiene Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsThe overview of sleep duration, regularity and sleep hygiene. If you sleep badly under stress, you will find the supported levers here.
  • Meditation 10 min daily Evidence 6/10 · Supported, with caveatsMeta-analyses show small to moderate effects on anxiety, depressive symptoms and pain. In the studies, meditation was not superior to other active treatments such as exercise.
  • Rhodiola rosea Evidence 6/10 · Supported, with caveatsRegistered in Europe as a traditional herbal medicinal product for the relief of stress symptoms such as tiredness and exhaustion. The studies are small and come mainly from a few groups.
  • Ashwagandha Evidence 5/10 · Thin human evidenceThe best-known adaptogen: small studies show less perceived stress and lower cortisol levels, mostly over eight weeks. The German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment warns of rare liver damage.
  • Box Breathing (4-4-4-4) Evidence 5/10 · Thin human evidenceEqually long phases for inhaling, holding, exhaling and holding acutely dampen heart rate and state anxiety. In a comparative study of several breathing techniques, the benefit was greatest with the variant with an emphasized exhale.
  • Measuring HRV daily Evidence 5/10 · Thin human evidenceHeart rate variability reflects autonomic balance and can help make load and recovery visible. Individual daily values fluctuate strongly.
  • Forest bathing (Shinrin-Yoku) Evidence 4/10 · Thin human evidenceTime in green spaces is associated with better well-being in large cohorts. The often-cited immune findings come from small studies by a single research group.

Frequently asked questions

What helps immediately against inner restlessness?

Slow breathing with a prolonged exhale, for example inhaling twice and then exhaling long. In one study, 5 minutes a day were enough to measurably improve mood and breathing rate over one month. A brisk walk also helps, and both can be used anywhere.

How quickly does ashwagandha work?

Most studies ran for eight weeks and measured the effects at the end. There are hardly any data for longer periods. Because of rare liver damage, you should discuss taking it with your doctor, especially if you take medication.

Is meditation better than exercise against stress?

In the studies, meditation was not better than other active treatments such as exercise. Both reduce stress and anxiety symptoms; exercise has the broader evidence base. What works best is what you can keep up over the long term.

Does magnesium help against stress?

Magnesium is often advertised as an anti-stress mineral. A systematic review found indications of less anxiety in susceptible groups, but the studies were of weak quality. As a well-tolerated thing to try, it is reasonable; as a replacement for the supported strategies, it is not suitable.

How do I know if stress is making me ill?

Warning signs are persistent sleep problems, exhaustion despite rest, irritability, withdrawal, concentration problems and frequent physical complaints without a clear cause. If these signs persist for weeks, a conversation at your family doctor’s practice makes sense. Early help shortens the way back.

Can I measure my stress levels?

Heart rate variability gives indications of load and recovery, but individual daily values fluctuate strongly. The trend over weeks is what is useful. Your own well-being remains the most important measurement.
