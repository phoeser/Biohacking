Question & Context

Enlarged prostate and night-time urination – what helps?

In brief

For symptoms caused by a benign enlarged prostate, structured self-management is the best-supported place to start: in a randomised trial, treatment failed after three months in 10 instead of 42 percent of men on a self-management programme. It includes drinking less in the evening, limiting coffee and alcohol, training the bladder and staying active. According to Cochrane, saw palmetto makes little difference compared with placebo, while pumpkin seed showed a benefit in one large trial that still needs confirmation. If that is not enough, well-studied medicines are available. Blood in the urine, fever, pain or a bladder you cannot empty need medical attention right away.

What matters first

Start with a self-management programme

In a randomised trial of 140 men referred to urology with uncomplicated urinary symptoms, 73 also received a short self-management programme of education, lifestyle advice and goal setting. After three months, treatment had failed in 10 percent of them versus 42 percent in the comparison group. The IPSS symptom score was 10.7 versus 16.4 points. In the larger TRIUMPH trial of 1,077 men in general practice, the benefit at 12 months was smaller at 1.81 points, but it lasted.

Change how you drink in the evening

The German Society of Urology guideline recommends spreading fluid intake evenly over the day, not drinking excessively in the evening and avoiding excessive coffee and alcohol, because both act as diuretics and irritate the bladder. Drinking too little, however, can make irritative symptoms worse. In a small uncontrolled study of 56 patients, fluid rules, less time in bed, daily exercise and keeping warm in bed reduced night-time trips to the toilet from 3.6 to 2.7 on average after four weeks.

Train your bladder

In bladder training you consciously hold on for a while when the urge is mild to moderate, gradually stretching the intervals between toilet visits. The German guideline lists this among the recommended behavioural measures, along with milking the urethra to prevent after-dribble and reviewing diuretic medicines taken in the evening. A Cochrane review of 6 trials with 652 men found signs of fewer symptoms with pelvic floor exercise and tai chi, but the certainty of the evidence was very low.

Stay active and watch your waistline

In a meta-analysis of observational studies with 43,083 men, those with moderate or vigorous physical activity were less likely to have prostate enlargement or urinary symptoms than sedentary men (odds ratio 0.74 for each). The guideline names the metabolic syndrome as the best-studied modifiable risk factor and advises weight loss, diet and exercise. Whether changing lifestyle directly improves the symptoms has not yet been clearly shown.

When you should have this checked by a doctor

Have new or bothersome urinary symptoms checked by a doctor, not least to rule out other causes. Seek help immediately, the same day, if you cannot pass urine despite a full bladder, if fever, chills or pain in the pelvis, when urinating or in the flanks develop, or if you see blood in your urine, even once and without pain. Also get prompt advice for frequent urinary tract infections, unintended leakage, a feeling that your bladder never empties completely, strong thirst with large amounts of urine, swollen legs, loud snoring with pauses in breathing, bone pain or unintended weight loss. These can point to urinary retention, prostate inflammation, bladder stones, a tumour of the bladder or prostate, diabetes, heart failure or sleep apnoea.

Why the prostate disturbs your nights – and why it is not always the prostate

The prostate surrounds the urethra just below the bladder and grows in many men with age, driven by the hormone dihydrotestosterone. When it presses on the urethra, the stream weakens, the bladder has to work against resistance, becomes more sensitive and signals more often. Doctors record the symptoms with the IPSS questionnaire on a scale from 0 to 35 points. The course cannot be predicted reliably for an individual, but on average symptoms tend to increase over the years.

Night-time urination often has several causes. Many men simply produce too much urine at night, for example from drinking late, alcohol, diuretic medicines, diabetes or heart failure. Sleep apnoea can also be behind it: in five before-and-after studies with 307 patients, the number of night-time toilet trips fell markedly with a CPAP mask. A bladder diary kept for a few days, noting fluid intake, times and urine volumes, helps narrow down the cause.

Saw palmetto, pumpkin seed and more: what the studies show

Saw palmetto is the best-known herbal remedy for the prostate and is sold as a herbal medicinal product. The 2023 Cochrane review of 27 trials with 4,656 participants reaches a clear conclusion, however: saw palmetto alone lowered the symptom score by only 0.90 points on the 35-point scale after three to six months and not at all after 12 to 17 months, both with high certainty. It is well tolerated, and side effects were probably no more frequent than with placebo. Combinations with other plant extracts showed 2.41 points, with low certainty.

For pumpkin seed there is one large German trial: in GRANU, with 1,431 men aged 50 to 80, 58.5 percent responded to pumpkin seed after 12 months versus 47.3 percent on placebo. The authors rate the difference as only descriptively significant and call for a confirmatory trial, and a pumpkin seed extract in capsules was no better than placebo. For nettle root the results are contradictory according to the guideline, and there are only few data on pollen extracts and African plum tree.

Medicines and the PSA test: what your doctor can offer

If self-management is not enough, several well-studied drug classes may be considered after a medical examination. Alpha blockers relax the muscle of the prostate and bladder neck, while 5-alpha reductase inhibitors such as finasteride and dutasteride slowly shrink an enlarged prostate. In the MTOPS trial of 3,047 men followed for 4.5 years on average, the alpha blocker doxazosin reduced the risk of worsening by 39 percent, finasteride by 34 percent and the combination by 66 percent. Urinary retention and surgery became less frequent only with finasteride and the combination.

Tadalafil, known for erectile dysfunction, is also approved for the symptoms of benign prostatic enlargement. According to a Cochrane review of 16 trials, PDE5 inhibitors lowered the symptom score by 1.89 points compared with placebo, similar to alpha blockers, with somewhat more side effects and study durations of no more than 12 weeks. 5-alpha reductase inhibitors can cause sexual side effects and lower the PSA value, which has to be taken into account in later measurements.

Screening for prostate cancer is a separate question, because benign enlargement is not considered a precursor of cancer. In the European ERSPC trial of 162,236 men aged 55 to 69, prostate cancer mortality after 23 years was 13 percent lower in the PSA testing group, and one death was prevented for every 456 men invited to testing. At the same time, 30 percent more cancers were diagnosed, including some that would never have caused symptoms. Whether the test is right for you is a decision to weigh up with your doctor.

Frequently asked questions

What can I do in the evening to get up less at night?

Spread your drinking over the day and drink less in the hours before bed, without drinking too little overall. Coffee, tea and alcohol in the evening increase the urge in many people. Go to the toilet deliberately before bed. A bladder diary shows whether you simply produce too much urine at night. If you still regularly get up several times, have the cause checked by a doctor.

Does saw palmetto help an enlarged prostate?

According to the 2023 Cochrane review, hardly: saw palmetto alone improved the symptom score by 0.90 out of 35 points in the methodologically sound trials and not at all after more than a year. One specific hexane extract performs better in a manufacturer-linked meta-analysis. Saw palmetto is well tolerated but does not replace a medical check-up.

Do pumpkin seeds help with prostate symptoms?

In the GRANU trial of 1,431 men, 58.5 percent responded to pumpkin seed after one year, compared with 47.3 percent on placebo. The difference counts as only descriptively significant, and an extract in capsules did not help. Pumpkin seeds are a food and well tolerated, but a confirmatory trial is still missing.

Does tadalafil also help with prostate symptoms?

Yes, since 2012 tadalafil has also been approved for the symptoms of benign prostatic enlargement. In a Cochrane review, PDE5 inhibitors lowered the symptom score by 1.89 points compared with placebo, similar to alpha blockers. Because it also works for erectile dysfunction, it addresses both complaints. It is prescription-only and must not be combined with nitrates.

Does an enlarged prostate turn into cancer?

According to current knowledge, no: benign enlargement is not a precursor of prostate cancer. Both can occur at the same time, however, and early prostate cancer usually causes no symptoms. Urinary symptoms therefore say little about whether cancer is present. Screening is a separate topic you can discuss with your doctor.

Should I have a PSA test from age 50?

That is a personal trade-off. In the European ERSPC trial, regular PSA screening reduced prostate cancer mortality by 13 percent after 23 years, but also led to more diagnoses, including tumours that would never have caused problems, with possible consequences of biopsies and treatment. Ask your doctor to explain the benefits and risks and then decide together.
