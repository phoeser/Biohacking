Question & Context

Lowering silent inflammation – what is proven?

In brief

Silent inflammation is not a diagnosis, but a permanently slightly raised level of inflammatory activity that can be measured in the blood, for example via high-sensitivity CRP. That it matters for the heart and blood vessels was shown by a large drug trial. Exercise and weight loss lower inflammation markers most reliably, along with healthy gums, not smoking and good sleep. Omega-3 and turmeric shift individual markers, but hard health benefits for broad use are not proven. A single raised value is often just an infection and no cause for concern.

What matters first

Move, in any form

A meta-analysis of 83 controlled trials with 3,769 participants shows that training lowers the CRP value, regardless of age and sex. It fell most when body fat was lost at the same time, but the effect was measurable even without weight loss. Exercise is therefore the anti-inflammatory with the broadest data base.

Reduce belly fat

As body fat goes down, so does inflammatory activity. In an analysis of 33 weight-loss studies, the CRP value fell on average by 0.13 milligrams per liter per kilogram of body weight lost. The relationship was approximately linear: every kilo counts.

Look after your gums

Chronic periodontitis is a permanent inflammation that often goes unnoticed for a long time. In Germany, according to the Sixth German Oral Health Study, around 14 million people have a severe form that needs treatment. In a randomized trial with 120 people, intensive periodontitis treatment improved the function of the blood vessels after two and six months. One day after treatment, inflammation markers were initially even raised; only afterwards did the picture reverse.

Stopping smoking and good sleep

Those who stop smoking lower their inflammatory messengers: in a small study with 30 people, the messenger TNF-alpha was still lower than before even one year after a smoking cessation program. With sleep, the picture is more precise than is often claimed: in a meta-analysis of 72 studies, disturbed sleep and very long sleep went along with higher inflammation markers, whereas short-term sleep deprivation in the lab did not. So it is more worthwhile to work on sleep quality than to stay in bed as long as possible.

When you should have this checked by a doctor

The CRP value rises sharply with any infection, any injury and after intensive training; a single raised value should therefore be rechecked during a healthy phase. You should have the following checked by a doctor: persistently raised inflammation markers with no recognizable cause, fever or night sweats lasting weeks, unintended weight loss, swollen and stiff joints in the morning, persistent diarrhea with blood or mucus, bleeding gums, and skin rashes that do not heal. These can be caused by treatable conditions such as autoimmune diseases, chronic inflammatory bowel diseases or hidden infections. Do not take anti-inflammatory drugs long term on your own initiative.

Why inflammation matters for the heart

For a long time it was only suspected that low-grade inflammation drives vascular disease. The CANTOS trial tested this directly for the first time in 2017: 10,061 people after a heart attack with a high-sensitivity CRP of at least 2 milligrams per liter received an antibody against the inflammatory messenger interleukin-1 beta or placebo. At the medium dose, repeat cardiovascular events occurred 15 percent less often, even though blood lipids did not change. However, the people studied already had heart disease; they were not healthy people.

But the trial also shows the downside: fatal infections were more common on the drug, and overall mortality did not differ. Inflammation is part of the body's defense; suppressing it completely is not a goal. For everyday life this means: reduce the causes of permanent inflammation instead of dampening the response with remedies.

What the CRP value can and cannot do

In an analysis of 54 long-term studies with 160,309 people, a higher CRP went along with more heart disease, strokes and deaths. Part of this link disappeared, however, once blood pressure, blood lipids, smoking and diabetes were taken into account. A follow-up analysis estimated that an additional CRP measurement in people at intermediate risk could prevent about one event per 400 to 500 people examined.

The value is therefore a useful piece of the puzzle, but not an oracle. If you have it measured, you should do so during a healthy phase, look at it together with blood pressure, blood lipids and blood sugar, and tackle the big levers first. Many of the measures that lower CRP improve all these other values at the same time.

What the database says

Sorted by strength of evidence. The BK-Score rates the state of knowledge, not the substance.

  • 6,000 to 10,000 steps a day Evidence 8/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedEveryday movement and training measurably lower the CRP value and, in large cohorts, are clearly linked to lower mortality.
  • Fiber – the best-supported component of food Evidence 9/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedIn large analyses, a high-fiber diet goes along with less heart disease, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer, precisely the diseases in which silent inflammation plays a role.
  • Reducing alcohol Evidence 9/10 · Well supportedDrinking less alcohol is among the best-supported health levers of all, with benefits for sleep, the liver and cancer risk.
  • Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) Evidence 8/10 · Well supportedOmega-3 fatty acids are considered anti-inflammatory. However, the large trials on cardiovascular events partly contradict each other, and in healthy people without preselection the VITAL trial missed its primary endpoint.
  • Extended biomarker panel Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsHigh-sensitivity CRP is part of the extended blood panel. The page explains what the value reveals about cardiovascular risk and how little it adds to the classic risk factors.
  • Sleep & sleep hygiene Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsRegular, restful sleep is the foundation. In studies, disturbed sleep is linked to higher inflammation markers.
  • Turmeric (curcumin) Evidence 6/10 · Supported, with caveatsIn knee osteoarthritis, curcumin lowered CRP in a meta-analysis and relieves pain similarly to ibuprofen. However, the formulations needed for absorption stand out in case series of liver damage.
  • Oral microbiome & periodontitis Evidence 4/10 · Thin human evidencePeriodontitis germs are suspected of putting a strain on the heart and blood vessels. A link is proven; that treatment prevents heart attacks, on the other hand, is not yet.

Frequently asked questions

What is silent inflammation?

It means a permanently slightly raised inflammatory activity in the body that causes no acute symptoms. It is associated with overweight, lack of exercise, smoking, periodontitis and cardiovascular disease. It is not a diagnosis in its own right; it can best be measured via high-sensitivity CRP.

What CRP value is normal?

The laboratory limits depend on the measurement method and are stated on your report. In the CANTOS trial, a high-sensitivity CRP of 2 milligrams per liter or more was considered raised. With infections, the value often rises far above that, which is why a recheck in healthy times makes sense.

Which diet lowers inflammation?

The best-studied effect is via body weight: for every kilogram lost, CRP falls measurably on average. A high-fiber diet with plenty of vegetables, legumes and whole grains is associated with less heart and metabolic disease. Stopping smoking and drinking less alcohol add to this.

Do omega-3 or turmeric help against inflammation?

Both shift individual inflammation markers in studies, turmeric for example in knee osteoarthritis. The data are not sufficient for protection against heart attack or other hard events in healthy people. For turmeric products with enhanced absorption, there are also reports of liver damage.

Can exercise also increase inflammation?

Directly after a hard session, inflammation markers rise briefly; this is a normal part of adaptation. Regular training, by contrast, measurably lowers CRP in the long run. Adequate breaks between hard sessions are important.

Should I have my CRP value measured regularly?

For healthy people, the added benefit of a routine measurement is small. It can make sense at intermediate cardiovascular risk, to refine the assessment. It is more important to know your blood pressure, blood lipids and blood sugar and to tackle the big lifestyle levers.
