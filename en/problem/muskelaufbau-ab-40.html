Question & Context

Building and keeping muscle after 40 – what matters?

In brief

Muscle can be built at any age, even beyond 60. By far the most important lever is regular strength training with increasing load; the WHO recommends it on 2 or more days per week. Add to that enough protein spread over the day, rather a little more as you get older. Creatine is the best-supported supplement and measurably boosts strength gains in older people. Protein powder helps mainly when food provides too little, and only together with training. Hormones belong in medical hands and only with a proven deficiency.

What matters first

Strength training with a plan

The WHO recommends that adults do muscle-strengthening exercises for all major muscle groups on 2 or more days per week, at at least moderate intensity. For older people, it advises varied training on 3 or more days with a focus on strength and balance to prevent falls. What matters is that the load increases over the weeks.

Enough protein, spread over the day

An expert group of geriatric societies recommends that healthy people over 65 get at least 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day, and active people at least 1.2 grams. In a large meta-analysis, protein beyond 1.62 grams per kilogram brought no further gain in fat-free mass.

Measure strength, not just mass

Strength says more about health than muscle size. In the international PURE cohort with 139,691 people from 17 countries, every 5 kilograms lower grip strength was associated with a 16 percent higher risk of death. A hand dynamometer or a log of your training weights shows the trend.

Recovery and sleep

Muscles grow during recovery. Enough sleep, rest days and a calorie intake that supports the training are the prerequisites. If you want to lose weight at the same time, you need strength training and protein all the more.

When you should have this checked by a doctor

Get medical advice before you start if you have cardiovascular disease, high blood pressure, diabetes or joint problems, or if you have not done any sport for a long time. Chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness or heart palpitations during exertion are a reason to stop training immediately and have it checked. Unintended muscle loss, increasing weakness, frequent falls, muscle pain without training or a marked drop in performance despite training also belong at your family doctor’s practice. In men with loss of libido, lack of drive and muscle loss, a testosterone deficiency may be behind it, which can only be established by repeated blood tests. If you have impaired kidney function, discuss the amount of protein beforehand.

What is different after 40

Muscle mass and above all strength decline over the decades, and the body responds to the same amount of protein with lower muscle protein synthesis than in younger years. That does not mean training brings less. A meta-analysis of 25 randomized trials with 1,302 older people with sarcopenia found marked improvements in strength, walking ability and grip strength from strength training.

This is also supported for everyday life: in the LIFE study with 1,635 old people, a structured exercise program over an average of 2.6 years reduced the proportion with major mobility disability to 30.1 percent, compared with 35.5 percent in the comparison group. If you start early, you build the reserve you will draw on later.

Creatine and protein: what they contribute

Creatine is well studied in older people. A meta-analysis of 11 randomized trials with people aged 60 and over found that creatine plus strength training increased strength more than training alone, with an effect size of 0.31. Muscle mass did increase on average, but the difference was not significant. In women after menopause, maximum strength on the leg press rose by 7.5 kilograms in another meta-analysis; at a low dose without strength training, no effect was measurable.

Protein powder boosts the training effect across all age groups: 49 studies with 1,863 participants showed more strength and more fat-free mass, although the additional benefit decreased with age. In people aged 60 and over, a meta-analysis of 30 studies found no effect without training and a small advantage in leg strength with strength training. The message is clear: training is the stimulus, protein and creatine are the amplifiers.

Hormones: where the line is

In men, testosterone slowly declines with age. Therapy is an approved medication for men with diagnosed hypogonadism, that is, repeatedly low values and matching symptoms. In the TRAVERSE safety trial with 5,246 men, serious cardiovascular events did not occur more often on testosterone than on placebo, whereas heart rhythm disorders occurred more often in a meta-analysis.

Peptides, SARMs and growth hormone boosters from the internet are not approved for muscle building in healthy people and their safety has barely been studied. For women in menopause, hormone therapy can improve symptoms and bones, but that is a medical decision and not a muscle-building tool.

  • Creatine monohydrate Evidence 9/10 · Well supportedThe best-studied supplement for strength: in older people, it boosted strength gains from training in a meta-analysis of 11 studies. The EFSA recognizes the effect for adults over 55 with daily intake together with strength training.
  • Testosterone & TRT Evidence 9/10 · Well supported, heavily overhypedTestosterone therapy is an established medication for proven hypogonadism; the large TRAVERSE safety trial found no increased risk of serious cardiovascular events. Without a diagnosed deficiency, it is not a means of building muscle.
  • Whey Protein Evidence 8/10 · Well supportedHigh-quality protein that supports muscle building during training, especially when food provides too little protein. In older people, it only works in combination with strength training.
  • Adjusting protein to age Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsWith age, protein needs for the same muscle protein synthesis increase. Geriatric societies give markedly lower amounts for this than the longevity scene.
  • Muscle as an Organ (Grip Strength & Myokines) Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsMuscle strength strongly predicts all-cause mortality in large cohorts, and structured training reduced major mobility disability in a randomized trial in old people.
  • Blood flow restriction training (BFR) Evidence 7/10 · Supported, with caveatsTraining with a light blood-flow restriction cuff creates a strong muscle stimulus even with low weights. Interesting for joint problems and in rehab, best with guidance.
  • Raising testosterone naturally Evidence 5/10 · Thin human evidenceStrength training, sleep and a balanced zinc status are the supported building blocks for healthy hormone balance. How strong their combined effect is has never been measured.

Frequently asked questions

Can you still build muscle at 50 or 60?

Yes, studies show marked strength gains even in people over 60 and even in older people with muscle wasting. What matters is regular training with increasing load. If you have taken a long break, it is best to start with guidance.

How often should I do strength training after 40?

The WHO recommends that adults do muscle-strengthening exercises on 2 or more days per week for all major muscle groups. For older people, balance exercises are added. More important than the perfect frequency is that you stick with it.

How much protein do I need after 40?

For healthy people over 65, geriatric societies recommend at least 1.0 to 1.2 grams per kilogram of body weight per day, and at least 1.2 grams for active people. In a large meta-analysis, protein beyond 1.62 grams per kilogram brought no further gain. Different rules apply for kidney disease.

Does creatine make sense after 40?

Creatine is one of the best-studied supplements and boosts strength gains from training in older people. The EFSA recognizes the effect for adults over 55 with a daily intake of at least 3 grams together with strength training. Without training, no measurable effect has been shown.

Do I need protein powder?

Only if you do not get enough protein from your normal diet. In older people, additional protein only worked in studies together with strength training. Powder is convenient, but not a must.

Should I take testosterone after 40?

Only with a medically proven deficiency and matching symptoms. For men with normal values, therapy for muscle building is neither approved nor sensible. Strength training and sleep are the supported natural levers.
