Lion’s Mane vs. Bacopa Monnieri

Lion’s mane or bacopa – which herbal nootropic is supported?

The short answer

Here the data clearly separate the two. Bacopa Monnieri has several meta-analyses; the largest, a 2026 network meta-analysis of 29 randomized trials with 2,107 healthy adults, finds an advantage for working memory, but none for attention and processing speed – it thus does not confirm the speed finding of an older meta-analysis. Lion’s mane has a pilot study with 41 healthy adults over 28 days: the faster response in the Stroop test was measured shortly after a single dose; after 28 days, what remained was a non-significant trend for stress, null findings and a few slightly negative results. The famous neurogenesis narrative around lion’s mane comes from cell culture and animals: the stimulation of nerve growth factor has never been measured in humans.

Why these numbers

Lion’s Mane

The neurogenesis narrative is the real finding: NGF stimulation is well documented in cell culture and animals, but has never been measured in humans. The best human study is a randomized pilot study with 41 healthy adults over 28 days (Nutrients 2023) – the faster response in the Stroop test (p = 0.005) was measured 60 minutes after a single dose; after 28 days, only a non-significant trend for stress remained (p = 0.051), along with null findings and a few slightly negative results. The authors themselves urge caution. The longest study ran for 49 weeks in mild Alzheimer’s dementia, with 6 of the 10 authors employed by the manufacturer of the product; 4 participants dropped out because of abdominal complaints, nausea and skin rash. Safety data beyond this duration are lacking.

Bacopa Monnieri

One of the few nootropics with several meta-analyses. The largest, a 2026 network meta-analysis of 29 RCTs with 2,107 healthy adults, finds an advantage for working memory compared with placebo (SMD 2.03; 95% CI 1.28–2.78) and explicitly none for attention and processing speed. It thus does not reproduce the speed finding of the older meta-analysis by Kongkeaw (9 RCTs, 518 participants: Trail B −17.9 ms, choice reaction time 10.6 ms, both p < 0.001). What holds across the studies is a single domain: memory, more precisely free and delayed recall (Pase 2012: 9 of 17 tests). The effect takes about twelve weeks, little is repeated at the level of individual tests, and a good share of the positive studies come from Australia, some from the same group with the same extract. Acetylcholinesterase inhibition has been measured in plasma in humans; long-term data are lacking.

Frequently asked questions

How quickly does bacopa work?

Not immediately. The studies with positive results typically run over twelve weeks. An acute effect after a single dose is not supported – that distinguishes bacopa from L-theanine, for example.

Does lion’s mane really promote nerve growth?

In cell culture and in animal models, the stimulation of nerve growth factor is well documented. In humans, it has never been measured directly. The leap from the Petri dish to neurogenesis in the human brain is the uncovered part of the advertising.

Are medicinal mushrooms generally poorly supported?

The data are thin for all of them, but thin to different degrees. For cordyceps, there is a meta-analysis on endurance performance in athletes, for reishi a Cochrane review with mixed results, for chaga practically nothing except two uncontrolled papers from the 1970s – alongside documented kidney damage from oxalate.
