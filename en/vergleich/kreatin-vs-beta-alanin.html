Creatine monohydrate vs. Beta-alanine

Creatine or beta-alanine – which does more for your training?

The short answer

There is no head-to-head trial, and the two act at different points. Creatine fills the phosphocreatine stores and increases muscle strength, shown in meta-analyses in people aged 60 and over and in postmenopausal women; EFSA recognises the link with muscle strength as causal under narrow conditions. Beta-alanine fills the carnosine store, an acid buffer, and gives a small edge in hard efforts of about 1 to 4 minutes, with an effect size of 0.18 across 40 studies. Where the data stop: beta-alanine shows no effect for repeated sprints, and for creatine, muscle mass and overall cognition miss significance. Creatine's safety is better studied; the German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) rates the safety data on beta-alanine as poor.

Why these numbers

Creatine monohydrate

One of the best-studied supplements of all: numerous RCTs and meta-analyses on strength and lean mass, the phosphocreatine pathway is measurable in humans, and safety data span years. Points are deducted on hype distance only because the cognitive promises go beyond the data.

Beta-alanine

Rarely is a mechanism so cleanly demonstrated in humans: muscle biopsies show a dose- and time-dependent rise in carnosine (Harris 2006: +64.2 % after 4 weeks with 6.4 g; Hill 2007: +80.1 % after 10 weeks), and the response rate is 99.3 %. The meta-analysis by Saunders et al. 2017 (40 studies, 1,461 participants) finds an effect size of 0.18, moderated by exercise duration; Hobson et al. 2012 see the clearest effect at 60 to 240 seconds, still an advantage beyond that, and none below 60 seconds. Georgiou et al. 2024 confirm the benefit at 4 to 10 minutes (ES 0.55). For repeated sprints, a meta-analysis of 17 RCTs shows no effect. Safety: Dolan et al. 2019 (101 human studies) found paraesthesia to be the only side effect; the BfR nevertheless rated the data quality as poor in 2026, because side effects were hardly recorded systematically, almost only young men took part and data beyond 24 weeks are missing, and it considers only single doses below 400 mg and less than 1.2 g per day to be safe. Application: widely used in sport since the first human studies in 2006, regulated inconsistently (Poland with a maximum amount, France as a medicine for hot flushes).

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for strength, creatine or beta-alanine?

For strength, creatine is clearly better documented. In people aged 60 and over, muscle strength as the primary endpoint reached significance (g = 0.31), and in postmenopausal women the one-repetition maximum on the leg press rose by 7.5 kg, with no variation between studies. For beta-alanine, the ISSN sees a need for further research on strength training. Its effect lies in efforts between roughly half a minute and ten minutes, especially in tests to exhaustion.

Does beta-alanine or creatine help with sprints?

For beta-alanine this has been tested, with a negative result: a meta-analysis of 17 randomized trials found no benefit for repeated sprints in either mean or peak power, and the first meta-analysis had already found no benefit for efforts under 60 seconds. The explanation given is that short sprints depend mainly on creatine phosphate, not on acid buffering. The sources reviewed here contain no sprint study on creatine.

How quickly do creatine and beta-alanine work?

Neither works acutely; both act through stores that first have to fill up. For beta-alanine this has been measured by muscle biopsy: carnosine content rose clearly after 4 weeks and by 80.1 percent after 10 weeks, with an estimated response rate of 99.3 percent. Creatine tops up the phosphocreatine stores in the muscle cell. Users report noticing the effect gradually over weeks, mainly through rising training weights; these are user reports, not measurements.

What side effects do creatine and beta-alanine have?

For creatine, the main effect described is water retention in the muscle; lean mass and strength were tracked over up to 104 weeks without reported safety signals. With existing kidney disease, a doctor should be consulted. For beta-alanine, skin tingling was the only side effect reported across 101 human studies, and according to the BfR it usually subsides within 60 to 90 minutes. The BfR nonetheless rates the safety data as poor, and beyond 24 weeks there are none at all.
