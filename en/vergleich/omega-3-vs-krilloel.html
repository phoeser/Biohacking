Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) vs. Krill Oil

Fish oil or krill oil – does the phospholipid form really deliver more?

The short answer

Both supply the same fatty acids, EPA and DHA, and both lower triglycerides. The difference lies in the depth of the data: for omega-3 there are large outcome trials such as VITAL, STRENGTH and ASCEND, although they missed their primary endpoints; in 2019 the EMA removed the indication after a heart attack, while the approval for lowering triglycerides remained. Cardiovascular events have never been studied for krill oil. Direct comparisons exist for absorption and blood lipids: per gram of omega-3, both lowered blood lipids equally (Kim 2020). For the rise in the blood, Loukil 2026 found an increase about 1.5 times higher with krill oil, while Vosskötter 2023 found no difference in the omega-3 index. Krill oil is unsuitable for people with a shellfish allergy.

Why these numbers

Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)

Large randomized trials (including VITAL, REDUCE-IT, STRENGTH) with hard endpoints exist – although their results partly contradict each other. Mechanism and safety are well studied; far more is promoted than the studies support.

Krill Oil

Evidence 6, because several randomized trials with krill oil itself exist whose results diverge by endpoint: triglycerides fell (Mozaffarian 2022, 520 patients), strength and muscle thickness rose in older adults (Alkhedhairi 2022, 102 participants), knee pain in osteoarthritis was unchanged (Laslett 2024, 262 participants); cardiovascular events have not been studied for krill oil. Mechanism 7, because the incorporation of EPA and DHA into blood and red blood cells has been measured in humans, but the advantage of the phospholipid form is inconsistent: a 1.5-fold greater rise in Loukil 2026, the same omega-3 index in Vosskötter 2023. Safety 6, because controlled data covering up to 6 months and an EU authorization as a novel food with maximum levels and crustacean labelling exist. Hype 5, because krill oil is marketed as a superior alternative to fish oil, while blood lipids show no difference per gram of omega-3 (Kim 2020) and the absorption advantage does not appear in all studies. Application 6, because it has been sold in the EU for years as an authorized novel food. Direction mixed: positive findings on triglycerides and muscle strength alongside a large negative trial on knee osteoarthritis.

Frequently asked questions

Is krill oil better than fish oil?

Not for blood lipids. A network meta-analysis of 64 randomized trials (Kim 2020) found no significant difference between krill oil and fish oil for any blood lipid value; what mattered was the amount of omega-3, not the source. On absorption, the direct comparisons contradict each other. For hard endpoints such as heart attack or stroke there are no studies on krill oil, whereas for omega-3 from fish and algal oil there are several large randomized trials.

Is omega-3 from krill oil absorbed better?

The evidence is inconsistent. In 72 healthy people over 12 weeks, double-blind and at the same omega-3 amount, Loukil 2026 found a plasma rise in EPA and DHA about 1.5 times stronger with krill oil. Vosskötter 2023 saw no difference in the omega-3 index between krill, fish and Calanus oil in 62 healthy people taking low amounts. The network meta-analysis by Pham 2024 across 26 studies sees an advantage for krill oil mainly at lower amounts.

Do omega-3 or krill oil protect the heart?

For omega-3 this was tested on a large scale: VITAL with 25,871 participants missed its primary endpoint (HR 0.92), as did STRENGTH and ASCEND. The heart attack finding from VITAL (HR 0.72) is a secondary result and therefore a signal, not proof. In 2019 the EMA removed the indication for secondary prevention after a heart attack. For krill oil there are no studies on heart attack or stroke. The authorized EU claim on normal heart function applies to EPA and DHA, not specifically to krill oil.

How do fish oil and krill oil differ on safety?

The data on omega-3 are exceptionally broad: a meta-analysis of 114,592 participants found increased atrial fibrillation only in people at high cardiovascular risk on high doses (OR 1.43). In STRENGTH, gastrointestinal complaints were more frequent, 24.7 versus 14.7 percent. For krill oil, controlled data extend to 6 months, with side effects about as frequent as on placebo. Krill is a crustacean, so it is unsuitable with a shellfish allergy. With anticoagulants and before surgery, both call for medical advice.
