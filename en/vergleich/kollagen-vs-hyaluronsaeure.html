Collagen peptides vs. hyaluronic acid

Collagen or hyaluronic acid – which does more for the skin?

The short answer

Both have human studies, and both show the same methodological pattern: short durations of eight to twelve weeks, cosmetic measurement methods instead of clinical endpoints, and a large share of the studies co-funded by raw material manufacturers. On human evidence, oral hyaluronic acid is now slightly ahead: a meta-analysis of seven RCTs shows improved skin moisture, elasticity and wrinkle depth, and the largest single study, with 150 healthy adults, confirms this after twelve weeks. For collagen peptides there are RCTs on skin moisture and joint complaints, but they are small and mostly manufacturer-funded. Mechanistically, for both it remains open how the swallowed molecule is supposed to act in the skin after being broken down in the gut – it is explained by a stimulated synthesis in the body, but that has not been proven.

Why these numbers

Collagen peptides

There are RCTs on skin moisture and joint complaints, but they are small and mostly manufacturer-funded. The mechanism remains disputed because collagen, like any protein, is digested. Very heavily advertised.

Full entry on collagen peptides

Hyaluronic acid

A meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (J Drugs Dermatol 2025) shows improved skin moisture, elasticity and wrinkle depth; the largest single study, with 150 healthy adults (Sci Rep 2025), confirms this after 12 weeks, more clearly with 120 mg than with 60 mg. In osteoarthritis, 9 of 11 studies with 597 patients report an improvement, but the one-year placebo-controlled trial only in the subgroup up to 70 years of age. Measurements use cosmetic methods, and the largest skin studies come from raw material manufacturers. That the molecule is broken down by gut bacteria, absorbed and distributed to the skin has only been shown in rats.

Frequently asked questions

Why is manufacturer funding a problem?

It does not make a study wrong, but it shifts the probabilities: studies with manufacturer involvement systematically report positive results more often. When an entire field is predominantly funded this way, you should read the overall effect more cautiously than the individual study.

What does cosmetic surrogate marker mean?

Skin moisture, elasticity and wrinkle depth are measured with devices – not whether someone ends up looking younger or being healthier. These are valid measurements, but they answer a narrower question than the advertising suggests.

Does hyaluronic acid also work for the joints?

Injection into the joint is an established, if disputed, treatment with its own body of studies. Oral intake for the joints should not be confused with it – the data for that are considerably thinner.
