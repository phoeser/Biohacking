NMN vs. Nicotinamide Riboside (NR)

NMN or NR – which NAD+ precursor is better supported?

The short answer

Neither has shown a clinical benefit in humans. Both reliably raise NAD+ levels in the blood – that is a surrogate marker, not an outcome. There are more placebo-controlled studies on nicotinamide riboside, and a critical review in Science Advances 2023 concludes that most clinical endpoints remained unaffected; the only replicated finding is a reduction in inflammatory markers. NMN also has a regulatory problem: since 2022, the US authority no longer classifies it as a dietary supplement because it is being studied as a drug candidate. If you choose between the two, you are choosing between two substances whose benefit is an open question.

Why these numbers

NMN

In humans there are now 15 small randomized trials lasting 2 to 24 weeks; one meta-analysis (Yang 2026) finds no effects on weight, blood glucose, blood lipids or blood pressure, another none on muscle function. Endpoint data are lacking; the NAD pathway is plausible, but the increase in tissue is weakly supported. Safety data are short-term only – and hardly any longevity product is more overhyped.

Full entry on NMN

Nicotinamide riboside (NR)

An unusually large number of RCTs for a longevity supplement – with a clear pattern: NAD+ levels in the blood rise reliably, and that has been reproduced. A critical review (Science Advances 2023) concludes that most clinical endpoints – insulin sensitivity, glucose control, body composition, blood pressure – remained unaffected; the only replicated finding is a reduction in inflammatory markers. For aging itself, there are no endpoint data in humans. Safety has been properly studied over weeks to months, including in a high-dose study in Parkinson's disease.

Frequently asked questions

Do NMN and NR really raise NAD+ levels?

Yes, that is the best-supported part. Several placebo-controlled studies show a measurable rise in NAD+ levels in the blood. What remains open is whether a health benefit follows from this – clinical endpoints such as insulin sensitivity, blood pressure and body composition remained largely unchanged in the studies.

Why is NMN no longer allowed as a supplement in the US?

The US authority no longer classifies a substance as a dietary supplement once it is in clinical testing as a drug candidate. That is exactly the case with NMN. In the EU, NMN is not authorized as a novel food.

What would proof of benefit require?

A randomized trial with a hard endpoint – mortality, disease incidence, functional capacity – over years rather than weeks. Such studies do not exist for either substance.
