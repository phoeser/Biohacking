Peptides 2026 compared: evidence and approval status

23 peptides from BPC-157 to thymosin alpha-1: what is approved, what they are marketed for and what has been shown in humans.

By Paul Höser · 5 min read · updated November 15, 2026

The short answer

The best-studied peptides are those approved somewhere for a specific disease: thymosin alpha-1 in over 30 countries, tesamorelin, PT-141 and SS-31 in the US, each for narrow indications. None of the 23 peptides is approved EU-wide. The best-known peptides in the scene, such as BPC-157, TB-500 or KPV, rest mainly on animal studies, and their effect in humans has barely been tested. Many of them are banned in sport at all times (as of 15 November 2026).

How to read the table

  • Approval does not mean suitable for biohacking. Tesamorelin, for example, is approved only for belly fat in HIV lipodystrophy, PT-141 only for women with low sexual desire. For other purposes, these data do not exist.
  • Animal studies are a start, not proof. For peptides such as BPC-157 or TB-500, results in rats are often impressive; whether they hold up in humans is open.
  • The BK-Score rates the state of knowledge. A low value means little tested, not necessarily ineffective.
  • No doses, no sources. For unapproved substances we deliberately give no amounts and link no sellers.

Caution with "research peptides"

Unapproved peptides are usually sold as research chemicals "not for human consumption". Purity, content and sterility are not tested, and injections from such sources carry infection risks. Competitive athletes should note that many of these substances are on the prohibited list.

Frequently asked questions

Which peptides are approved?

None of those listed here EU-wide. Thymosin alpha-1 is approved as Zadaxin in over 30 countries and nationally in Italy. In the US, tesamorelin (belly fat in HIV lipodystrophy), PT-141 as Vyleesi (women with low sexual desire) and SS-31 as elamipretide (Barth syndrome) are approved. Semax and Selank are registered in Russia.

Has BPC-157 been studied in humans?

Barely. The healing effects come from animal studies. In humans there are a few small reports, and one completed trial was never published. BPC-157 is banned in competitive sport.

Are peptides legal?

In Germany, unapproved peptides are not marketable medicines, and placing them on the market is prohibited. Some, such as CJC-1295 and ipamorelin, are also named in the German Anti-Doping Act, which also penalises possession of larger amounts. This is not legal advice.

Which peptide has the best evidence?

Measured by human data, thymosin alpha-1 and tesamorelin, each for their approved uses. For typical biohacking goals such as faster healing, more muscle or longevity, none of the peptides is convincingly supported in humans.
