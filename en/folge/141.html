Podcast episode 141 (in German)

Humanin – the peptide from the part of the brain that had remained intact

November 18, 2026

Humanin was found in Tokyo in 2001 in the part of an Alzheimer’s brain that had remained intact, and its blueprint lies in the mitochondria. The mechanism is well supported; to this day, nobody has tested the peptide in humans.

What it is about

Humanin consists of 24 amino acids and is encoded not in the cell nucleus but in the DNA of the mitochondria. It was the first evidence that the cell’s power plants also send signals to the body; in 2003, 2 other research groups independently came across the same molecule. The episode goes through the observations in humans, which for a long time formed a nice picture until a larger Italian cohort shook it. Added to this is the only experiment close to actual use: mice whose body composition improved, but not their lifespan. Humanin hardly appears in peptide forums; those who write there are, by their own accounts, mostly looking for repair for tendons and everyday life. In the end there is a way to raise one’s own level that has been measured in humans: training.

Key points

  • In the naked mole-rat, which lives exceptionally long, the humanin level remains stable over 2 decades, while in almost all other species studied it declines with age.
  • An Italian study of 569 people found that the level rose with age in healthy people, and 102 children of centenarians did not differ from 92 controls.
  • Nematodes with a permanent humanin excess lived on average 19 instead of 17.7 days; the effect depended on the same gene through which calorie restriction also works.
  • Mice that received a humanin analogue twice a week for 14 months from the age of 18 months had less abdominal fat, more lean mass and lower growth factor levels, but no longer lifespan.
  • The authors themselves explain the unchanged lifespan by humanin being stable in the blood for only about 30 minutes; there is no registered study with humanin as an active ingredient in humans.
  • As a report: a British forum analysis of 493 discussion threads and just under 15,000 posts describes mainly men between 40 and 60 who are concerned with tendons and everyday life; humanin hardly appears in it at all.
  • After 12 weeks of strength training, 55 men with impaired glucose metabolism had 35 percent more humanin in muscle, and after hard intervals it also rises in the blood.

Where the data stop

In humans there are only measurements of the body’s own level, with different test methods and values that differ many times over. None of them says whether a high level is a cause or merely the indicator of healthy mitochondria. The sister substance MOTS-c is further along: a study with 120 adults has been running since February 2026, with no result as of September 2026. For humanin there is nothing comparable.

No reported side effects here only means that nobody looked. In a mouse model of an aggressive type of breast cancer, administered humanin made tumors grow faster and weakened chemotherapy; anyone who has or has had cancer discusses experiments with cell protection with a doctor. For the products there is no pharmacopoeia and no purity standard; forums tell of a vial that turned to slime in the refrigerator. Such reports are meant seriously but are not representative, and those who notice nothing write less often.
