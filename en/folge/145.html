Podcast episode 145 (in German)

Update: Retatrutide and women – cycle, pill and menopause

November 22, 2026

In the phase 2 trial, women on retatrutide lost 28.5 percent of their body weight, men 21.9 percent. Paul and Paula look at what the new weight-loss drug means for women – from the cycle and the pill to menopause.

What it is about

Retatrutide mimics three hormones: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. It is not approved anywhere yet; Eli Lilly plans to file in the US in the first quarter of 2027. This episode takes up a question several listeners asked after the first retatrutide update: what is known about women? Much of it so far comes from the sibling drugs tirzepatide and semaglutide – Paul and Paula say so at every point.

Key points

  • In the phase 2 trial, women at the second-highest dose lost 28.5 percent of their weight, men 21.9 percent. In the phase 3 trial TRIUMPH-1, where almost two thirds of participants were women, women reached up to 30.8 percent according to congress reports, men 23.2 percent – the publication itself does not state this.
  • Tirzepatide shows the same gap: in Mayo Clinic treatment data on just over 1,000 people, women lost 15.1 percent after 15 months, men 10.7 percent. Sex predicted success, age did not. Why remains unclear.
  • Urinary tract infections occurred in TRIUMPH-1 in 7.5 to almost 9 percent depending on dose, on placebo in just over 5 percent – 92 percent of those affected were women. Mostly mild to moderate, hardly anyone stopped because of them. Other side effects have not yet been published by sex.
  • The pill: a trial of retatrutide with a combined pill was completed in July 2024, no results have been released. For tirzepatide, the US FDA advises adding a non-oral method for four weeks after starting and after each dose increase; the EMA considers the reduction in the same measurements not clinically relevant.
  • Pregnancy plans: in women with PCOS, such drugs roughly triple the chance of a spontaneous pregnancy in an analysis of three trials. Before a planned pregnancy, a break of two months applies for semaglutide and one month for tirzepatide; retatrutide has no prescribing information and therefore no such period.
  • Menopause: in the SURMOUNT trials of tirzepatide, weight loss was 26 percent before menopause and 23 percent each during and after. In a Mayo Clinic observational study of 120 women, those on hormone therapy lost 19.2 percent, those without 14 percent.

Where the data stop

Almost everything on the pill, the cycle, pregnancy and menopause so far comes from tirzepatide and semaglutide, not from retatrutide itself. The sex differences from TRIUMPH-1 are congress reports, not a peer-reviewed analysis.

The Mayo study on hormone therapy is not randomised; the women may have differed in other ways. The Spanish Menopause Society sees no reason to start hormone therapy for weight loss alone.

In an analysis of more than 13,500 user reports on retatrutide, cycle problems do not appear; for semaglutide and tirzepatide, users mention cycle irregularities and hot flushes that trials barely recorded. Such reports say nothing about frequency or cause. What is sold online as retatrutide is not the trial drug.
