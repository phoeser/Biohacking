Podcast episode 5 (in German)

SLU-PP-332: exercise in a syringe? The training molecule fact-checked

July 12, 2026

SLU-PP-332 flips a switch in the cell that endurance training normally flips. Untrained mice ran around 70 percent longer and 45 percent farther after a single administration, and an experiment with genetically modified animals shows that it really is this switch. The substance has not been studied in humans.

What it is about

SLU-PP-332 is not a peptide but a small molecule designed in the lab by the group of pharmacologist Thomas Burris; SLU stands for Saint Louis University. It switches on the ERR receptors, all three variants, most strongly the alpha variant. Despite their name, they do not bind the body’s own estrogen but control, in the cell nucleus, the genes for new mitochondria, cellular respiration and fat burning, above all in the heart, skeletal muscle and liver. Their most important partner is the protein PGC-1-alpha, and exactly these switches also turn on during endurance training. The episode goes through the mouse experiments on endurance and overweight, the data on the heart, kidney and human muscle cells, and the reports from the scene. At the end stands a carefully tested mechanism whose benefit in humans nobody has studied yet.

Key points

  • In the 2023 study in ACS Chemical Biology, untrained mice ran around 70 percent longer and 45 percent farther than untreated animals after a single administration; there were 6 animals per group.
  • Mice genetically lacking the ERR-alpha variant in muscle gained no endurance after 14 days of treatment: the effect really does run through this switch. In mouse muscle, a gene program switched on that resembles the one after acute endurance exercise.
  • After longer administration, grip strength and the share of enduring, oxidative type IIa muscle fibers increased, and the amount of mitochondrial DNA in the muscle rose; in normal-weight mice, fat burning was already 25 percent higher within the first hours.
  • In 2024, overweight mice weighed around 12 percent less after 28 days of treatment, even though they ate just as much: the control animals gained about 5 grams of fat, the treated ones less than half a gram, and insulin sensitivity improved.
  • In a mouse model of heart failure, SLU-PP-332 and a sister compound improved pumping function, reduced fibrotic remodeling and prolonged survival; in 21-month-old mice, an 8-week treatment reversed age-related kidney changes, including protein in the urine.
  • In 2025, muscle precursor cells from physically inactive women who were receiving a new hip formed more PGC-1-alpha and ERR-alpha in the lab, had less oxidative stress and formed plenty of precursors of muscle fibers: the mechanism also works in human tissue.
  • As a report: people who use the substance from the scene consistently describe a quickly noticeable endurance effect, and some measure it by their own running or cycling times.

Where the data stop

A systematic review from 2026 found exclusively preclinical work, and the efficacy data come mostly from one research network around the same group; independent replications are rare. Whether the amounts and routes common in the scene reach active levels like those in the animal experiments at all has never been measured, and expectation and day-to-day form cannot be factored out of one’s own stopwatch. In 2026 the developers themselves write that SLU-PP-332 is not sufficiently absorbed by mouth and have developed a successor, SLU-PP-915, that also works when swallowed; tablets of the original therefore lack a basis. Training is also more than mitochondria: it changes blood vessels, the autonomic nervous system, inflammation and the messengers released by muscle, and a molecule acting on a single switch does not reproduce that.

Safety data in humans are completely lacking. In animals, the group found no obvious toxicity after 10 days, and blood count, blood salts and the muscle marker creatine kinase remained unremarkable; because these switches broadly reset the metabolism of the heart, liver, kidney and muscle, long-term consequences are hard to predict, and there is nothing on interactions, pregnancy or pre-existing conditions. SLU-PP-332 is not approved as a medicine anywhere, and with research material, purity and content are untested. In sport it falls under class S0 of the prohibited list and is banned at all times, and doping labs already described its breakdown products in 2026. The BK-Score reads “Not studied in humans”, with 0 for human studies and 5 for the mechanism: not tested and refuted, but not yet tested. Anyone toying with the idea should get medical advice first.
