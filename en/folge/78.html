Podcast episode 78 (in German)

Urolithin A: the mitochondria renewer fact-checked

September 16, 2026

Urolithin A is not formed in fruit but in the gut — when the right bacteria break down pomegranate tannins. Only some people have them. The episode checks what the substance sets in motion in the cell and how much of that is actually supported in humans.

What it is about

Mitophagy is the quality control by which a cell recognizes, dismantles and recycles its damaged mitochondria; with age this clean-up slows down, especially in muscle. Urolithin A demonstrably restarts it — it does not imitate energy but maintenance, and that makes it mechanistically the most elegant candidate in the supplement corner. The episode goes through the production chain from the fruit shelf to the cell, sorts the randomized human studies with their moderate but consistent muscle effects, names the price tag and manufacturer dominance, and asks for whom the experiment makes the most sense based on current data and for whom the expected additional benefit remains small.

Key points

  • Only about a third to 40 percent of people form appreciable urolithin levels after pomegranate juice, and you cannot tell your own microbiome from the outside.
  • The 2016 paper showed a more than 40 percent longer lifespan in roundworms and made old mice measurably more enduring.
  • In the 2022 randomized study, just over 60 adults over 60 took 1,000 milligrams daily for 4 months: better muscle endurance and lower inflammatory markers, but unchanged walking speed.
  • A Swiss study in 40- to 65-year-olds found around 12 percent more muscle strength compared with placebo over 4 months.
  • The studies work with 500 to 1,000 milligrams of pure urolithin A; products that instead tout pomegranate extract shift the problem back into the bacteria lottery.
  • Branded products quickly cost 80 to 120 euros a month; generics are following and slowly pushing prices down, and the natural route via pomegranate remains a microbiome lottery.
  • Anyone who tries it sets a before-and-after anchor over 3 to 4 months, following the pattern of the series: grip strength, number of repetitions in a standard exercise or simply breathlessness on the stairs.

Where the data stop

Almost all human research on urolithin A is funded by one manufacturer or accompanied by it through co-authorship. That does not make the data wrong, but independent replication is largely missing, and the specialist press is calling for exactly that. The effects are moderate and concentrated on muscle endpoints; whether this translates in the long term into less frailty, a better healthspan or even years of life, nobody knows.

The long-term data end at 4 months, and 4 months without problems is something different from ten years of proven use. The advertising has long since outrun what is supported: on skin there are first small placebo-controlled data, on the immune system an animal study from 2024, on the brain mouse data and an ongoing study — hypothesis, not result. Moreover, the substance has never been tested against or together with systematic strength training, which performs considerably better on the same endpoints.
