Podcast episode 84 (in German)

Ca-AKG – eight years younger, re-tested twice, nothing both times

September 22, 2026

One researcher appears at four points in this story: on a celebrated mouse study, on an analysis of customers of a longevity product, on a null result – and on a study in Singapore whose result nobody knows yet. The episode follows the zigzag path of calcium alpha-ketoglutarate from start to finish.

What it is about

Alpha-ketoglutarate is an intermediate of the citric acid cycle that the body constantly produces itself, and at the same time the co-factor of those enzymes that remove methylation marks from DNA. That gives it everything a longevity story needs: endogenous, central to metabolism, epigenetically relevant, lower in old age. The episode covers the 2020 mouse study, the origin of the “8 years younger” figure, the independent re-test in mice and the experience that already exists in humans – from dialysis patients to women with declining bone density. Finally, it looks at the randomized ABLE trial, whose main result is expected soon.

Key points

  • In the Buck Institute study from 2020, mice received Ca-AKG in their feed from the age of 18 months; the females lived around 12 percent longer on average. The period of illness and frailty at the end of life shrank by just over half in females and by 40 percent in males, and there were fewer inflammatory messengers in the blood.
  • The “8 years younger” figure comes from an analysis of 42 customers of a Ca-AKG product, around 63 years old on average, who had their methylation age tested before and after: it fell in 40 of them, by just over 8 years on average in men and just under 7 in women.
  • The 13 participants who, according to questionnaires, had changed nothing about diet, sleep or exercise ended up almost 10 years below their calendar age, compared with just over 2 before. There was no placebo group; one of the authors advises the manufacturer and sits on its board, which is stated openly in the paper.
  • In the independent testing program in genetically mixed mice, starting at 18 and at 7 months, the animals did not live measurably longer, the males about 841 versus 813 days. However, only around a sixth of the targeted amount actually reached the feed; the program itself writes that timing and dose appear to be decisive.
  • There is experience in humans: in the nineties, Ca-AKG was tested as a phosphate binder in dialysis patients, and in 14 patients over three years the phosphate level fell. Its relative ornithine-AKG improved appetite, weight and independence in 194 older people recovering from acute illness.
  • In a small randomized study of 76 postmenopausal women with declining bone density, a blood marker of bone loss fell by just over a third after six months and density rose slightly, compared with calcium alone.
  • The ABLE trial from Singapore gives 120 people aged 40 to 60 whose methylation clock runs older than their calendar age either Ca-AKG or placebo for six months, double-blind. The intake phase is complete, an analysis of the trial conduct appeared in September 2026, and the main result has not yet been published; a smaller placebo-controlled study from China with 34 participants has also been completed, without a published result.

Where the data stop

On biological age, the only human data so far are the customer analysis without a control group, and epigenetic clocks can jump by years from one measurement to the next. In mice the question is open: one positive finding and two null results in which only a fraction of the planned amount reached the feed. The BK score stands at human evidence 2, mechanism 4, application experience 4 and hype gap 1, because the package speaks louder than the data; with the ABLE result, the 2 may soon move. The greatest hope lies in what is hardly advertised: less frailty and more stable bones.

User reports are thin and unspectacular: some describe stomach rumbling that usually improves with food, occasionally heartburn. Taken by mouth, alpha-ketoglutarate raises the blood level only briefly. The calcium counts if you already take some; anyone with a history of kidney stones should discuss it with a doctor.
