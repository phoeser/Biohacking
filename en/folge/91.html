Cagrilintide – 22.7% and still “disappointing”

September 29, 2026

In 1987, two research groups found an unknown hormone in deposits in the pancreas: amylin. With cagrilintide and the duo CagriSema, it has become one of the most powerful weight-loss drugs ever tested – and the FDA is deciding on it in these weeks.

What it is about

Cagrilintide is a replica of amylin, a satiety hormone that is released together with insulin from the same cells. It is a second route to the same goal, independent of GLP-1 — and this is exactly what the combination CagriSema relies on, in which cagrilintide and semaglutide are combined in one injection. The episode tells the backstory with pramlintide and the buffet experiments, the studies on cagrilintide alone and in the duo, why a record result was seen as a disappointment on the stock market, the head-to-head comparisons with tirzepatide and what participants report about food thoughts becoming quieter. Plus the question of why what you can buy on its own is not what was tested.

Key points

  • Amylin is released together with insulin from the same cells and signals the end of a meal to the brainstem. Its predecessor pramlintide has been approved since 2005: in buffet experiments, people with overweight ate about a sixth less after one injection, and users rated their treatment better than their previous one – but it had to be injected with every meal.
  • Cagrilintide alone, once a week, reduced weight in the phase 2 study in the Lancet in 2021 with just over 700 participants by 10.8 percent after 26 weeks, versus 3.0 percent on placebo; in REDEFINE 1, the cagrilintide arm reached 11.8 percent after 68 weeks, semaglutide alone 16.1 percent.
  • In REDEFINE 1 with 3,417 adults over 68 weeks, participants on CagriSema lost 22.7 percent when taking it as planned, versus 2.3 percent on placebo, and 20.4 percent when all randomized participants including dropouts are counted, versus 3.0 percent. Only 57 percent were on the highest dose at the end.
  • On the day of the data in December 2024, Novo Nordisk’s share price fell by just over 20 percent because 25 percent had been announced beforehand. In the head-to-head comparison with tirzepatide over 84 weeks (REDEFINE 4), CagriSema reached 23.0 versus 25.5 percent; in type 2 diabetes, a low CagriSema dose was ahead of a low tirzepatide dose after 60 weeks, at 12.4 versus 9.1 percent (REIMAGINE 5, September 2026).
  • Almost 80 percent of participants in REDEFINE 1 had gastrointestinal complaints versus 40 percent on placebo, mostly mild to moderate and mainly during dose escalation; 6 percent stopped because of them, versus 3.7 percent.
  • In a study presented at the EASD congress in September 2026, participants reported after just over five months and after one year that constant thoughts about food had become quieter and cravings less frequent; in the brain scanner, images of high-calorie foods triggered less response in reward regions.
  • The application has been with the FDA since December 2025, with a decision expected in the fourth quarter of 2026; CagriSema is not approved in the EU. The episode gives human evidence 8, safety 7, application 4 and hype gap 5 with a positive direction – the 8 belongs to the combination and not to the single substance.

Where the data stop

What happens to weight after stopping is the question that matters most in everyday life; a study with an 80-week extension is due to report in the first half of 2027, and a cardiovascular study with around 7,000 people is under way. The result also belongs to the group in which it was measured: adults with overweight or obesity, not someone who wants to lose a few kilos for the summer. With figures, it is always worth asking for whom they were calculated – for everyone or for those who stayed on.

The second limit concerns the product itself. The big numbers belong to the combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide, not to the single peptide. Peptide shops sell cagrilintide on its own, sometimes as a pre-mixed “CagriSema”; in a not yet peer-reviewed analysis of more than 6,000 independent lab tests from this market, just under 42 percent of samples failed standards that a pharmacy would have to meet. Drugs of this kind belong in a physician’s hands, because losing 20 kilograms in a year is an intervention in metabolism that someone should accompany.
