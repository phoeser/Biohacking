Podcast episode 85 (in German)

Ergothioneine – a door in every cell, and the study nobody cites

September 23, 2026

First extracted in 1909 from ergot of all things, produced only by fungi and a few soil bacteria – and yet our body keeps a dedicated cell door ready for ergothioneine. The episode takes a walk through the woods: from cohort studies in Sweden and Japan to a placebo-controlled study with oyster mushrooms.

What it is about

Ergothioneine is a derivative of the amino acid histidine that neither plants nor animals can produce — only fungi and some soil bacteria. The episode explores why a substance that we take in exclusively with food has been given its own cell door, and what the data behind it show. It covers the large observational studies that link high blood levels with less heart disease and less dementia, the small studies in which the substance was given, the recurring signal on sleep and a randomized study with oyster mushrooms. At the end comes the question of whether research itself currently prefers the frying pan to the capsule.

Key points

  • Only fungi and some soil bacteria can make ergothioneine; the body takes it up via the transporter OCTN1, which carries almost nothing but this substance. Of ergothioneine swallowed by volunteers, less than 4 percent reappeared in the urine; the rest stays in the tissues for weeks to months.
  • In the Malmö cohort, 112 metabolites were measured in 3,236 people without heart disease, who were followed for 21 years; of all of them, ergothioneine was most clearly linked to a healthy diet. Each standard deviation more in the blood went along with 21 percent fewer cardiovascular deaths and 14 percent fewer deaths overall.
  • In the Japanese Hisayama study of just over 1,300 people over 65, the quarter with the highest levels developed dementia 44 percent less often over 11 years. The association held after vegetable intake was accounted for; a beverage company co-funded the study.
  • In Singapore, 19 older people with mild memory problems improved on a learning test over one year on ergothioneine, and a blood marker of nerve damage remained stable. The largest study, with 147 participants aged 55 to 79, missed its primary goal, a composite memory score, after 16 weeks, but reaction time and falling asleep improved.
  • Sleep comes up repeatedly: in Japan, 100 adults reported waking up more refreshed and sleeping longer after four months, and in China, 40 exhausted mothers felt less mentally tired after weaning. Almost all of these studies were paid for or carried out by companies – from three countries and three different firms.
  • In a randomized study from Reading (June 2026), 80 healthy people aged 60 to 80 who took four portions of oyster mushroom powder per week for twelve weeks remembered learned words better than on placebo powder, and their mood held steady.
  • Porcini contain more than 7 milligrams per gram of dry weight, oyster mushrooms up to 4, button mushrooms around 0.4 — 100 grams of fresh button mushrooms come to roughly 3 milligrams. Synthetic ergothioneine has been authorized in the EU as a novel food since 2017, with a maximum of 30 milligrams per day for adults; in the studies there were no side effects beyond placebo.

Where the data stop

Much of what speaks for ergothioneine comes from observation. The authors of the Malmö paper name the obvious explanation themselves: people who eat a lot of mushrooms and vegetables automatically have more of it in their blood. Hisayama weakens this objection, but an observational study can never rule it out. The studies in which the substance was given are small and mostly funded by manufacturers; the longest lasted one year. A large study in Singapore with around 600 participants over two years only starts in October 2026 according to the registry – and it tests powder from golden oyster mushroom, not isolated ergothioneine. The BK score stands at human evidence 5, safety data 7, application experience 3 and hype gap 2: one of the rare cases in which safety is better studied than efficacy.

The mechanism is open as well. Barry Halliwell, who has studied the substance for years, wrote in 2023 that the evidence for a primary antioxidant role in the body is weak; his review from August 2026 was more confident, but much of the basic biology remains unclear. There are no data on high amounts over decades, and variants in the transporter gene are linked to Crohn’s disease and rheumatoid arthritis – what this means for a permanently higher intake is unknown. Pregnant and breastfeeding women are excluded from the authorization because the application left them out, not because of a known risk.
