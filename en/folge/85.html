Podcast episode 85 (in German)

Ergothioneine – a door in every cell, and the study nobody cites

September 23, 2026

60 capsules, 10 milligrams, around 20 euros — and on the label the words “longevity vitamin”. Behind it lies unusually elegant biology: a transporter that the body has apparently built specifically for this mushroom compound.

What it is about

Ergothioneine is a derivative of the amino acid histidine that neither plants nor animals can produce — only fungi and some soil bacteria. The episode explores why a substance that we take in exclusively with food has been given its own door into the cell, and what the data behind it actually show. It puts into context the large observational studies that link high blood levels with less heart disease and less dementia, and sets beside them the only larger intervention study, which missed its primary endpoint. It ends with a sober comparison with the vegetable aisle that shifts the question: capsule or frying pan.

Key points

  • The transporter described in 2005 carries ergothioneine more than 100 times more efficiently than anything else that passes through it — cells with this door accumulate the substance and retain it.
  • In the Malmö cohort, 112 metabolites were measured in 3,236 people without heart disease, who were followed up for 21 years; of all of them, ergothioneine was most strongly associated with lower mortality.
  • There, each standard deviation higher in the blood went along with 21 percent less cardiovascular death and 14 percent lower all-cause mortality.
  • A Japanese cohort of 1,300 people over 65 found 44 percent less dementia over 11 years in the top quarter of blood levels — co-funded by a beverage company.
  • The largest intervention study had 147 participants aged 55 to 79, ran for 16 weeks and missed its primary endpoint, a composite memory score.
  • The EU authorized ergothioneine as a novel food in 2017, with a maximum amount of 30 milligrams a day for adults.
  • Porcini mushrooms contain more than 7 milligrams per gram of dry weight, oyster mushrooms up to 4, button mushrooms around 0.4 — 100 grams of fresh button mushrooms come to roughly 3 milligrams.

Where the data stop

Everything that speaks in favor of ergothioneine so far comes from observation. The authors of the Malmö study name the obvious explanation themselves: people who eat a lot of mushrooms and vegetables automatically have more of it in their blood — in which case the substance would be the messenger and not the message. The studies in which it was given rather than measured are small: 45 healthy people over 7 days, 19 older people over one year. And the one large study missed its primary endpoint.

The mechanism, too, is more open than the label suggests. Barry Halliwell, who has been researching the substance for years, wrote in 2023 that the evidence for a primary antioxidant function in the body is weak. The longest human study lasts one year; for high doses over decades there is nothing, and for a substance that stays in tissue for weeks to months, that is not a footnote. Authorization as a novel food, moreover, assesses tolerability, not efficacy — pregnant and breastfeeding women are excluded because authorization was never applied for them.
