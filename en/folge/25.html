Selank: Relieving anxiety without benzo side effects? The Russian peptide fact-checked

July 5, 2026

Does Selank work like a benzodiazepine? In 3 Russian trials in anxiety patients it was similarly anxiety-relieving, without the typical drowsiness, and the strongest finding shows it as a companion to a benzo rather than a replacement. The episode goes through the trials and shows why the data are real but preliminary.

What it is about

Selank consists of 7 amino acids. The front part is a copy of tuftsin, a tiny piece of an antibody, and at the back a small attachment protects it from rapid breakdown. It was developed in Moscow, in the same research environment as Semax: Semax is meant to make you alert, Selank to calm you. In Russia it is approved as non-prescription nasal drops; via the nasal mucosa it reaches the brain almost directly, bypassing the stomach and liver. The episode starts with the question of whether Selank works like a benzo, explains the 2 pathways via GABA and BDNF, and goes through user reports and the 3 clinical trials. At the end stands an answer that is neither yes nor no, and a peptide with real but preliminary patient data.

Key points

  • On rat brain membranes, Selank enhanced the binding of GABA as a positive allosteric modulator: it acts next to the main site and amplifies what is already there. The slower pathway runs via BDNF; in a 2019 rat model, Selank prevented alcohol-induced memory impairment, and there are also indications for serotonin and enkephalins.
  • Zozulia 2008: 62 patients with generalized anxiety disorder or neurasthenia, 30 on Selank, 32 on the benzodiazepine medazepam. The anxiety-relieving effect was comparable, Selank additionally worked against exhaustion, and the overly fast breakdown of enkephalins in the blood returned to normal.
  • Medvedev 2014: 60 patients with anxiety disorders and physical complaints without organic findings, compared with phenazepam. Selank had a clear anxiety-relieving effect and was mildly nootropic, the effect lasted 1 week after the last use, and quality of life improved.
  • Medvedev 2015: 70 patients, 30 on phenazepam alone, 40 on phenazepam plus Selank. In the combination the effect set in earlier, and sedation, memory problems and emotional blunting decreased, even after the benzo was stopped.
  • This fits an animal finding: in rats under chronic, unpredictable stress, the combination of Selank and diazepam worked best against the increased anxiety.
  • As a report: users describe an effect within minutes, less inner tension and rumination, without the cotton-wool, slowed-down feeling of many sedatives, in short: calm but clear. Some report a slightly lifted mood; it is used mainly in phases when people want to stay able to work despite tension.
  • The BK-Score gives 4 each for human studies, mechanism and track record of use, 3 for the safety data and 2 for the hype gap; the label reads “Thin human evidence”.

Where the data stop

All 3 trials are small, 60 to 70 people, were published in Russian-language journals and compare against a benzodiazepine; none has a placebo arm. If both groups improve, it remains open whether both drugs work or whether the complaints would have improved on their own. 2 trials are indexed as randomized, for the third this is not stated, blinding is reported nowhere, and an independent replication outside Russia is missing. For healthy people under stress who see Selank as a gentle focus aid, there are no studies. Signs of dependence or withdrawal have not been described, but this has never been specifically studied in humans. The user reports come from a tightly networked circle, and expectation cannot be factored out.

Most often mentioned are mild nasal irritation and occasionally fatigue or headache; there are no long-term data in healthy long-term users. Because Selank contributes to the binding of diazepam in the lab and enhanced its effect in rats, any combination with sedatives belongs in the hands of a physician. In Germany and the EU, Selank is not approved; what is offered is research material whose purity and content nobody checks pharmaceutically. Persistent anxiety belongs in the hands of a physician or psychotherapist.
