Podcast episode 152 (in German)

Hexarelin – the strongest growth hormone peptide, and what remains after weeks

November 29, 2026

In humans, hexarelin releases more growth hormone than the body’s own regulating hormone; that has been measured cleanly. More interesting is what is left of it after weeks — and why it acts on the heart even without any growth hormone at all.

What it is about

In the late 1970s, a painkiller made by the body itself was chemically modified, and the result was a family of peptides that release growth hormone. The matching docking site was found only in 1996, and the body’s own counterpart, ghrelin, in 1999 in the stomach. Hexarelin, 6 building blocks long, is the most potent member of this family. The episode goes through what was measured in humans, mainly in the 1990s: the potency, the accompanying rise in cortisol, the rapid loss of effect and an effect on the heart that runs via a second binding partner. Added to this are analyses of forums in which users talk mainly about recovery after injuries, not about records; these are reports, not evidence of efficacy. The conclusion: the potency is real, but after 16 weeks nothing measurable had changed in the body.

Key points

  • In 12 healthy young volunteers in Turin, hexarelin released about twice as much growth hormone as the body’s own regulating hormone, and a second run produced almost the same curve.
  • In 12 healthy older people in Manchester, a good half of the hormone response was still there after 16 weeks; most of the loss had already occurred after 1 week, and 4 weeks after stopping it had disappeared.
  • Little happened in the body during these 16 weeks: the growth factor IGF-1, fat, lean mass and bone density remained unchanged; only a single bone marker rose.
  • Acutely, cortisol and ACTH rise as well, in 6 young adults about as much as after the starting signal of the stress axis; over 16 weeks, however, cortisol in 24-hour urine and prolactin remained unchanged.
  • During bypass surgery in 24 men, only hexarelin improved pumping function, from 10 minutes and for up to 1.5 hours; growth hormone, the regulating hormone and a sham preparation did nothing.
  • In 7 young men in Munich, hexarelin given at night reduced deep sleep in the first half of the night, whereas the body’s own ghrelin tends to promote deep sleep.
  • In a British forum analysis on peptides in general, 493 threads with just under 15,000 posts, the writers are mainly men between 40 and 60 who want to keep training after injuries; some report noticing nothing at all.

Where the data stop

Almost all human data are around 30 years old, the groups comprise 6, 7 or 12 people, and no study asked whether anyone was better off after weeks. The heart findings are real but come from single doses under anesthesia, measured over minutes; in an enlarged heart chamber nothing happened, and everything longer-term comes from animal studies. The faster growth of 7 children in Tel Aviv, from 5.3 to 7.4 centimeters per year, is a hint without a comparison group. Not a single study is registered in the American trial registry.

Hexarelin is not approved in Germany, there is no purity standard, and in sport it is prohibited at all times according to the 2026 Prohibited List. Forum reports are not representative, and those who notice nothing write less often; in 239 analyzed social media posts on peptides, only 1 in 8 mentioned a side effect, and almost 1 in 3 linked to a seller. Anyone who takes such substances and has blood drawn should tell their doctor: an elevated prolactin can come from the peptide.
