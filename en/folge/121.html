Podcast episode 121 (in German)

Dietary fiber – one hundred thirty-five million person-years for six grams

October 29, 2026

135 million person-years from 185 observational studies lead to an unspectacular recommendation: 6 to 10 grams more dietary fiber per day. This episode shows why one of the best-supported numbers in nutrition research lies behind it — and where a powder cannot keep up.

What it is about

Dietary fiber is the part of plant foods that the small intestine does not break down; it arrives undigested in the large intestine, where bacteria live on it. The episode draws on a 2019 paper in the Lancet, calculated on behalf of the World Health Organization, that pools 185 prospective observational studies and 58 randomized trials. People who eat the most fiber die less often and fall ill less often with 4 major diseases; the greatest reduction lies at 25 to 29 grams per day. Surprisingly, the much-discussed glycemic index comes off clearly weaker in the same table. After that, the episode turns to the gap in everyday life, to the question of whether a powder does the same as a plate of food, and to the initial complaints when switching.

Key points

  • High compared with low intake was associated with 15 to 30 percent lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, and with 16 to 24 percent fewer cases of coronary heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer.
  • Expressed in people, that is 13 fewer deaths and 6 fewer cases of coronary heart disease per 1,000.
  • For every 8 grams more per day, deaths and new cases fell by 5 to 27 percent, depending on the endpoint; beyond 30 grams, data are lacking.
  • The glycemic index has been talked about for 30 years, yet it shows smaller or no risk reduction at low to very low certainty of evidence — dietary fiber stands at moderate.
  • The 58 randomized trials with 4,635 adults show 0.37 kilograms less body weight, 1.27 millimeters of mercury lower systolic blood pressure and 0.15 millimoles per liter lower total cholesterol.
  • According to the German National Nutrition Survey, women eat 18 grams per day and men 19; the German Nutrition Society recommends at least 30, and the greatest effect lies at 25 to 29.
  • In its 2023 guideline, the World Health Organization strongly recommends at least 25 grams per day, explicitly from fiber naturally occurring in foods.

Where the data stop

All figures on mortality and disease come from observation. People who eat a lot of fiber smoke less, exercise more and eat less meat; the studies adjust for this as well as they can, but it never fully succeeds. The randomized trials did not follow anyone up to a heart attack: the 15 to 30 percent apply to people who have eaten this way for decades, the 0.37 kilograms to a few weeks with a few grams more. Moderate does not mean shaky here, but that later studies may still shift the result.

The second limit concerns the shop shelf. What was measured is what people ate, and an apple contains not only fiber but also potassium, polyphenols, water, volume and chewing time. Nobody knows which part had the effect. Individual fibers have their own studies on cholesterol and blood sugar, with different endpoints; they do not inherit the big numbers. Increasing intake abruptly often brings a feeling of fullness and bloating, which subside as the gut flora adapts — drinking enough is part of it.
