Air quality – the world’s second-largest risk factor and the box in the bedroom

November 7, 2026

According to the latest analysis, particulate matter is the world’s second-largest risk factor, just behind blood pressure. Unlike blood pressure, there is a device with a plug against it — the episode clarifies what it can do and why the window still comes first.

What it is about

According to the Global Burden of Disease analysis, published in the Lancet in October 2025, 8.2 percent of all healthy life years lost are attributable to particulate matter, 8.4 to blood pressure. This refers to particles smaller than 2.5 micrometers that reach the alveoli and, the smallest ones, the blood; particulate matter is above all a matter of the blood vessels. More is generated indoors than you might think, above all at the stove. The episode goes through the filter studies, almost all of which measure blood pressure, and through a survey of people who had a device in their bedroom: almost all let it run around the clock, some appreciated the noise against the sound from outside and the cool, moving air. They did not feel the dust itself. At the end stands an order of priority: first the extractor hood and the window, then the filter, and the filter only if it is running.

Key points

  • Among almost 61 million Medicare beneficiaries over 13 years, mortality rose by 7.3 percent for every 10 micrograms more particulate matter per cubic meter, and below 12 micrograms even by 13.6 percent.
  • In 2025, every German monitoring station complied with the EU limit of 25 micrograms; 98 percent of the almost 300 stations missed the WHO guideline value of 5 micrograms, lowered in 2021.
  • When searing food in an apartment in Seattle, particulate matter in the kitchen and living room reached peaks of 200 to 1,400 micrograms, and depending on ventilation it took 1 to 10 hours to clear.
  • In Shanghai, 48 hours of filtration in 35 students, double-blind against a dummy, reduced particulate matter by 57 percent and the upper blood pressure value by almost 3 percent.
  • Pooled, 10 studies with 604 people yielded an average of almost minus 4 mmHg, a second review of 14 studies minus 2.3 mmHg, with explicitly very low certainty.
  • In 33 older people, particulate matter in the bedroom fell from 8.3 to 1.3 micrograms over 4 weeks; the upper blood pressure value remained practically the same at 127 versus 126.5.
  • Next to a highway near Boston, 1 month of filtration in 154 people showed an effect of 3 mmHg only in those with already elevated blood pressure, and none with normal blood pressure.

Where the data stop

Particulate matter is as well established as a risk factor as few other things, with no recognizable lower threshold. The same does not apply to the solution to the same degree: the filter studies are small and short and measure blood pressure; there is no randomized trial showing that an air purifier prevents heart attacks or deaths. That it helps most with elevated blood pressure is how the data read; it is not proven. The first link in the chain, less dust in the room, is on the other hand very well established.

The 150 respondents from the highway study were followed as study participants and do not speak for all buyers. After 1 month, about one in ten felt disturbed by the noise, after 3 months half as many. A device only works as long as it is running, so it is better to choose one size larger so that the medium setting is enough, and to place it close to the stove when frying. Devices that deliberately produce ozone do not belong here. Anyone with asthma or a lung or heart disease should discuss protective measures with a doctor.
