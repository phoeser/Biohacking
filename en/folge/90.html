Podcast episode 90 (in German)

SS-31 (elamipretide) – the first approval that proves the opposite

September 28, 2026

A young man who could not manage 400 meters in one go as a child has been injecting an investigational drug since 2017 – and his father asks what has happened to his son. The episode tells how a peptide from pain research became SS-31, the first approved therapy for a mitochondrial disease.

What it is about

SS-31 is a very short peptide that migrates to the inner mitochondrial membrane and binds there to the lipid cardiolipin. Cardiolipin holds together the tight folding of this membrane, on whose surface energy is generated; it breaks down with age and in certain diseases. Unlike most substances in this series, there is a named target structure here and a phase 3 trial in humans. The episode goes through the discovery by Hazel Szeto and Peter Schiller, the studies in mitochondrial myopathy including the subgroup by genotype, the approval for Barth syndrome that families campaigned for, and the retinal data in macular degeneration. And it sorts out what remains of all this for someone who is healthy and wants to prevent something.

Key points

  • SS-31 comes from a family of opioid peptides that Hazel Szeto and Peter Schiller studied in the early 2000s; it accumulates about a thousandfold in the inner mitochondrial membrane and binds to cardiolipin there. Szeto describes these compounds as a repair where energy production is disturbed, without changing the healthy organism.
  • In the first study in mitochondrial myopathy with 36 participants, people at the highest dose walked 64 meters farther, versus 20 on placebo, in a dose-dependent manner; in a second small study, fatigue improved on several questionnaires.
  • The phase 3 trial MMPOWER-3 with 218 participants missed both primary endpoints, walking distance and fatigue. In patients with defects in nuclear DNA, however, people on SS-31 walked 25 meters farther on average, versus practically none on placebo; the authors call this encouraging, and the follow-up study NuPower with 102 participants has been completed but not yet published.
  • In Barth syndrome, the defect lies in a gene for remodeling cardiolipin. The study with 12 participants missed its goals in the blinded part; in the open-label extension, the 8 who stayed on walked almost 100 meters farther after just over eight months. An FDA advisory committee voted 10 to 6 in favor, the FDA initially said no in May 2025 and granted accelerated approval to elamipretide in September 2025.
  • The most common side effect is injection-site reactions; in MMPOWER-3, just over 7 percent stopped because of side effects, versus just under 2 on placebo. Reports from the biohacking scene also name the irritated injection site as the most common complaint.
  • In dry macular degeneration, a study with 176 patients missed its primary endpoints, but the mitochondria-rich retinal layer declined about 40 percent more slowly, and almost 15 percent could read two more lines in dim light, versus 2 percent on placebo. The FDA has accepted this retinal finding as the primary endpoint of the phase 3 trial; results are expected at the end of 2027.
  • In the BK score, SS-31 stands at human evidence 5, mechanism 7, safety 6, application 3 and hype gap 2, because online marketing runs far ahead of the data.

Where the data stop

In healthy people there is only one small study with a single dose: in 39 older adults with rather weak mitochondrial function, energy capacity in the muscle rose measurably, while fatigability did not change. A four-week study with 30 healthy older adults started at the end of 2025; no results are available yet. Everything else comes from disease settings, and Szeto’s own sentence applies here too: repairing something that is disturbed is different from topping up something that is sufficient.

The approval covers Barth syndrome and patients weighing 30 kilograms or more, nothing else. The safety data come from studies with a preparation manufactured under regulatory requirements and given under medical supervision. They do not apply to a powder or vial from an unclear source: the safety of a substance and the safety of a product are two different things.
