Podcast episode 31 (in German)

NAD+ and NMN – the longevity star fact-checked

August 5, 2026

NAD+ drives energy, DNA repair and the sirtuins in every cell, and its level declines with age. Precursors such as NMN demonstrably raise it again. Whether this lets humans live longer or healthier is the open question of this episode.

What it is about

NAD+ is a coenzyme that accepts and donates electrons and thus drives metabolism in the mitochondria; at the same time, the cell needs it for DNA repair and for the sirtuins, its maintenance crews. Because NAD+ itself barely gets cleanly into the cell as a capsule, the scene relies on precursors: NMN, NR and, in the end, plain vitamin B3. The episode explains where the hype about the rejuvenated mice from David Sinclair’s circle comes from, what the human studies show and what they do not, and which safety questions are open. What remains is a plausible, well-researched molecule without proof of rejuvenation in humans, and two free ways to raise NAD+ as well.

Key points

  • The NAD+ level declines with age; some data suggest that at 50 only about half of what was there at 20 remains.
  • NMN is only a single chemical step away from NAD+; both NMN and NR measurably raise NAD+ levels in the blood, and that is undisputed. A clear superiority of either one has not been proven.
  • In mouse studies from the circle of Harvard researcher David Sinclair, old animals given NMN showed more endurance, better blood vessels and better insulin sensitivity.
  • In humans, some small studies suggest improvements, others see almost nothing; so far there is no evidence of more strength, fitness, less disease or a longer life.
  • In the short term, NMN and NR are considered well tolerated in studies; reliable safety data over 10 years do not exist.
  • NAD+ infusions often run for 1 to 2 hours and are expensive; the evidence that they slow biological ageing is thin, and much of it rests on personal accounts.
  • Exercise, especially intense exercise, as well as fasting and calorie restriction are considered the best-supported ways to boost the body’s own NAD+ production, without any supplement at all.

Where the data stop

The mechanism, the age-related decline and the fact that NAD+ can be raised are well supported. What is missing is the step from mouse to human, at which many hopefuls in longevity research have already failed: whether a higher level in humans leads to a longer, healthier life is open. A theoretical question is also being discussed: NAD+ is also fuel for cell growth, possibly also for cancer cells. There is no evidence for this, but with a known cancer or a high risk, the topic belongs in a conversation with a physician.

In Germany, NMN is a legal gray area, as of August 2026. The episode’s order of priorities is clear: sleep, exercise and diet come before any precursor, and anyone who wants more approaches it with realistic expectations.
