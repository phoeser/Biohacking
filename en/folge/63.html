Podcast episode 63 (in German)

Coffee & caffeine: the everyday booster fact-checked

September 2, 2026

The world’s most widely used psychoactive molecule is in almost every kitchen. This episode asks whether coffee is just a forgivable everyday vice or actually a longevity tool — and the answer depends surprisingly strongly on the when.

What it is about

The episode starts with the mechanism: caffeine does not wake you up, it occupies the receptors of the sleep-pressure molecule adenosine and thereby sticks a note over the tiredness display. From this follow the timing rules that carry the rest of the episode. Then come the long-term data from the large cohorts, the mechanisms behind them — chlorogenic acids as the largest polyphenol source of the Western diet and the activation of the energy sensor AMPK — and the individual side via the liver enzyme CYP1A2. Finally, it covers preparation, sport, the well-known L-theanine combination and the groups for whom coffee is the wrong drink. At the end stands a formula: early, black, filtered, with a curfew.

Key points

  • Around 90 percent of adults take caffeine daily — it is the most consumed active substance in the world and blocks the adenosine receptors instead of supplying energy.
  • The half-life is 5 to 6 hours: the espresso at 4 p.m. is still half in the blood at 10 p.m., and even 6 hours before bedtime caffeine measurably shortens sleep.
  • The large cohorts show a U-curve with the optimum at 2 to 4 cups daily and around 15 to 30 percent lower all-cause mortality compared with non-drinkers.
  • An analysis of the Harvard Nurses’ Health Study over 30 years found that each daily cup increased the chance of reaching old age without chronic disease by about 5 percent.
  • The European authority classifies up to 400 milligrams daily as safe for healthy adults; for pregnant women a limit of 200 milligrams applies.
  • The ballpark figures for adding up: mug of filter coffee 90 to 120 milligrams, single espresso only 60 to 80, black tea about 50, can of energy drink 80, cola 35.
  • Regular coffee drinkers have about a 30 percent lower risk of Parkinson’s in the cohorts, dose-dependent and replicated on several continents; in sport, 3 to 6 milligrams per kilogram are the best-supported legal aid.

Where the data stop

The longevity data are observational data. The dose-response curve, consistency across cultures and plausible mechanisms make them one of the most robust nutritional associations of all, but they are not proof of causality. And the benefits are tied to conditions: unfiltered coffee contains cafestol, which measurably raises LDL cholesterol, and in the Tufts analysis the mortality benefit disappeared as soon as significant amounts of sugar and cream were added.

Individually, the range is wide. The variants of the liver enzyme CYP1A2 divide people into fast and slow metabolizers, and in the slow ones individual studies even point to more cardiac risk with high consumption. The 90-minute wait after getting up is thinly supported. With untreated high blood pressure, arrhythmias, anxiety disorders, reflux and in pregnancy, the topic belongs in medical consultation.
