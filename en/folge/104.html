Long-term alkaline bathing – thirty hours in the water, and what really happens

October 12, 2026

Around 30 hours in the bathtub over 3 days, and afterwards the claim is: that went deep into the body and the organs. The sentence is even true — only the route runs through the kidneys and circulation instead of the skin, and the skin pays for the duration.

What it is about

This time it is not about half an hour on a Sunday, but about baths lasting many hours; one guide recommends working up from one hour to as many as seven. The usual justification, that acid leaves the body through the skin, does not hold; that job is done by the kidneys. What does hold is immersion itself: water pressure shifts blood from the legs and abdomen into the chest, stretch sensors in the atrium of the heart report too much volume, and the body excretes water and sodium. The episode tells this through the space clinic in Toulouse, physiology experiments, spa studies on back pain and the experiences of bathers, from soft skin to thirst and fatigue.

Key points

  • The kidneys excrete around 71 millimoles of acid per day, measured in a metabolic study in 18 postmenopausal women — the skin plays no significant role in this balance.
  • 13 healthy men passed 758 milliliters of urine and 56 millimoles of sodium in 3 hours of immersion, a good 3 grams of table salt.
  • After 5 hours in water, urine flow and sodium excretion at 32 and 34.5 degrees were six times higher, at 36 degrees only three times — warmer is not more.
  • In the space clinic in Toulouse, 18 women lay in dry immersion at 32 to 34 degrees for five days: by the first evening about 12 percent of their blood plasma was gone, after five days they weighed 1.5 to 2 kilos less, and blood salts stayed in the normal range.
  • The first night was the hardest: discomfort and sleep got worse, 13 of 18 women developed back pain, and this eased from day to day.
  • After 4 hours in water the skin's horny layer was three times as thick, after 24 hours four times; small pools of water formed between the horny cells.
  • A 2006 meta-analysis found, in 5 randomized studies on spa therapy for low back pain, an advantage of around 27 points on a 100-point pain scale compared with waiting lists; these were repeated baths over weeks.
  • In people with heart failure, a small Danish study found parts of the circulatory reflexes weakened after just half an hour in the water — anyone with a weak heart, low blood pressure or diuretic tablets should seek medical advice before baths lasting hours.
  • The BK-Score separates rationale and effect: acid through the skin does not hold, circulation and kidneys are well measured — human evidence 6.

Where the data stop

The longest experiments in body-warm, wet water end after 5 hours. Toulouse lasted five days, but dry and without alkaline salts; nobody has yet measured ten or twelve hours in warm alkaline water. The back pain studies, too, test spa protocols with repeated baths over weeks, not one long day. That acid leaves the body through the skin in meaningful amounts has never been measured; bases work by mouth, and the bath drives exactly the organ that disposes of acid anyway.

A long bath is therefore a real intervention in fluid balance. In warm water at 36 degrees, the heart's output was about 80 percent higher; young healthy people cope well with that, but caution is advised with heart failure. And skin that has swollen needs time to settle again after very long baths.
