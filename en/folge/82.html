Amycretin – 24.3% and the price that sits right next to it

September 20, 2026

In 1987, researchers in Oxford found a previously unknown hormone in the deposits of a diseased pancreas: amylin. It has since become the basis of one of the most interesting weight-loss candidates – amycretin, now called zenagamtide, with a 24.3 percent weight loss in its first study in people with overweight.

What it is about

Amycretin is a drug that targets two satiety signals in a single molecule: the receptor for GLP-1 from the gut and the one for amylin from the pancreas. Two channels into the brain instead of two substances in one injection — pharmacologically a feat, and simpler to manufacture and dose than a combination. The episode tells how amylin was discovered as part of stubborn deposits and turned into a drug via pramlintide, and puts the studies into context: the early data on the injection and the tablet, the side effects and the diabetes trials published in full in the Lancet since July 2026. Plus the context that matters most: the substance is an investigational drug, and the approval trials are under way.

Key points

  • Amylin was discovered in 1987 in amyloid-rich pancreatic tissue. It is released together with insulin and signals the end of a meal to the brainstem; GLP-1 from the gut tends to dampen appetite earlier.
  • Amylin is not a new idea: pramlintide has been approved in the US since 2005, but had to be injected with every meal and never really caught on. In a study of 266 people with type 1 diabetes, participants on pramlintide were nevertheless more satisfied than on placebo and lost 1.5 kilos, while the placebo group gained weight.
  • The early injection study published in the Lancet in 2025 included 125 people with overweight, several dose levels and placebo, over up to 36 weeks; its main aim was safety. At the highest level, weight fell by 24.3 percent on average, versus 1.1 percent on placebo.
  • The tablet form reached up to 13.1 percent after 12 weeks in the first study in humans, with 144 participants.
  • Almost all participants in the injection study reported side effects, mainly nausea and vomiting, mostly mild to moderate. A third left the study early, more than half of them for reasons unrelated to the drug; one case of pancreatitis caused by gallstones is also documented.
  • The two phase 2 trials in type 2 diabetes (262 participants on the injection, 186 on the tablet) have been published in full in the Lancet since July 2026: long-term blood sugar (HbA1c) fell by up to 1.7 percentage points with the injection. For weight, Novo Nordisk reports up to 14.6 percent with the injection versus 2.1 on placebo, and around 10 versus 2.5 percent with the tablet.
  • Since September 2026, the injection has been compared head-to-head with semaglutide in the phase 3 trial AMAZE 7. In the BK score, amycretin stands at human evidence 5 and mechanism 8 – the large trials are only just beginning.

Where the data stop

Everything available on amycretin is early. The weight figure comes from a study with 125 people and a duration of 36 weeks; for the approved drugs in this class, the data now span years and many thousands of people. A head-to-head comparison has only just started with AMAZE 7; until then it is impossible to say whether combining two satiety signals ultimately turns out to be better tolerated than GLP-1 alone. A network meta-analysis puts amycretin ahead, but such indirect comparisons are shaky, as the authors themselves note. As for the whole class, open questions remain about how much of the lost weight is muscle, what happens after stopping and the benefit over years. The phase 3 trials have been running since August and September 2026; approval is more likely towards the end of the decade.

The decisive point here is not a data point. Amycretin is an investigational drug, not approved and not legally available anywhere. Anyone looking for help with weight loss today should discuss the approved options with a doctor; investigational substances belong in trials whose registries are publicly accessible. Anything sold online under this name cannot be the original – only Novo Nordisk makes it.
