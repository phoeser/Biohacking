MOTS-c: the mitochondrial peptide & exercise mimetic fact-checked

July 12, 2026

Mitochondria do not only make energy, they also send signals. MOTS-c is one of them: a peptide of 16 building blocks, encoded in the cellular power plants’ own genome, which the longevity scene celebrates as “exercise in molecular form”.

What it is about

MOTS-c was described in 2015 by the group around Changhan David Lee and Pinchas Cohen at the University of Southern California and is considered a mitokine, a hormone-like signal directly from the mitochondrion. It activates AMPK, the cell’s energy sensor, which is otherwise switched on by exercise and fasting, and migrates into the cell nucleus under stress. The episode explains what follows from this in studies for insulin sensitivity, metabolic flexibility and performance, why the level falls with age and how MOTS-c, alongside humanin, belongs to a whole family of mitochondrial peptides. At the end stands an endogenous signal with elegant biology, whose strongest data come from cell and animal experiments.

Key points

  • MOTS-c is a peptide of only 16 building blocks and is not encoded in the cell nucleus, but in the tiny genome of the mitochondria themselves.
  • The discovery paper appeared in Cell Metabolism in 2015: according to it, MOTS-c regulates metabolism and insulin sensitivity, mainly via AMPK, the same switch that exercise and fasting press.
  • In a 2021 paper by Reynolds and colleagues in Nature Communications, mice given MOTS-c ran markedly farther, and old animals also regained performance.
  • In humans, the body’s own MOTS-c level rises measurably during exercise; it falls with age, and particularly healthy agers reportedly tend to have higher levels.
  • In animal models, MOTS-c slowed weight gain on a high-fat diet; under stress it migrates into the cell nucleus and switches on genes for stress defense and antioxidant protection there.
  • MOTS-c belongs to a family of mitochondrial peptides whose first known member was humanin; the partnership with the once independent bacteria goes back around 2 billion years.
  • As a report: users mention more stable energy throughout the day, better endurance in training and less of an afternoon slump; the side effect most likely mentioned is a reaction at the injection site.

Where the data stop

The most convincing data on MOTS-c come from cell and animal experiments. That the level rises with exercise and falls with age shows an association, but not yet a proven benefit of external supply; large long-term studies in humans are lacking, as of July 2026. The user reports fit the mechanism but are subjective and not study endpoints.

MOTS-c is a research peptide and not approved as a medicine, as of July 2026. Purity and origin of the goods are therefore up to the supplier. Anyone doing competitive sport should check its status with the World Anti-Doping Agency. And the route that measurably raises the body’s own MOTS-c is freely available anyway: exercise.
