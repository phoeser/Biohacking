Podcast episode 93 (in German)

Snap-8 – Botox from a tube?

October 1, 2026

Snap-8 is found in creams from every drugstore and is sold as “Botox from a tube”. The episode shows why the famous figure behind it can hardly be verified — and why the real warning lies somewhere else entirely.

What it is about

Snap-8 is a peptide of 8 amino acids, listed in the ingredients as acetyl octapeptide-3. It is modeled on a component of the SNARE complex, through which nerve cells release their messenger substance to the muscle. If this step is slowed, the muscle contracts less, and expression lines turn out flatter — the same principle that botulinum toxin acts on, just without an injection. The episode follows up the figure that appears everywhere: 63 percent wrinkle reduction in 28 days. It has no source in a peer-reviewed journal; it comes from the documents of the raw material manufacturer. Added to this is a problem that comes before any question of efficacy: the molecule first has to get through the stratum corneum. At the end there is a serious warning, but it is not about the cream.

Key points

  • Snap-8 consists of 8 amino acids and is modeled on a piece of the protein SNAP-25, which nerve cells need in order to release their messenger substance to the muscle.
  • The 63 percent wrinkle reduction in 28 days quoted everywhere comes from the raw material manufacturer’s product documents and cannot be found in any peer-reviewed journal.
  • A review notes that most studies on cosmetic peptides are produced by the raw material suppliers for advertising claims and that the methods are often not disclosed.
  • The most presentable study in this class of substances concerns its relative argireline: 30 percent less wrinkle depth versus 10 percent on placebo after 30 days — in 10 female participants.
  • How little gets through the skin without aids is shown by a figure from the literature: microneedles increase uptake 40-fold compared with simple application.
  • For the cream, the episode gives human evidence 3, safety 5, use 7 and hype 3 — the low evidence number stands for a lack of verifiability, not a lack of measurements.
  • There is not a single study on Snap-8 as a powder to dissolve and inject; the substance was tested and developed as a cosmetic raw material for application to the skin.

Where the data stop

The measurements on Snap-8 are not missing; they just cannot be verified. What is missing is a randomized study in a peer-reviewed journal showing an anti-aging effect for these peptides on the skin; for Snap-8 itself, the relevant review does not cite a single efficacy study. Then there is the stratum corneum: peptides are large, charged and water-loving, roughly the opposite of what passes easily through the skin. Part of the visible effect is probably simply due to the fact that well-moisturized skin shows fine lines less.

The leap under the skin is completely unsupported. For injection there are no data, no suitable purity standard and no study of what blocking nerve-muscle transmission at an unknown site does. The cream is weak not because there is too little in it, but because hardly anything gets through — getting more of it into the body is not a higher dose of the same thing.
