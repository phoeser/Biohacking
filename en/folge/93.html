Podcast episode 93 (in German)

Snap-8 – Botox from a tube?

October 1, 2026

Around the turn of the millennium, a research group in Elche, Spain, asked a bold question: can the world’s most famous nerve toxin be recreated, just without the toxin? The answer is found in drugstore creams today – and Snap-8 is a descendant of it.

What it is about

Snap-8 is a peptide of 8 amino acids, listed in the ingredients as acetyl octapeptide-3. It is modeled on a component of the SNARE complex, through which nerve cells release their messenger substance to the muscle. If this step is slowed, the muscle contracts less and expression lines become shallower — the same principle that botulinum toxin targets, only without an injection. The episode tells how its model Argireline was created in Elche, follows up the figure that appears everywhere – up to 63 percent wrinkle reduction in 28 days –, and shows where the most interesting data lie: in microneedle patches and in Argireline as a companion after Botox treatment. Plus user reports and a short note on why Snap-8 does not belong in a syringe.

Key points

  • Argireline, the model for Snap-8, was created at the university in Elche together with a cosmetics company near Barcelona and described in 2002: six amino acids, modeled on a piece of the protein SNAP-25; in a first cream test in 10 women, wrinkles became up to 30 percent shallower after 30 days. Snap-8 is the version extended by two amino acids.
  • According to manufacturer and retailer information, the widely quoted 63 percent wrinkle reduction in 28 days is a maximum value (“up to”), not an average; no average is publicly available, and the measurement has not appeared in a peer-reviewed journal.
  • A review notes that most studies on cosmetic peptides come from the raw material suppliers and that methods are often not disclosed. Peptides pass poorly through the stratum corneum; with microneedles, around 40 times as much of one of these peptides gets into the skin.
  • In 2024, a Korean team tested a dissolving microneedle patch with Snap-8 and a vitamin C derivative in 24 participants, compared with a patch made only of hyaluronic acid: after four weeks, the active side showed fewer wrinkles and more elastic skin, without side effects – how much is due to Snap-8 alone cannot be said.
  • There is more on Argireline: a randomized study in China with 60 participants, in which investigators rated treatment around the eyes a success in almost half, never on placebo; and a split-face study of 19 women with no difference between the sides.
  • As a companion to Botox: in a study at the US National Institutes of Health, the effect of Botox in eyelid spasm lasted 3.7 instead of 3 months on average with Argireline cream, not yet statistically certain, and considerably longer in a third. In an Israeli study from September 2026, the interval until the next injection rose from 13.5 to just over 22 weeks in 15 Botox users with peptide-containing skin care, without a control group.
  • In around 45 collected user reports, forehead lines and crow’s feet become softer, usually after four to eight weeks. In the BK score, Snap-8 stands at human evidence 3, safety data 5 and application experience 7, Argireline at 4 for evidence.

Where the data stop

Independently peer-reviewed studies on Snap-8 alone are hard to find; the data come from multi-ingredient formulations, from its relative Argireline or from manufacturer documents. Part of the visible effect is probably because well-hydrated skin shows fine lines less – a real effect, just not at the muscle. Whether peptide creams prolong the effect of Botox is a promising lead, but not yet established in a controlled study. A dermaroller at home is the untested home version of the microneedle idea, with a risk of infection.

Snap-8 was never developed or tested as a powder to dissolve and inject. Cosmetic purity is not injection purity, and for its relative Argireline a persistent mycobacterial infection after facial injections has been described. Questions about this belong in a dermatology practice.
