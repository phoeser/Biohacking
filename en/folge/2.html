BPC-157: the Body Protection Compound – healing peptide fact-checked

July 12, 2026

BPC-157 is one of the best-known peptides in the recovery scene: a fragment of a protective protein from gastric juice that makes tendons and ligaments heal faster in animal studies. It has been tested in humans, but the results were never fully published. The episode shows how far the animal data, user reports and the few human data reach.

What it is about

BPC stands for “Body Protection Compound”. The peptide consists of 15 amino acids, is derived from a protective protein the body makes in gastric juice and is considered exceptionally acid-stable. It became known because it is said to let the stomach lining heal, but it became famous for tendons, ligaments and muscles: tissue that is barely supplied with blood and therefore heals slowly. The episode starts with a registered study using a gummy bear, explains the mechanisms from animal models, tells the story of patient trials that were never published, goes through the experiences from a large forum thread and ends with the situation in the US and in Germany. At the end stands a peptide with strong animal data and a lot of user experience, whose first proper human trial is only now underway.

Key points

  • In animal models there are 2 pathways: via the FAK-paxillin signaling pathway, connective tissue and tendon cells migrate better to the injured site and form more type 1 collagen, the building material of tendons; via the VEGF receptor, eNOS and nitric oxide, new blood vessels sprout, and inflammation is dampened as well.
  • In a study on tendon cells, the growth hormone receptor was at the very top of the genes that BPC-157 switched up: more antennas for the body’s own signal, though in cell culture.
  • Under the code PL-14736, the Croatian manufacturer Pliva ran phase 2 trials in ulcerative colitis that are said to have shown positive effects without toxic effects; the full data were never published as a clinical paper of their own.
  • A systematic review screened 544 articles up to June 2024 and included 36, 35 of them animal or tissue studies; the only clinical paper is a case series on chronic knee pain in which 7 of 12 people reported relief over 6 months. A second review counts 3 small pilot studies in humans in total.
  • As a report: in a large Longecity thread running since 2017, the most common theme is faster recovery, for irritated elbows, wrists, old biceps injuries, heel spurs and after surgery, often within a few days; for disc degeneration and sciatica, long-time users write that it does nothing.
  • Since February 2026, a randomized phase 2 trial on hamstring strains has been recruiting, designed for 120 participants, with completion expected in February 2028; a small study on the rotator cuff is also registered.

Where the data stop

After more than 3 decades of research, almost entirely in rodents, there are no clinical safety data. The only phase 1 study in 42 healthy people started in 2015 and never posted results; the same goes for a registered study listed as completed that used a gummy bear, with 40 participants, 8 weeks, a single study arm and no placebo. The knee case series was analyzed retrospectively, without a control group and without blinding. Improvement overnight fits a fading of pain better than healed tissue, and a tennis elbow often resolves on its own. Online, users report pain and swelling at the injection site, joint pain, restlessness and palpitations, sleep problems, fatigue, loss of appetite and low mood; the fact that hardly any side effects are reported reflects a lack of data, not proof of safety.

BPC-157 is traded as research material without any drug quality control, and experienced users warn of counterfeits. In the US, the FDA placed it in category 2 in 2023 because of possible immune reactions and impurities and removed it again in April 2026; in July 2026 the FDA’s own experts recommended a no, but an expert panel still voted in favor by a majority, 8 to 6 with 1 abstention. Nothing has been decided yet, and a formal procedure is unlikely to begin before 2027. In Germany, BPC-157 is not an approved medicine, and WADA bans it in competitive sport. The BK-Score calls this “Tested in humans, results unpublished”, with 1 for the human data and 5 for track record of use. Anyone considering it should discuss it with a doctor.
