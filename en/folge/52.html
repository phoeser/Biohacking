Podcast episode 52 (in German)

HGH & longevity: growth hormone fact-checked

August 14, 2026

Growth hormone is almost completely understood and yet constantly overrated. The episode travels through 4 decades, from Rudman’s famous 1990 study to the sober balance sheet in healthy older adults: 2.1 kilos less fat, 2.1 kilos more fat-free mass, but no confirmed gain in strength. For people with a genuine deficiency it is a blessing.

What it is about

Until 1985, growth hormone was extracted from the pituitary glands of deceased people; since then somatropin has been made by genetic engineering, a protein of 191 building blocks identical to the body’s own. Much of its work is done by IGF-1 from the liver, which triggers build-up in muscle, bone and connective tissue. From about 30 onwards, secretion declines by roughly 14 percent per decade; researchers speak of the somatopause. The episode is a journey through time: from Rudman in 1990 via a double-blind trial from 2002 and a meta-analysis from 2007 to the TRIIM trial from 2019, plus the experiences of users and deficiency patients and the legal framework. At the end stands an honest balance sheet: a blessing in genuine deficiency, for healthy people a measurable remodeling without strength gain, with side effects and open long-term questions.

Key points

  • In 1990, Rudman gave men over 60 with low IGF-1 growth hormone for 6 months: almost 9 percent more lean mass, a good 14 percent less fat and thicker skin. He compared this to a reversal of 10 to 20 years of aging; the measurements were real.
  • In 2002, a double-blind trial in JAMA with 131 healthy people aged 65 to 88 over 26 weeks confirmed the direction: in men, fat-free mass rose by 3.1 kilos, versus 0.1 on placebo, and by 4.3 with added testosterone; in women by 1.0 versus 0.4 kilos, and by 2.1 with hormone replacement.
  • Liu’s 2007 meta-analysis with 220 treated healthy older adults, on average 69 years old: fat mass minus 2.1 kilos, fat-free mass plus 2.1 kilos, body weight practically unchanged, bone density and blood lipids essentially the same.
  • In genuine, medically diagnosed deficiency, the therapy demonstrably improves quality of life, bone density and blood lipids; for deficiency and defined conditions there are data from 4 decades.
  • In the 2019 TRIIM trial, men aged 51 to 65 received growth hormone with DHEA and metformin for one year; the thymus rebuilt itself, and after one year epigenetic age was on average about 1.5 years lower.
  • As a report: users describe better recovery, firmer skin, stronger hair and nails and often deeper sleep, things that do not show up in any fat measurement.
  • The BK-Score gives 8 for the human data, 9 for the mechanism, 8 for the safety data and 9 for track record of use, but only 3 for the hype gap: “Well supported, heavily overhyped”.

Where the data stop

Rudman’s study was small and short: 21 men, 12 treated, 9 as an untreated comparison, not randomized. In the 2002 trial, strength did not rise significantly, with one narrow exception in men on growth hormone plus testosterone, 13.5 kilos more at a p-value of exactly 0.05. In the meta-analysis, tissue swelling, joint pain, carpal tunnel syndrome and enlarged breast glands in men occurred clearly more often, and new diabetes and impaired fasting glucose tended to as well; the authors do not consider it advisable as an anti-aging therapy for healthy older adults. Users report similar things: water in the tissue, stiff fingers in the morning, tingling up to a carpal tunnel feeling, aching joints. Persistently very high IGF-1 is associated in observational data with somewhat more cases of some cancers, which is why balance in the middle range is considered the goal. TRIIM was small and had no placebo group; a follow-up study is underway.

The prescribing information of a somatropin product from 2024 lists growth disorders in children and replacement in proven deficiency in adults; anti-aging use is not listed there and is off-label. It warns of increased mortality in acutely critically ill patients, of second tumors and of decreasing insulin sensitivity, and somatropin is not intended for active cancer. In Germany it is prescription-only, health insurance pays only for diagnosed deficiency, and anyone who acquires it without a prescription above a certain amount commits an offense under the German Anti-Doping Act; in sport it is banned. In the US, prescribing it off-label against aging is explicitly prohibited by law. Gray-market products labeled “for research purposes only” undergo no checks for authenticity and purity. Whether something is lacking is shown by the lab: IGF-1 in the lower third for one’s age plus matching complaints is a reason to talk to an endocrinologist.
