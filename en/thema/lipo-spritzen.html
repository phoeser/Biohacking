Lipo injections (Lipo-B, Lipo-C, MIC)

Lipotropic injections · methionine, inositol, choline (MIC), vitamin B12, L-carnitine

11 min read · 10 sources, checked November 12, 2026

In the BK-Score: Hype far ahead of evidence – human evidence 2 out of 10, safety data 3 out of 10. The marketing promises much more than the thin data can carry so far.

“Lipo injections” promise to boost fat metabolism from the inside: methionine, inositol and choline, often with vitamin B12, and with L-carnitine added in Lipo-C. The idea is not plucked out of thin air, because each of these substances has a real job in fat metabolism, and vitamin B12 injections are standard medicine for a deficiency. However, we found no controlled trial with published results showing that the injection helps with weight loss.

What lipo injections are

Lipo injections are offered in aesthetic, wellness and weight-loss practices, usually as a series of several appointments. There is no standard formula. Three building blocks are common: MIC, a blend of the amino acid methionine, inositol and choline; plus vitamin B12, then often called “Lipo-B”; and in “Lipo-C”, additionally L-carnitine. Some blends contain further B vitamins.

The word “lipotropic” comes from nutrition research and describes substances that prevent fat from building up in the liver. Lipo injections share little more than the name with fat-dissolving injections: there, deoxycholic acid is injected directly into a fat deposit to destroy fat cells. Lipo injections, by contrast, are meant to stimulate fat metabolism throughout the body.

How it works

The plausible core lies in the liver. Choline is needed for the liver to release fat. How important this is became clear in people who were fed intravenously for years: without choline in the nutrient solution, many developed fatty liver, and with choline it receded. Methionine is a precursor of the methyl group donor SAMe, which the body uses, among other things, to build choline compounds. L-carnitine carries long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, where they are burned. Vitamin B12 is indispensable for blood formation and nerves.

That does not mean, however, that extra amounts break down fat in healthy people. The fatty liver in the choline studies was the result of a genuine deficiency. For L-carnitine, a study in healthy men showed that it only passes from the blood into muscle when insulin levels are high at the same time; at fasting insulin, the muscle content did not change. After intravenous administration, according to the US prescribing information, around 76 percent is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. We found no supported route by which the injection reduces body fat in people without a deficiency.

What is well supported

  • Vitamin B12 injections for deficiency. For a proven B12 deficiency, the injection is a standard treatment. A Cochrane review found three randomised trials with 153 participants in which high-dose oral B12 raised levels similarly, with low certainty of evidence.
  • Choline deficiency causes fatty liver, and choline fixes it. In a randomised trial with 15 patients on parenteral nutrition, the fat content of the liver improved with choline in the nutrient solution and liver enzymes fell; after stopping, the fatty liver returned.
  • L-carnitine as a dietary supplement has a small effect on weight. In a meta-analysis of 37 randomised trials with 2,292 participants, body weight fell by an average of 1.21 kg and fat mass by 2.08 kg, mainly in people with overweight. Waist circumference and body fat percentage did not change.
  • The single ingredients are known as medicines. Injectable L-carnitine is approved in the US to treat carnitine deficiency in inborn errors of metabolism and in dialysis patients, with documented side effects.

What the studies show

On the lipo injection itself: no published study

We searched Europe PMC and the trial registry ClinicalTrials.gov for studies on lipotropic, MIC, Lipo-B and Lipo-C injections and found no controlled trial with published results. The closest is a randomised, quadruple-blind pilot trial that set out in 2012 and 2013 to test in 22 people with obesity whether weekly B12 injections on top of the appetite suppressant phentermine lead to more weight loss than phentermine with saline injections. It is registered as completed; results have appeared neither in the registry nor in a journal.

Talenezhad 2020: L-carnitine and body weight

The meta-analysis (Clin Nutr ESPEN) pooled 37 randomised trials with 2,292 participants. L-carnitine lowered body weight by an average of 1.21 kg, BMI slightly and fat mass by 2.08 kg; there was no effect on waist circumference or body fat percentage. In the high-quality trials, only the effect on weight was confirmed. The authors describe a modest effect, most likely in adults with overweight. What was studied was L-carnitine as a dietary supplement, not as an injection in a lipo blend.

Buchman 2001: choline and fatty liver

15 patients who were fed mainly intravenously were randomly assigned to receive their usual nutrient solution or the same solution with choline for 24 weeks. From week 4, liver density on CT, as a measure of fat content, differed significantly in favour of the choline group, and the liver enzymes ALT and later AST fell. Ten weeks after choline was stopped, the fatty liver had returned. The study shows that choline is a required nutrient, not that extra choline breaks down fat in healthy people.

Stephens 2006: does injected L-carnitine reach the muscle?

Eight healthy men received intravenous L-carnitine for five hours on two occasions, once with fasting and once with artificially high insulin levels. Only together with high insulin did the carnitine content of muscle rise, from 22.0 to 24.7 mmol per kg dry mass; at fasting insulin it stayed the same. The authors note that earlier studies had not been able to raise muscle content either through diet or intravenously.

Wang 2018: swallowing or injecting B12

The Cochrane review found three randomised trials with 153 people with B12 deficiency, with treatment lasting three to four months. High-dose oral B12 raised blood levels similarly to the injection into the muscle, in one trial even more. The certainty of evidence was low because there were only a few small trials. None of the trials looked at weight.

Where the data stop

  • The injection itself. For Lipo-B, Lipo-C or MIC injections for weight loss we found no controlled trial with published results, and none on energy or metabolism either.
  • From deficiency to healthy people. Choline and B12 demonstrably help with a deficiency. It cannot be concluded from this that more of them breaks down fat when supply is adequate.
  • From capsule to injection. The small weight effect of L-carnitine comes from trials with dietary supplements. Whether individual injections in a blend achieve anything comparable has not been studied.
  • The blends. Composition and manufacture differ from practice to practice. There are no data on how the substances behave together in the blend or how much of them takes effect.

Status, approval and legal

We found no medicine with this composition approved for weight loss. Legally, each single ingredient counts: L-carnitine for parenteral use is prescription-only in Germany (Annex 1 of the Ordinance on Prescription-Only Medicines). Hydroxocobalamin is listed there only for the treatment of cyanide poisoning; cyanocobalamin, methionine, choline and inositol are not on the list.

Physicians and Heilpraktiker (state-licensed non-medical practitioners) do not need a manufacturing authorisation when they make a medicine under their direct professional responsibility for personal administration to a specific patient (§ 13 para. 2b German Medicines Act). For prescription-only medicines, this exemption applies only to physicians and dentists. A Lipo-C injection with L-carnitine therefore requires a medical prescription or a physician who makes it themselves.

For competitive athletes: methionine, inositol, choline, vitamin B12 and L-carnitine are not on the WADA Prohibited List. Under M2.2, however, intravenous infusions or injections of more than 100 millilitres per 12 hours are prohibited, except for those received in the course of hospital treatment, surgical procedures or clinical diagnostic investigations.

Safety

The single ingredients are known as medicines, and their side effects are documented. For intravenous L-carnitine, the US prescribing information lists transient nausea and vomiting, body odour and gastric complaints, as well as serious hypersensitivity reactions up to anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema and bronchospasm, mostly in dialysis patients and in some cases minutes after administration. Seizures have been described in people with and without a prior history, and in epilepsy also more frequent or more severe seizures.

For the blends there are neither studies nor a registry. With any injection or infusion, what matters is how cleanly the preparation is made and given: a case report from 2021 describes a 52-year-old woman without prior illness who developed a nearly fatal bloodstream infection with the rare germ Pseudomonas fluorescens after a naturopathic vitamin infusion at home; more on infusions is on the page on IV vitamin therapy. Whether a B12 deficiency exists at all is shown by a blood test. Injections belong in the hands of a physician, with a preparation of traceable origin.

Frequently asked questions about lipo injections

What are lipo injections?

Injections with so-called lipotropic substances: usually methionine, inositol and choline (MIC), often with vitamin B12 as “Lipo-B”, and with L-carnitine added as “Lipo-C”. They are meant to stimulate fat metabolism throughout the body. There is no standard composition.

Do lipo injections help with weight loss?

We found no controlled trial with published results on this. A small pilot trial of B12 injections on top of an appetite suppressant was completed in 2013, but its results never appeared. The only supported finding is a small effect of L-carnitine as a dietary supplement, an average of 1.21 kg across 37 trials.

How do they differ from fat-dissolving injections?

Fat-dissolving injections such as deoxycholic acid are injected directly into a fat deposit and destroy fat cells there; for the double chin this is supported by large trials. Lipo injections, by contrast, are meant to stimulate metabolism as a whole and have not been studied for weight loss.

Does a vitamin B12 shot do anything?

For a proven deficiency, yes, it is a standard treatment for that; oral B12 worked similarly in three small comparison trials. That B12 injections help with weight loss in people without a deficiency has not been shown.

Are naturopaths (Heilpraktiker) allowed to give lipo injections?

Heilpraktiker in Germany may manufacture and administer medicines themselves for a specific patient, but not prescription-only ones. Injectable L-carnitine is prescription-only in Germany; vitamin B12 as cyanocobalamin, methionine, choline and inositol are not on the list.
