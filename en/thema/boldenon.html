Boldenone (Equipoise)

Anabolic androgenic steroid, testosterone derivative; approved only as a veterinary drug (US, horses), not approved for humans · boldenone undecylenate, Boldenon-Undecylenat, Equipoise, EQ, androsta-1,4-dien-17β-ol-3-one

Boldenone is a derivative of testosterone and, as boldenone undecylenate, brand name Equipoise, a veterinary drug in the US for debilitated horses. It is not approved for humans. Nevertheless it is one of the widely used steroids in strength sports – without a single study on its effect in humans.

In short

What holds up: boldenone is an androgen, its metabolism in the human body has been studied, and in veterinary medicine it has a clear, narrow approval – for debilitated horses, not for horses intended for food production. Where the data stop: whether and how strongly it builds muscle, stimulates blood formation or increases performance in humans has not been studied by anyone. The harm side is known from case reports, such as severe heart failure in a 39-year-old strength athlete on testosterone and boldenone, and from the class risks of anabolic steroids. What is in circulation is veterinary drug or underground product; in Zurich drug checking, a good half of all steroid samples tested were counterfeit. In Germany, boldenone is listed in the Anti-Doping Act; in sport it is prohibited at all times.

What boldenone is

Boldenone is an anabolic androgenic steroid, chemically a testosterone with an additional double bond in the first ring of the steroid skeleton. It is sold as an ester, boldenone undecylenate, for injection; the best-known name is Equipoise, in the scene EQ for short.

It is approved only in veterinary medicine: in the US as a prescription drug for debilitated horses, when weight, coat or general condition are to improve. It is not approved for humans; anyone who uses it anyway is using a veterinary drug or an underground product. The page Anabolic steroids gives an overview of the whole group of substances.

How it works

Boldenone binds to the androgen receptor. This results in anabolic and masculinizing effects and, as with any supplied androgen, feedback on the hormonal axis: the body’s own testosterone production is shut down.

How strong these effects are in humans has not been measured. What is said about boldenone comes from veterinary medicine and from analogy with other anabolic steroids. In humans, only how the body breaks down and excretes the substance has been studied.

What is well supported

  • Veterinary approval. In the US, boldenone undecylenate is approved for debilitated horses, only on veterinary prescription and not for horses intended for food production.
  • Metabolism in humans. An excretion study identified boldenone and nine metabolites in urine; more than 95 % of the metabolites are excreted as stable conjugates.
  • Endogenous traces. Boldenone can also form in small amounts in the body itself; doping laboratories distinguish this via the carbon isotope ratio.

What the studies show

Schänzer and Donike 1992: metabolism in the human body

The research group gave boldenone to volunteers and examined their urine. It identified boldenone itself and nine metabolites; more than 95 % of the metabolites are excreted as stable conjugates. Effects on muscles, strength or blood values were not the subject of the study.

Piper 2010: endogenous or supplied?

Boldenone is not approved for humans but turns up in doping samples. The research group developed a method that determines the origin via the carbon isotope ratio. Of 23 samples examined, 11 did not fit an external intake; two of them came from the same athlete one month apart – a strong indication that some people form boldenone themselves in traces.

White 2018: heart failure in a strength athlete

A 39-year-old weightlifter was admitted to hospital with acute, severe heart failure; the pumping function of the left ventricle was about 15 %. The treating physicians attributed the heart muscle disease to testosterone and boldenone in the preceding three months. A single case does not prove a connection, but it fits the cardiac findings in anabolic steroid users overall.

Winnett 2011: false kidney finding

A 37-year-old was referred because of abnormal kidney values, without symptoms and without further signs of kidney disease. The cause of the markedly elevated creatinine was boldenone. The case shows that anabolic steroids can distort laboratory values – and that physicians need to know about the use in order to interpret findings correctly.

Where the data stop

  • No efficacy study. On muscle mass, strength, blood formation or performance there is no human study and no entry in ClinicalTrials.gov.
  • No safety study. Individual cases are known; how often harm occurs, nobody knows.
  • Veterinary medicine is no substitute. The approval for horses says nothing about benefits and risks in humans.
  • Products from the gray market. In Zurich drug checking for steroids, 52 % of the samples tested were counterfeit; the content and purity of underground products are unknown.

Status, approval and legal

Boldenone is not approved for humans. In the US, boldenone undecylenate is approved as a prescription veterinary drug for horses. In Germany, finished medicinal products may only be placed on the market with a marketing authorization (§ 21 of the German Medicinal Products Act).

Boldenone is listed by name in the annex of the German Anti-Doping Act. Manufacturing, trafficking and placing on the market for the purpose of doping in sport are prohibited, as are acquisition, possession and bringing into Germany in non-small quantities for this purpose; violations are punishable under § 4 with imprisonment of up to three years or a fine. In sport it is prohibited in and out of competition under the WADA Prohibited List 2026 under S1.1, anabolic androgenic steroids.

Safety

There are no substance-specific safety data on boldenone in humans, only case reports: severe heart failure under testosterone and boldenone and apparent kidney damage due to elevated creatinine. Everything else comes from the data on the class of substances: supplied androgens shut down the body’s own hormonal axis, with reduced fertility, and strain the heart and blood vessels; in addition there are psychological and metabolic effects, and in women masculinization. The Endocrine Society describes the widespread assumption that use is safe or manageable as a misjudgment.

Two practical points, without finger-wagging. First, boldenone distorts laboratory values such as creatinine; anyone having blood values measured should disclose the use. Second, a veterinary drug or underground product has not been tested for humans, and a good half of the steroid samples tested in Zurich were counterfeit. Before any step here, a conversation with a physician is needed, because of consequences that can be permanent.

Frequently asked questions about boldenone (Equipoise)

What is boldenone?

Boldenone is an anabolic androgenic steroid, a derivative of testosterone. As boldenone undecylenate, brand name Equipoise, it is a veterinary drug in the US for debilitated horses.

Is boldenone approved for humans?

No. Boldenone undecylenate is approved only as a veterinary drug, in the US for debilitated horses. For use in humans there is no approval and no study on efficacy.

Does boldenone work in humans?

Nobody has studied that. As an androgen it presumably has an anabolic effect, but how strong, with what balance of benefit and harm and with what long-term consequences is open. In humans, only metabolism and excretion have been measured.

Is boldenone legal in Germany?

It is not approved for humans. Boldenone is listed in the annex of the German Anti-Doping Act: trafficking and supplying it for doping are prohibited, as are acquisition and possession in non-small quantities for doping in sport. In sport it is prohibited at all times under the WADA list.

Can boldenone form in the body itself?

In traces, yes. That is why doping laboratories check low values via the carbon isotope ratio to see whether the substance came from outside; in one study, 11 of 23 samples did not fit an external intake.
