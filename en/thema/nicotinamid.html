Nicotinamide (niacinamide)

Vitamin · Niacinamide, nicotinic acid amide, NAM, nicotinamide, vitamin B3 (amide form)

Nicotinamide is the amide form of vitamin B3; in cosmetics it is called niacinamide. As a tablet, in a large Australian trial in people with several non-melanoma skin cancers, it reduced the number of new tumors by 23 percent, for as long as it was taken. In organ transplant recipients and for the prevention of type 1 diabetes, there was no effect.

What nicotinamide is

Alongside nicotinic acid, nicotinamide is the second form of niacin, that is, vitamin B3. It is found in meat, fish, grains and dairy products, and the body also makes niacin from the amino acid tryptophan. Unlike nicotinic acid, nicotinamide does not trigger a flush, the typical sensation of heat with reddening of the skin.

The distinction matters: nicotinamide riboside (NR) and NMN are different molecules, sold as NAD precursors carrying a sugar residue. The skin cancer trials in this text were carried out with plain nicotinamide; their results cannot be transferred to NR or NMN.

How it works

Nicotinamide is a precursor of NAD+, a coenzyme that every cell needs for energy production and DNA repair. UV radiation damages the DNA of skin cells and at the same time dampens the skin’s defenses. According to the study authors, nicotinamide improves the repair of UV-induced DNA damage and reduces the UV-induced suppression of the skin’s defenses.

This is a plausible pathway, shown mainly in the laboratory and on skin endpoints. Whether it results in less skin cancer is decided by the controlled trials, and their results differ depending on the patient group.

What is well supported

  • Fewer non-melanoma skin cancers in high-risk patients. In the double-blind phase 3 trial ONTRAC with 386 people who had had at least two non-melanoma skin cancers in the previous 5 years, 23 percent fewer new basal cell and squamous cell carcinomas occurred under nicotinamide over 12 months.
  • Fewer skin cancer precursors. In ONTRAC, actinic keratoses were 11 percent less frequent than under placebo after 3 months and 20 percent less frequent after 9 months.
  • A similar signal in everyday practice. In a cohort of 33,822 US veterans, intake was associated with 14 percent less skin cancer, and with 54 percent less when started after the first skin cancer.
  • Well tolerated over one year. In ONTRAC, the number and type of adverse effects did not differ appreciably from placebo.

What the studies show

ONTRAC 2015: phase 3 in people with several non-melanoma skin cancers

Chen and colleagues randomly assigned 386 people with at least two non-melanoma skin cancers in the previous 5 years to 500 mg of nicotinamide twice daily or placebo for 12 months, with dermatological checks every 3 months. New non-melanoma skin cancers occurred 23 percent less often under nicotinamide (95% interval 4 to 38, p = 0.02). For squamous cell carcinomas, the difference was 30 percent, for basal cell carcinomas 20 percent, the latter without significance. In the 6 months after stopping, there was no longer any advantage.

ONTRANS 2023: no effect in organ transplant recipients

The same research group tested the same regimen in 158 people after an organ transplant, who have a particularly high risk of skin cancer because of immunosuppression. After 12 months, there were 207 new non-melanoma skin cancers under nicotinamide and 210 under placebo (rate ratio 1.0). There was also no difference in actinic keratoses and quality of life. The trial was stopped early because of slow recruitment.

US veterans 2025: a large cohort

Breglio and colleagues analyzed health data from 12,287 veterans taking nicotinamide and 21,479 comparable veterans not taking it, with an average age of 77 and 98 percent men. The risk of skin cancer was 14 percent lower overall and 54 percent lower when started after the first skin cancer; the later it was started, the smaller the advantage. In transplant recipients, there was no significant reduction overall. As a retrospective observational study, it can show associations but cannot prove cause.

Two meta-analyses, two conclusions

Mainville 2022 found, across 5 studies with 552 patients, a halving of skin cancer risk (rate ratio 0.50; 0.29 to 0.85) with moderate strength of evidence, plus more gastrointestinal complaints. Tosti 2023 pooled 4 studies, including the transplant trial, and found no established effect (rate ratio 0.82; 0.61 to 1.12). The difference lies mainly in whether immunosuppressed patients are included.

Glaucoma: early signals

In a double-blind crossover trial with 57 treated glaucoma patients, an electrophysiological measure of the inner retina improved by 14.8 percent under nicotinamide and by 5.2 percent under placebo (Hui 2020). Whether this slows vision loss is to be clarified by an ongoing phase 3 trial with nicotinamide and pyruvate over 21 months (Shukla 2026).

Type 1 diabetes: ENDIT 2004

In the European ENDIT trial, 552 relatives of people with type 1 diabetes who already had islet cell antibodies received high-dose nicotinamide or placebo for 5 years. 82 versus 77 developed diabetes (hazard ratio 1.07). The number of serious adverse effects did not differ, and the children’s growth was not impaired.

Where the data stop

  • Skin cancer protection for everyone. ONTRAC studied people with a history of several non-melanoma skin cancers. For people without this history, there is no trial.
  • In immunosuppression. In organ transplant recipients, the only phase 3 trial showed no effect, and the cohort only a partial signal.
  • Lasting protection. The effect lasted only during intake; data on skin cancer over more than one year of intake come only from observational studies.
  • Melanoma. The endpoint of the trials was non-melanoma skin cancer, not melanoma.
  • Anti-aging and “NAD booster”. For aging goals, there are no endpoint studies on nicotinamide as a tablet.

Status, approval and legal

In the EU, nicotinamide may be added to food supplements as a source of niacin. The German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) recommends a maximum of 160 mg per recommended daily intake for this. In 2002, the EU’s former Scientific Committee on Food derived a tolerable upper intake level of 900 mg per day for adults. The daily amount of 1,000 mg used in ONTRAC is above both values; the trials ran under regular dermatological monitoring.

For products with more than 16 mg of nicotinamide per daily dose, the BfR recommends a warning that pregnant women should not take them.

Safety

In ONTRAC there were no appreciable differences in adverse effects over 12 months, nor in ONTRANS, including in blood and urine values. ENDIT found no accumulation of serious adverse effects over 5 years. The 2022 meta-analysis saw more gastrointestinal complaints. A review on the safety of high doses describes reversible liver damage at very high amounts and advises regarding nicotinamide in adults from 3 g per day as a drug with toxic potential and not taking it unsupervised. Frequently asked questions about nicotinamide

What is the difference between nicotinamide and nicotinamide riboside?

Both are precursors of NAD+, but they are different molecules. Nicotinamide is a simple form of vitamin B3; nicotinamide riboside additionally carries a sugar residue. The studies on non-melanoma skin cancer were carried out with nicotinamide. Their results do not automatically apply to nicotinamide riboside or NMN.

Does nicotinamide protect against skin cancer?

In the phase 3 trial ONTRAC, 23 percent fewer new non-melanoma skin cancers occurred over 12 months in people with a history of at least two non-melanoma skin cancers. After stopping, the advantage disappeared. For people without a history of skin cancer and for melanoma, there are no corresponding studies.

Does nicotinamide help after an organ transplant?

The only phase 3 trial in organ transplant recipients so far found no difference after 12 months: 207 new non-melanoma skin cancers under nicotinamide, 210 under placebo. The trial was stopped early. A large observational study saw a signal for fewer squamous cell carcinomas in transplant recipients only when started early.

Is nicotinamide the same as niacinamide and niacin?

Nicotinamide and niacinamide are the same substance; the second name is common in cosmetics. Niacin is the umbrella term for nicotinamide and nicotinic acid. In high amounts, nicotinic acid changes blood lipids and triggers a flush; nicotinamide does neither.

Is nicotinamide permitted in Germany?

Yes, nicotinamide may be added to food supplements as a source of niacin. The BfR recommends a maximum of 160 mg per daily dose for this. The tolerable upper intake level for adults is 900 mg per day. In the skin cancer trials, 1,000 mg daily was given under dermatological monitoring.

What side effects does nicotinamide have?

In the skin cancer trials, adverse effects over 12 months did not differ appreciably from placebo; a meta-analysis found more gastrointestinal complaints. At very high amounts, liver damage has been described. For pregnant women, the BfR recommends a warning on products with more than 16 mg. This information does not replace medical advice.
