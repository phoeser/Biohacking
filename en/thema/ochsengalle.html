Ox bile

Enzyme · beef bile, Ox Bile, Fel tauri, bile salts, conjugated bile acids

Ox bile is dried beef bile, a mixture of natural bile acids. It is often marketed for the period after removal of the gallbladder, because bile acids are needed for fat digestion. That supplemental bile acids can increase fat absorption has been shown in individual patients with short bowel syndrome; for the period after gallbladder surgery there is no study.

What ox bile is

The liver makes bile acids itself. With the bile they reach the small intestine, where they make dietary fat digestible. Ox bile extract from dried beef bile supplies a mixture of natural conjugated bile acids. A related bile acid that has been studied on its own is TUDCA.

The BK-Score gives 2 out of 10 points for human evidence. The principle has been shown in genuine bile acid deficiency; the advertised use after gallbladder surgery has not been studied.

How it works

In short bowel syndrome, bile acids are lost through the gut, too few reach the duodenum, and fat is poorly digested. Supplemental bile acids can close this gap; balance studies measured how much fat is actually absorbed afterwards.

After removal of the gallbladder the situation is different. Bile continues to flow into the duodenum, just without intermediate storage. Some of those who have had the operation tend to develop diarrhea instead, because more bile acids reach the colon, where they draw water and salts into the gut.

What is well supported

  • More fat absorption in short bowel syndrome. In balance studies with individual patients, fat absorption under natural conjugated bile acids rose by around 40 g per day.
  • Weight gain in individual cases. A severely emaciated patient gained from 80 to 98 pounds in 4 months under bile acids from ox bile; another patient also gained weight over 3 months.
  • An approved medicine based on the same principle. Cholic acid (Orphacol) has been approved in the EU since 2013 for inborn errors of bile acid synthesis.

What the studies show

Short bowel with stoma: Gruy-Kapral 1999

The study examined a severely emaciated patient with short bowel syndrome and an ileostomy in whom intravenous nutrition was not possible. In balance studies, both natural bile acids from ox bile and the synthetic cholylsarcosine increased fat absorption by about 40 g per day; calcium absorption also rose. Stoma output did not increase appreciably. In a four-month trial with 2 g of natural bile acids per meal, her weight rose from 80 to 98 pounds, without side effects.

Short bowel with colon: Emmett 2003

In a patient with short bowel, a preserved colon, elevated urinary oxalate and kidney stones, 9 g of natural conjugated bile acids per day lowered fecal fat excretion from 119 to 79 g per day and urinary oxalate from 87 to 64 mg per day. In a three-month trial at home, oxalate normalized, the patient gained weight and was less hungry. The synthetic cholylsarcosine had a similar but weaker effect.

Ox bile versus cholylsarcosine: Kapral 2004

Four short bowel patients with a preserved colon received the synthetic cholylsarcosine, two of them also ox bile extract for comparison. Cholylsarcosine increased fat absorption from 65.5 to 94.5 g per day. Natural bile acids also reduced fat loss, but less, and markedly worsened diarrhea. The authors stress that, because the condition is rare, the study was not controlled.

After gallbladder removal: Mao 2025 and Huang 2023

In a telephone follow-up survey of 3,385 people after laparoscopic gallbladder removal in China, 14.2 percent had diarrhea after the operation in the first year. A review describes the main cause as bile acids that continue to flow into the gut after the operation and arrive in the colon in larger amounts. We did not find a study testing ox bile after gallbladder removal.

Where the data stop

  • After gallbladder surgery. No study, and it has not been shown that people who have had the operation lack bile acids.
  • In healthy people. No data on digestion, bloating or nutrient absorption.
  • Controlled studies. All human data come from individual cases and very small series, without placebo.
  • Diarrhea as a risk. Precisely the complaint that is common after the operation was worsened by natural bile acids in a short bowel series.

Status, approval and legal

Ox bile extract is sold in Germany as a food supplement. Health claims on fat digestion are not authorized for it in the EU; bile does not appear in the list under Regulation (EU) No 432/2012. We did not find an official assessment of ox bile as a food ingredient by EFSA or the BfR.

Pure cholic acid is approved as a medicine in the EU: Orphacol since September 12, 2013, for adults and children from one month of age with two rare inborn enzyme defects of bile acid synthesis. It is prescription-only; treatment is started and monitored by a physician specializing in liver disease.

Safety

Reliable safety data are lacking. In the short bowel studies, natural bile acids were taken for weeks to months, by one patient without side effects, by two patients with a preserved colon with markedly more diarrhea. Anyone who has diarrhea after gallbladder removal should have the cause clarified by a doctor, because excess bile acids are precisely a common cause there.

Frequently asked questions about ox bile

What is ox bile?

Ox bile is dried bile from cattle. It supplies a mixture of natural conjugated bile acids that support fat digestion in the small intestine. It is sold as a food supplement, often for people after removal of the gallbladder.

Do I need ox bile after gallbladder removal?

There is no study on this. After the operation, bile continues to flow into the gut. More common than a deficiency is then diarrhea from increased bile acids in the colon, in a large follow-up survey in 14.2 percent of those operated on. Complaints after the operation should be clarified by a doctor.

Does ox bile improve fat digestion?

In individual patients with short bowel syndrome who lack bile acids, fat absorption rose by around 40 g per day under natural bile acids. In healthy people and after gallbladder removal this has not been studied. There are no controlled studies.

What side effects does ox bile have?

Systematic data are lacking. In a small series of short bowel patients with a preserved colon, natural bile acids markedly worsened diarrhea. Another patient tolerated them over 4 months without side effects.

Are bile acids available as medicines?

In the EU, cholic acid (Orphacol) has been approved since 2013 for rare inborn errors of bile acid synthesis, prescription-only and under the supervision of a liver specialist. A related bile acid is TUDCA, which is prescribed as a medicine in Italy.
