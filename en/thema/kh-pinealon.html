Bioregulator

Pinealon

Tripeptide, neuro/pineal bioregulator · EDR · Glu-Asp-Arg

Pinealon is the brain peptide of the Khavinson school: three amino acids, Glu-Asp-Arg, EDR for short, derived from the Russian cerebral cortex preparation Cortexin. In cell and animal models it protects nerve cells against oxygen deprivation, oxidative stress and Alzheimer-type damage. Several Russian observations in humans report better memory and slower biological aging; controlled studies are missing.

In short

Pinealon is a synthetic tripeptide that Khavinson’s group isolated from the peptide mixture Cortexin and uses as a neuroprotector. For a bioregulator peptide, the preclinical data are broad: fewer free radicals and less cell death in nerve cells, protection against oxygen deprivation, preserved nerve connections in the Alzheimer model and longer dendrites in human nerve cells grown in the lab. In humans there are several Russian observations, for example in 72 patients after traumatic brain injury and in professional drivers, all with favorable results. The catch: none of these studies is recognizably randomized and blinded, and the only independent animal experiment has so far found only a trend.

What it is

Pinealon belongs to the cytogens, the synthetic short peptides of the Khavinson school. According to a review by the group, the sequence glutamic acid, aspartic acid, arginine was isolated from Cortexin, a polypeptide mixture from cerebral cortex that is used in Russia as a neuroprotective medicine. The name is therefore somewhat misleading: Pinealon sounds like pineal gland, but is a derivative of the cerebral cortex preparation.

Pinealon is promoted for mental clarity, concentration and recovery after stress. Russian occupational physicians have used it specifically in occupational groups under high strain, such as truck drivers and train drivers. In the study on locomotive crews, the workers took capsules for two weeks, after which markers of biological age and adaptability improved. The idea behind it: those who age faster because of occupational strain and unfavorable working hours should be able to counteract this with peptides.

How it is supposed to work

Like the other short peptides of the school, Pinealon is supposed to enter the cell and reach the nucleus. For Pinealon this has actually been shown with labeled molecules: in HeLa cells, the peptide was found in the cytoplasm, the nucleus and the nucleolus. In the test tube, it preferentially binds to certain DNA sections.

Measurable above all are protective effects against oxidative stress. In cerebellar nerve cells, neutrophils and PC12 cells, Pinealon limited the accumulation of reactive oxygen species and necrotic cell death, and it altered the cell cycle. Among four short peptides tested, it was the most effective in the oxygen deprivation model; the authors attribute this to activated endogenous antioxidant enzymes and dampened overexcitation via the NMDA receptor. In cortical cultures it also increased serotonin.

In old rats exposed to acute oxygen deprivation, Pinealon lowered inflammatory responses in the brain to the reference level. In 18-month-old animals it influenced behavior and messenger substances under oxygen deprivation and hypothermia. And the offspring of pregnant rats with elevated homocysteine learned better when the mother animals received Pinealon.

Alzheimer models and human nerve cells

Pinealon has received the most attention in the Alzheimer context. In mouse hippocampal neurons damaged with amyloid, the number of mature dendritic spines rose by 71 percent under Pinealon and returned to normal levels. In Alzheimer mice it prevented the loss of such spines. In nerve cells reprogrammed from skin cells of older women, it lengthened the dendrites overall by 46 percent and increased the main processes by 28 percent.

What users report

A biohacking blogger describes, in 2023, after about a month of taking capsules, subtly faster problem solving but no noticeable effect on memory, and considers Pinealon more a remedy for incipient decline than a performance boost for healthy people. The post contains affiliate links. This is a single, uncontrolled report, but it fits the study situation well: the human data come almost entirely from people with prior damage or high strain.

What is well supported

The strongest point is the consistency of the preclinical findings. Whether cell culture, old rats, rat offspring or Alzheimer mice: Pinealon protects nerve cells in very different damage models, and this extends to human nerve cells grown in the lab. In 2026, the first animal experiment outside the Khavinson circle was added. In a Swiss and Spanish study, mice on Pinealon showed a trend toward better working memory, without recognizable side effects and without abnormalities in the blood count.

In humans there are more observations than is often assumed. According to a review by the group, Pinealon improved memory, headaches and performance in 72 patients with sequelae of traumatic brain injury. In a comparison of methods with 110 participants, the combination of Pinealon and Vesugen had the strongest effect on biological age and was among the safest procedures.

What the studies show

72 patients after traumatic brain injury

A Russian review from 2020 reports that Pinealon, in addition to standard therapy, improved memory, shortened and weakened headaches and increased performance in 72 patients with sequelae of brain injury and cerebrasthenia. How the patients were allocated and whether there was a comparison group is not stated in the review.

Comparison of methods with 110 participants (2016)

Myakotnykh and colleagues compared dry carbon dioxide baths, hyperbaric oxygen therapy, massage and the peptides Vesugen and Pinealon. The combined peptide administration had the strongest effect on markers of biological age; massage and peptides proved to be the safest. The abstract does not describe allocation or blinding.

Truck drivers under chronic stress (2012)

Bashkireva and Artamonova examined 150 truck drivers and 150 metalworkers. The drivers more often showed signs of psychological overload; under the bioregulator peptides, psycho-emotional scores and stress resistance improved, most clearly with Pinealon plus Vesugen. The metalworkers served as a comparison for strain, not as a placebo group.

Independent mouse experiment (2026)

Marín-Jerez and colleagues tested five longevity candidates in 5 male mice each, aged 7 months, over 8 weeks. Pinealon was the only candidate to show a trend toward better working memory, which, however, was not significant; no side effects were found. The work is a preprint that has not yet been peer-reviewed.

Where the data stop

There is no randomized, blinded study, and none is registered in ClinicalTrials.gov. The human studies are published in Russian, often use Pinealon in combination with Vesugen and measure soft endpoints such as a calculated biological age or questionnaires. The series with 72 patients is accessible only via a review by the developers. The independent mouse experiment is very small with 5 animals per group and found only a trend.

For some advertised uses, studies are missing entirely, for example for sleep quality. How much Pinealon reaches the brain after swallowing has never been measured; uptake via peptide transporters has only been made plausible by computer modeling. Whether the effects occur in healthy, young people without prior damage is open.

Status, approval and legal

Pinealon is not an approved medicine in Germany and the EU. As a food supplement it would require authorization under the Novel Food Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, which has not been granted. It is traded as a capsule product or research peptide via online retailers. It is not named on the World Anti-Doping Agency’s Prohibited List, but as a non-approved substance it falls under group S0 and is prohibited in sport at all times.

Safety

There are no systematic safety data in humans. In the comparison of methods, the peptides were among the safest procedures, and the independent mouse experiment found no side effects and no abnormal blood count. A Russian observation in 32 multimorbid patients, however, reported pro-oxidative activity and fewer blood stem cells in the blood under Pinealon and Vesugen, which was never followed up. Interactions with psychotropic drugs, antiepileptics or anticoagulants have not been studied. After a traumatic brain injury or with memory problems, the medical assessment belongs in the hands of a physician. There are no data for pregnancy and breastfeeding, and with research products, content and purity are untested.

Frequently asked questions about Pinealon

What is Pinealon?

Pinealon is a synthetic tripeptide with the sequence Glu-Asp-Arg from Vladimir Khavinson’s bioregulator series. It was isolated from the cerebral cortex preparation Cortexin and is supposed to protect nerve cells.

Does Pinealon act on the pineal gland?

Despite its name, Pinealon comes from a cerebral cortex preparation, not from the pineal gland. The studies mainly examine nerve cell protection, oxygen deprivation and memory, not the pineal gland or melatonin.

Does Pinealon help against brain fog?

There is no targeted study on this. Russian observations report better memory after traumatic brain injury and better stress resistance in professional drivers; controlled studies in healthy people are missing.

Are there studies on Pinealon in humans?

Yes, several small Russian observations, for example in 72 patients after traumatic brain injury, in truck drivers and in older people. None of them is recognizably randomized and blinded.

Is Pinealon legal in Germany?

It is approved neither as a medicine nor as a food supplement and is sold online as a research peptide or capsule product. For athletes it counts as a prohibited substance of group S0.

Is Pinealon combined with Vesugen?

In several Russian studies, yes, and there the combination had the strongest effect on stress resistance and biological age. Which share is due to which peptide cannot be cleanly separated from these studies.
