Synephrine (Bitter Orange)

Stimulant · p-synephrine, oxedrine, Sympatol, bitter orange, Seville orange, Citrus aurantium, Bitter Orange Extract, Advantra Z

Synephrine is the active compound of bitter orange and is found in many fat burners and pre-workout powders. During exercise it makes the body burn measurably more fat, as several small studies show. For weight loss over weeks, none of this has materialized so far, and in combination with caffeine the authorities urge caution.

In short

Synephrine, more precisely p-synephrine, is a plant compound from bitter orange, chemically related to adrenaline and ephedrine. A single dose increases fat burning during endurance training without energy expenditure or heart rate rising measurably; this has been shown in several double-blind studies. Weight loss over weeks, by contrast, is not established: the 2022 meta-analysis found no difference from placebo in 3 studies. The catch is the cardiovascular system: after 8 weeks blood pressure rose, and together with caffeine the effects can amplify each other. The BfR recommends no more than about 6.7 mg per day from food supplements, the expert commission of BVL and BfArM about 21 mg in total.

What it is

Bitter orange, also called Seville orange, is the parent plant of orange marmalade, candied orange peel and bitter liqueurs. Extracts are obtained from its unripe fruits and peels, usually containing 3 to 6 percent synephrine. The most important constituent is p-synephrine, which also occurs in oranges, mandarins and clementines. Depending on the measurement, orange juice contains 3 to 85 mg per kilogram. Anyone who eats a lot of citrus fruit therefore takes in some synephrine anyway.

Synephrine is not a new substance. As oxedrine, trade name Sympatol, it was sold from the 1930s as a medicine for low blood pressure; in Germany no such medicine is approved today. Synephrine experienced its second rise when ephedra and ephedrine disappeared from food supplements. Bitter orange extract became the replacement in fat burners and pre-workout products, usually together with caffeine.

How it is supposed to work

Synephrine acts on the sympathetic nervous system, according to the expert commission of BVL and BfArM mainly via alpha-1 and beta-3 adrenoceptors. Beta-3 receptors are located mainly in fat tissue, where they promote fat breakdown; alpha-1 receptors constrict blood vessels. Because synephrine is more water-soluble than ephedrine, it reaches the brain less easily and is hardly stimulating. The side effects to be expected therefore lie more with the heart and circulation.

An important point for interpretation: in rodents, p-synephrine binds more strongly to adrenergic receptors than in humans. Animal experiments therefore cannot simply be transferred. The form also matters, because the more potent m-synephrine does not occur naturally in bitter orange and points to adulteration. In a pharmacokinetic study, the half-life of synephrine was 3.0 hours.

What users are looking for

Most people take synephrine to burn more fat before training or as part of a diet. Many pre-workout powders combine it with high amounts of caffeine and other plant extracts. User reports often mention more drive, less hunger and sweating more easily. These are uncontrolled reports that only partly match the study data: the increased fat burning during exercise has been measured, but an effect on appetite and body weight over weeks has not been established.

What is well supported

The acute effect during endurance exercise is best studied. A Madrid research group tested a single dose of 3 mg per kilogram of body weight in several double-blind crossover studies. In 18 healthy people, maximal fat burning during cycling rose from 0.29 to 0.40 g per minute, while energy expenditure remained the same. In 14 healthy people, the body burned 37.3 g of fat instead of 33.6 g under placebo in one hour of cycling; in 15 elite cyclists, too, the fuel mix shifted in favor of fat. Heart rate did not rise in these studies. Synephrine acts about as strongly as caffeine, but the combination brought no additional effect.

It is also well supported that single doses without caffeine often hardly change circulation in healthy people. With 49 mg p-synephrine, heart rate, ECG and systolic blood pressure remained unchanged over 8 hours in 18 healthy people; with 46.9 mg, blood pressure likewise did not rise in another study. A study with 50 mg found a resting metabolic rate 65 kcal higher without a change in heart rate and blood pressure.

What the studies show

The 2022 meta-analysis

An independent group from Hungary pooled 18 placebo-controlled studies with 341 participants. Shortly after intake, blood pressure and heart rate did not rise significantly but showed an upward tendency. After 8 weeks with 10 to 49 mg per day, blood pressure was markedly higher in 2 studies with 75 participants, systolic by 6.37 mmHg and diastolic by 4.33 mmHg. For weight, 3 studies found no difference from placebo. The authors’ conclusion: no evidence of weight loss, but a trend toward higher blood pressure.

The fat oxidation study

18 healthy adults took 3 mg per kilogram of p-synephrine or placebo once and then performed a graded cycling test. At rest, nothing changed. Under load they burned more fat and fewer carbohydrates; maximal fat burning rose from 0.29 to 0.40 g per minute. Energy expenditure and the intensity at which the most fat was burned remained the same.

The 8-week strength training study

80 strength-trained men received placebo, a pre-workout powder with 284 mg caffeine, or the same powder with added bitter orange extract and 20 mg synephrine for 8 weeks. Body composition, strength, resting heart rate and blood pressure did not differ at the end, and synephrine brought no additional benefit over the powder without it. Side effects did not occur more frequently. One co-author worked for the manufacturer.

Where the data stop

For weight loss, the proof is missing. The meta-analysis could only evaluate 3 studies on weight lasting 6 to 8 weeks, all with a training program, two with a diet, and found no difference from placebo. The expert commission of BVL and BfArM also writes that a weight-reducing effect of the combination with caffeine has not been demonstrated so far. More fat in the fuel mix during a training session is simply not the same as less body fat after months. For performance, the picture is similarly thin: sprinters did not run faster with synephrine; in a study with 12 men, they managed more repetitions in the squat.

In addition, there are limits in study design. The longest controlled data cover 8 weeks; nobody has studied hard endpoints such as cardiac events. The findings on fat burning come predominantly from one research group, and some of the safety studies are close to industry. And the results contradict each other: in 15 healthy people, systolic blood pressure rose by up to 7.3 mmHg and heart rate by up to 4.2 beats per minute after 900 mg of bitter orange extract; other studies found nothing. There are no studies for people with high blood pressure, heart disease or for pregnant women.

Status, approval and legal

Bitter orange extract with synephrine may be marketed in Germany as a food supplement. According to the EU Novel Food Catalogue, the fruits of bitter orange are not a novel food, and leaves and flowers are not novel in food supplements. In 2021 the Joint Expert Commission of BVL and BfArM found that products containing synephrine cannot currently be classified as medicines, and recommends no more than about 21 mg p-synephrine per day across all foods combined. In 2012 the BfR had recommended about 6.7 mg per day for food supplements. There is no statutory maximum amount; other countries set their own values, France 20 mg, Canada 50 mg without caffeine. Health claims on fat burning are not authorized. In sports, synephrine is on WADA’s 2026 monitoring program and is not banned, whereas the related substances octopamine and oxilofrine (methylsynephrine) are.

Safety

The concern is the heart and circulation. The meta-analysis found higher blood pressure after 8 weeks, and the BfR warns that synephrine and caffeine can amplify each other’s effects on heart rate, heart rhythm and blood pressure. A 2023 review counted 30 case reports with 35 patients after pre-workout products containing synephrine, including heart attacks, arrhythmias and strokes. This does not prove a causal relationship; mixed products with caffeine were usually involved. The BfR advises people with high blood pressure, overweight or cardiovascular disease, pregnant women, breastfeeding women and children against it, and urges caution with intensive exercise. Bitter orange extract inhibits the liver enzyme CYP3A4; with MAO inhibitors, blood pressure and thyroid medications, intake should be discussed with a physician beforehand.

Frequently asked questions about Synephrine (Bitter Orange)

Does synephrine help with weight loss?

That has not been established so far. A 2022 meta-analysis found no difference in weight compared with placebo in 3 studies over 6 to 8 weeks. All that has been measured is somewhat higher fat burning during training.

Is synephrine the same as ephedrine?

No, but the substances are chemically related. Synephrine is more water-soluble and reaches the brain less easily, so it is less stimulating. The effect on the heart and circulation is weaker, but not ruled out.

Can I combine synephrine with caffeine?

The BfR advises against it, because the two substances can amplify each other’s effects on heart rate and blood pressure. In one study, the combination also brought no additional effect on fat burning. Most case reports involved exactly such mixed products.

How much synephrine is safe?

The BfR considers about 6.7 mg per day acceptable for food supplements, the expert commission of BVL and BfArM about 21 mg per day from all foods combined. There is no statutory maximum amount in Germany. Many sports products exceed this, so it is worth checking the label.

Is synephrine doping?

No, in 2026 synephrine is only on WADA’s monitoring program and is not banned. The related substances octopamine and methylsynephrine, however, are banned. Methylsynephrine has already been found as an adulterant in food supplements.

Who should avoid synephrine?

People with high blood pressure, heart disease or arrhythmias, pregnant women, breastfeeding women and children. Anyone taking blood pressure medication, thyroid medication or MAO inhibitors should ask a physician first.
