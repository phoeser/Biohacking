Tretinoin

Retinoid for use on the skin, prescription-only · retinoic acid, all-trans retinoic acid, vitamin A acid, Cordes VAS, Renova, Retin-A

Tretinoin is the acid form of vitamin A and is approved in Germany as a prescription-only cream for acne. For light-induced skin aging it is one of the few anti-aging agents with blinded studies lasting up to two years: fine wrinkles and roughness improve, and at 0.05 % so do pigment spots. The effect is smaller than “rejuvenation” suggests; the US approval explicitly states that tretinoin does not eliminate wrinkles. It is contraindicated in pregnancy.

What tretinoin is

Tretinoin (all-trans retinoic acid) is a retinoid, that is, a derivative of vitamin A, for use on the skin. In Germany it is prescription-only and approved, for example, as a 0.05 % cream for acne vulgaris; according to the summary of product characteristics, it is applied thinly once or twice daily. In Germany it is not approved for skin aging.

In the US, a 0.02 % cream is explicitly approved as an adjunct for fine facial wrinkles, embedded in skin care and consistent sun protection. What is rated is the state of knowledge: for acne and photoaging there are randomized trials, tissue samples and decades of use.

How it works

Tretinoin binds to retinoic acid receptors in the cell nucleus and thereby changes which genes are read in skin cells. In acne, it targets the disturbed keratinization in the hair follicle.

In sun-damaged skin, tissue samples under tretinoin showed a thicker epidermis, a more compact horny layer and less melanin. In a two-year study, a marker of new collagen formation rose in the dermis without any increase in abnormal cell changes. The chain of action is therefore well described in humans.

What is well supported

  • Ranked for acne. A network meta-analysis of 221 randomized trials with more than 65,000 participants found topical retinoids to be about as effective as antibiotics for inflammatory lesions. The strongest, after isotretinoin tablets, were triple combinations including a topical retinoid.
  • Photoaging measured under blinding. In a study with 296 participants, the skin improved after 24 weeks in 68 % under 0.05 % cream and in 43 % under cream without active ingredient. Fine wrinkles, pigment spots and roughness improved more than under the comparison cream.
  • Over two years as well. In a placebo-controlled study with 204 participants over up to two years, fine and coarser wrinkles, pigment spots and sallow skin improved more than under placebo; the tissue samples showed no increase in abnormal cell changes.
  • A separate approval for fine wrinkles. In the US, the approval of the 0.02 % cream rests on five controlled studies with 324 treated and 332 comparison patients over 24 weeks.

What the studies show

Weiss 1988: the first blinded study

Weiss 1988 (JAMA) treated one forearm with tretinoin and the other with cream without active ingredient for 16 weeks; on the face, half received tretinoin and the other half the comparison cream. All 30 participants who completed the study showed improvement on the treated forearm, not on the comparison arm. On the face, the skin improved in 14 of 15 under tretinoin and in none under the comparison cream. The side effect was skin irritation.

Olsen 1992: three concentrations

Olsen 1992 (J Am Acad Dermatol) compared three tretinoin concentrations with cream without active ingredient over 24 weeks in 296 participants with sun-damaged facial skin. Under 0.05 %, the skin improved in 68 %, under the comparison cream in 43 %; a good or very good result was seen in 26 % versus 11 %. The two lower concentrations did not differ significantly from the comparison cream. Redness, peeling and burning were mostly mild to moderate.

Kang 2005: two years

Kang 2005 (Am J Clin Dermatol) treated 204 participants with moderate to severe photoaging for up to two years with 0.05 % cream or placebo. Fine and coarser wrinkles, pigment spots, age spots and sallow skin improved more than under placebo (p < 0.05). Tissue samples showed no increase in abnormal skin or pigment cells; a marker of new collagen formation rose after twelve months (p = 0.0074).

Huang 2023: acne treatments compared

Huang 2023 (Ann Fam Med) analyzed 221 randomized trials with 65,601 participants on acne treatment. The most effective were isotretinoin tablets, followed by triple combinations of a topical retinoid, benzoyl peroxide and an antibiotic. As single agents, topical retinoids worked about as well as antibiotics on inflammatory lesions.

Where the data stop

  • How large the effect is. Even under cream without active ingredient, the skin improved in 43 % of participants. The US prescribing information states that many in the comparison group achieved the desired improvement in fine wrinkles with skin care and sun protection alone.
  • Deep wrinkles and rejuvenation. For the 0.02 % cream approved in the US, no effect on deep wrinkles, sagging, age spots or pigment spots has been shown. According to the approval, it does not eliminate wrinkles, does not repair sun damage and does not reverse skin aging.
  • Duration. In the US approval, daily use for more than 52 weeks is considered insufficiently studied. One placebo-controlled study ran for two years.
  • UV and skin cancer. In mouse experiments, tretinoin may have enhanced the tumor-inducing effect of UV radiation; this has not been studied in humans. People with a history of skin cancer were excluded from the US approval studies.

Status, approval and legal

Tretinoin is prescription-only in Germany (Medicines Prescription Ordinance). It is approved as a cream for acne vulgaris, for example at 0.05 %. There is no approval in Germany for skin aging; use for that purpose is an off-label use and belongs in the hands of a physician.

In the US, tretinoin cream 0.02 % is approved as an adjunct for softening fine facial wrinkles, under medical supervision and together with sun protection.

A European review of retinoids determined in 2018 that topical retinoids, too, must not be used in pregnancy or by women planning a pregnancy. Absorption through the skin is considered low; the rule is a precautionary measure. Warnings about psychiatric side effects apply only to oral retinoids.

Safety

Common are redness, peeling, burning, stinging, dryness and itching, especially at the start; according to the summary of product characteristics, they usually decrease over time. At the start, acne may temporarily worsen. The skin becomes more sensitive to light, which is why the summary of product characteristics advises avoiding sun and tanning beds and protecting treated skin.

Tretinoin is contraindicated in pregnancy and when planning a pregnancy; if a pregnancy occurs during use, treatment must be stopped. During breastfeeding it must not be applied to the breast area.

Frequently asked questions about tretinoin

Does tretinoin work against wrinkles?

Against fine wrinkles caused by photoaging, yes, in blinded studies lasting up to two years. In the largest 24-week study, the skin improved in 68 % under 0.05 % cream and in 43 % under cream without active ingredient. Tretinoin does not eliminate deep wrinkles and does not reverse skin aging.

Is tretinoin approved in Germany?

Yes, as a prescription-only cream for acne vulgaris. In Germany it is not approved for skin aging. In the US there is a separate approval for a 0.02 % cream for fine facial wrinkles.

Can tretinoin be used during pregnancy?

No. Since a European review in 2018, retinoids for application to the skin must not be used in pregnancy or when planning a pregnancy either. Absorption through the skin is low; the rule applies as a precaution.

What side effects does tretinoin have?

Most common are skin irritations such as redness, peeling, burning and dryness, especially at the beginning. The skin becomes more sensitive to light, and at the start acne may temporarily worsen.

Does tretinoin work against acne?

Yes. In a network meta-analysis of 221 randomized trials, topical retinoids worked about as well as antibiotics on inflammatory lesions. The strongest were combinations of a topical retinoid, benzoyl peroxide and an antibiotic.
