Botulinum toxin (“Botox”) for expression lines

Biohacking

10 min read · 10 sources, checked November 7, 2026

In the BK-Score: Well supported – human evidence 9 out of 10, safety data 8 out of 10. The data in humans are solid.

Botulinum toxin is a protein from the bacterium Clostridium botulinum, and it is one of the best-studied agents in aesthetic medicine. Injected in very small amounts, it relaxes individual facial muscles for a few months, and the lines above them smooth out. In the longevity scene it is increasingly also seen as prevention against skin ageing. For the lines there are strong data, for prevention so far hardly any.

In short

Botulinum toxin type A inhibits the release of the messenger acetylcholine at the nerve ending, and the treated muscle tenses less. For moderate to severe frown lines, this is one of the best-supported aesthetic treatments: in seven placebo-controlled double-blind trials with 1,474 participants, far more of those treated responded than on placebo, in the US approval trials after 30 days 80 versus 3 percent. The effect lasts about three to four months. Botulinum toxin is prescription-only and belongs in experienced medical hands. Whether early, regular treatments slow skin ageing in the long term rests so far only on a case report of twins.

What botulinum toxin is and how it works

Botulinum toxins prevent nerve endings from releasing acetylcholine, the messenger that gives the muscle the signal to contract. The result is a temporary, partial paralysis of the injected muscle; in glands, the same mechanism dampens sweat production. Against lines, this works where facial expressions fold the skin again and again: between the eyebrows (glabellar or frown lines), at the outer corners of the eyes (crow’s feet) and on the forehead.

According to the US prescribing information, the effect sets in one to two days after the injection, increases during the first week and lasts about three to four months for frown lines. After that, the muscle contracts again as before. The products differ, and their units cannot be converted into one another. For anyone interested in skin ageing as a whole: other procedures target the skin structure itself, for example laser, microneedling and radiofrequency or tretinoin; botulinum toxin, by contrast, acts on the muscle beneath the line.

What is well supported

For moderate to severe frown lines, the effect is as well established as for hardly any other aesthetic treatment. A meta-analysis pooled seven randomized, placebo-controlled double-blind trials with 1,474 participants, all with a single dose: the response rate was many times higher on botulinum toxin than on placebo, the lines at rest also improved, and side effects did not occur more often than on placebo. A network meta-analysis compared five type A products in 2023; all were clearly superior to placebo, and no difference in side effects was found between them.

It is also well established that the treatment rarely immunizes the body against the agent: in the US approval trials, 14 of 916 of those treated (1.5 percent) formed binding antibodies, and neutralizing antibodies were found in none.

What the studies show

Guo 2015 — meta-analysis on frown lines

Seven randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trials from 2002 to 2014 with 1,474 participants, all with 20 units. The relative risk of a response as rated by the investigators was 33.54, for the lines at rest 5.88. In no trial did the frequency of side effects differ between treatment and placebo.

US approval trials — how long it works

Two identically designed double-blind trials with 405 treated and 132 placebo participants aged between 18 and 75. A responder was anyone whose frown line on frowning was only absent or mild. After 30 days this applied to 80 percent of those treated and 3 percent of the placebo group, after 90 days to 48 versus 2 percent, after 120 days to 25 versus 2 percent. People aged 65 and over responded less often than younger people.

Li 2023 — products compared

A network meta-analysis of randomized trials on onabotulinumtoxinA, abobotulinumtoxinA, incobotulinumtoxinA, prabotulinumtoxinA and daxibotulinumtoxinA. All were far superior to placebo. DaxibotulinumtoxinA performed best for the proportion with at least one point of improvement; in side effects the products did not differ.

Binder 2006 — twins over 13 years

A case report on identical twin sisters: one was treated on the forehead and frown line two to three times a year for 13 years, the other only twice. In the regularly treated sister, no etched lines at rest were visible on the forehead and glabella, in the other they were; untreated facial regions aged equally in both. It is a single pair, without measurement and without blinding.

Schulze 2021 — mood and depression

The starting point is the idea that facial expression feeds back on mood. In five randomized trials, depressive symptoms improved more after injection into the frown line than after placebo injection, pooled effect size d equal to 0.98. The authors themselves name methodological limitations; botulinum toxin is not approved for this purpose.

Where the data stop

The studies measure whether a line is less visible after a treatment, and over weeks to months. What they do not answer: whether regular treatments from the mid-twenties, often called “prejuvenation” or “baby Botox”, slow skin ageing in the long term. For this there is no controlled long-term study, but the case report of a single pair of twins. Botulinum toxin is not intended for, and not studied for, facial ageing as a whole – volume, elasticity, pigment.

Safety over decades of repeated treatments has also hardly been recorded systematically; the FDA’s reporting data are incomplete because doctors do not report side effects completely, and many reports concerned uses outside the prescribing information, for example in dose, dilution or storage. The effect on mood is an interesting research question with small studies, not an established treatment.

Status, approval and legal

In Germany, botulinum toxin type A and B is prescription-only (German Ordinance on Prescription-Only Medicines, Annex 1). According to the Dear Doctor letter (Rote-Hand-Brief), agreed with the Federal Institute for Drugs and Medical Devices (BfArM), the products may only be used by physicians with appropriate experience. There are several approved products, some for neurological, some for cosmetic indications; which lines a product covers is stated in its prescribing information.

The US approval shows the range: cosmetically, onabotulinumtoxinA is approved for the temporary improvement of moderate to severe frown lines, crow’s feet, forehead lines and neck bands (platysma). For frown lines, 20 units are provided there, distributed over five injection sites; treatment more often than every three months has not been tested. Medically, it is approved among other things for overactive bladder, for the prevention of chronic migraine with at least 15 headache days a month, for spasticity, cervical dystonia, severe underarm sweating, eyelid spasm and squint. Aesthetic treatments are paid for out of pocket.

Safety

In the approval trials, the most common side effects were a drooping upper eyelid with frown lines (3 percent), eyelid swelling with crow’s feet (1 percent), and headache (9 percent) and a lowered eyebrow (2 percent) with forehead lines. In addition there are the usual consequences of an injection such as pain, redness and bruising. Treatment must not be given in case of allergy to any of the ingredients or an infection at the injection site.

All botulinum toxins carry a warning: very rarely the effect spreads beyond the injection site, with muscle weakness, difficulty swallowing, speaking or breathing, up to deaths. The risk is greatest with high doses in neurology and in people with pre-existing neurological conditions or swallowing disorders. Under cosmetic use, the FDA reported no deaths and 36 serious events up to 2003, 30 of which were known as a possible complication.

The greatest avoidable risk lies outside the doctor’s practice: in 2024, seven women in the USA fell ill after injections in non-medical settings, with drooping eyelids, difficulty swallowing and shortness of breath among other symptoms; four had to go to hospital, and at least four had probably received a counterfeit product.

Frequently asked questions about botulinum toxin (Botox)

How long does Botox last?

For frown lines about three to four months. In the US approval trials, 80 percent of those treated counted as responders after 30 days, and still 25 percent after 120 days. After that, the muscle contracts again as before.

Who is allowed to inject Botox in Germany?

Botulinum toxin is prescription-only. According to the Dear Doctor letter (Rote-Hand-Brief), its use belongs in the hands of physicians with appropriate experience. After injections outside medical facilities, serious illnesses up to hospital admission have been documented.

Does Botox help prevent lines?

That has not been studied. There is a case report on twins in which the regularly treated sister had fewer lines at rest on the forehead and frown line after 13 years. A controlled long-term study is lacking.

Is Botox dangerous?

In cosmetic doses and in medical hands, the most common side effects are a drooping upper eyelid or headache. Very rarely the effect spreads and causes difficulty swallowing or breathing; the risk is greater with high doses in neurology.

Does Botox help with depression?

In five small randomized trials, depressive symptoms improved more after injection into the frown line than on placebo. This is a research question with methodological limitations; botulinum toxin is not approved for it.
