Biotin (Vitamin B7)

Vitamin · Vitamin B7, vitamin H, D-biotin, coenzyme R

Biotin, also called vitamin B7 or vitamin H, is the vitamin on almost every pack for skin, hair and nails. It is indeed indispensable, and a genuine deficiency shows up exactly there. The interesting question is what it does when there is no deficiency, and what the high doses do to your lab values.

In short

Biotin is a coenzyme for enzymes in fat, protein and sugar metabolism. In a deficiency, whether congenital or acquired, skin, hair and nails reliably improve with biotin, and brittle nails responded well in older uncontrolled studies. For hair growth in people without a deficiency, however, there is no positive controlled study. The most important catch is a technical one: biotin in milligram doses can distort lab values, for example thyroid values and troponin in the heart attack test. Anyone taking biotin should say so before every blood draw.

What it is

Biotin is a water-soluble vitamin from the B group. The D-A-CH nutrition societies estimate the requirement of adults at 40 µg per day, for breastfeeding women at 45 µg; EFSA arrives at the same values. According to the German National Nutrition Survey, men in Germany take in a median of 43 to 48 µg, women 39 to 42 µg. Most people are therefore well supplied through food.

Good sources are liver, eggs, fish, nuts and seeds. A boiled egg provides about 10 µg. Hair and nail preparations, by contrast, often contain milligram amounts. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) cites products with up to 650 times the recommended daily amount.

How it works

Biotin sits as a cofactor in several carboxylases. These enzymes build fatty acids, break down certain amino acids and help with gluconeogenesis. If biotin is lacking, these pathways stall. This shows up first in fast-growing tissue, that is, in skin, hair and nails, and later also in the nervous system.

This explains its reputation as a beauty vitamin. But the logic applies in one direction: anyone who has too little benefits. Whether additional biotin produces more hair or stronger nails when supply is already full is another question, and so far the data do not answer it positively.

When a deficiency occurs

A classic deficiency is rare, but not exotic. Congenital biotinidase deficiency affects about 1 in 25,000 newborns and is part of newborn screening in Germany. Detected early and treated with biotin, these children have an excellent prognosis.

Acquired deficiencies arise from heavy alcohol use, smoking, long-term antibiotic or antiepileptic therapy and from raw eggs, whose egg-white protein avidin binds biotin. In pregnancy, according to the NIH, at least one third of women develop a mild deficiency. In a Zurich hair clinic, 38 percent of 541 women with hair loss had biotin levels in the deficient range.

What is well supported

The role in genuine deficiency is well supported. A review found 18 case reports in which biotin improved hair or nails, and in all of them an underlying disease or a deficiency was present. For brittle nails there are encouraging older series: in a Swiss study, 71 patients took 2.5 mg daily; of 45 evaluable patients, 41 showed firmer nails after an average of 5.5 months. Under the electron microscope, the nail plate became 25 percent thicker in 8 patients. A US practice reported improvement in 63 percent of its patients. The mild deficiency in pregnancy can also be measured and corrected: in a randomized study with 26 pregnant women, 300 µg daily for 14 days normalized a metabolic marker that continued to rise on placebo. And biotin is exceptionally well tolerated. Even very high doses in a large MS study were at placebo level for side effects.

What the studies show

Hair loss and biotin levels

Ralph Trüeb evaluated 541 women who came to his Zurich practice in 2014 because of hair loss and whose biotin levels were measured. 38 percent were below 100 ng/L and thus in the deficient range, 13 percent in the optimal range. Only 11 percent of the women with a deficiency had known risk factors. His conclusion: give biotin selectively after measurement, not across the board. The study is retrospective and has no treatment arm.

High dose in multiple sclerosis

In the first study, 154 patients with progressive MS received 100 mg of pharmaceutical biotin three times daily or placebo. 12.6 percent on biotin improved, nobody on placebo. The large follow-up study SPI2 with 642 patients in 13 countries did not confirm this: 12 versus 9 percent, statistically no difference. The manufacturer had already withdrawn the marketing authorization application in the EU in 2017. This is study information only, not a treatment option.

Lab values after 10 mg biotin

Six healthy adults took 10 mg biotin daily for one week, a typical dose in hair preparations. Afterwards, 37 lab tests were checked. Of 23 tests that work on the biotin-streptavidin principle, 9 gave false values, of the 14 others none. Depending on the test design, the results came out falsely high or falsely low.

Where the data stop

For the main promise, more and stronger hair in people without a deficiency, proof is lacking. A 2024 review found only three studies on oral biotin and hair growth. The best of them, double-blind and placebo-controlled, saw no difference from placebo. In a small randomized study with 10 healthy men, 5 mg biotin over 14 days did not change the growth rate. Only in combination with minoxidil did it increase, and that is a medicine. The nail studies date from 1989 to 1993, were uncontrolled or retrospective and in part subjectively assessed. There is no modern placebo-controlled nail study. The authorized EU health claim on the maintenance of normal hair sounds strong, but is based on the role in preventing deficiency. According to the German consumer advice center (Verbraucherzentrale), EFSA emphasizes that the population is adequately supplied and that additional biotin has no further effect on skin and hair. For nails there is no authorized claim at all. In multiple sclerosis, the high dose is off the table after the negative phase 3 study.

Status, approval and legal

Biotin is an authorized vitamin for food supplements and is also on the market as a medicine. Because there is no known toxicity, neither the EU nor the US has set a tolerable upper intake level, and the BfR has refrained from setting a maximum amount since 2016. Instead, it recommends a note on every biotin-containing preparation that physicians or the laboratory should be informed before lab tests. For medicines with at least 150 µg per dose, this warning has been mandatory since a Dear Doctor letter (Rote-Hand-Brief) in 2019. Authorized health claims include energy metabolism, the nervous system, skin, hair and mucous membranes. Biotin is not on any doping list.

Safety

Biotin itself is remarkably safe. The NIH reports patients who took up to 200 mg daily without signs of toxicity. The real risk lies in the laboratory. Many tests use the strong binding of biotin to streptavidin, and too much biotin in the blood confuses them. Thyroid values can then mimic Graves’ disease, as a case from Düsseldorf University Hospital showed. Troponin, the central marker for heart attack, can come out falsely low. The FDA issued warnings in 2017 and again in 2019 and reported one death. The EMA sees a possible risk from 150 µg daily. After 5 mg, the interference lasted 8 hours in one study, after 10 mg 1 to 2 days. In everyday clinical practice this is rare: among 572 acute patients in Maastricht, not a single troponin value was disturbed. Caution applies especially in impaired kidney function, in children and in pregnancy, because biotin then stays in the blood longer or has a more sensitive effect. Antiepileptics can lower biotin levels.

Frequently asked questions about biotin (vitamin B7)

Does biotin help against hair loss?

If a biotin deficiency is behind it, yes. Without a deficiency there is no controlled study showing more hair growth; the best study found no difference from placebo. With hair loss, it makes sense to have the cause clarified instead of taking high doses across the board.

Does biotin make nails stronger?

Older studies from Switzerland and the US report improvements in brittle nails in the majority of those treated, for example 41 of 45 patients. However, these studies had no placebo group. There is no authorized health claim for nails in the EU.

How much biotin do you need per day?

The D-A-CH societies and EFSA estimate 40 µg per day for adults, 45 µg for breastfeeding women. Men in Germany take in a median of 43 to 48 µg, women 39 to 42 µg. Hair preparations are often many times higher.

Do I have to stop biotin before a blood draw?

Biotin can interfere with many lab tests, including thyroid values and troponin. Before every blood draw, say that you take biotin, so the laboratory can respond. In one study, the interference lasted about 8 hours after 5 mg, 1 to 2 days after 10 mg.

Can you overdose on biotin?

No poisoning from biotin is known, which is why there is no upper limit in either the EU or the US. The risk of high doses is false lab values. In impaired kidney function, biotin stays in the blood longer.

Which foods contain biotin?

Liver, eggs, fish, nuts and seeds contain a lot of biotin. A boiled egg provides about 10 µg. Raw egg white, by contrast, binds biotin and can promote a deficiency if eaten regularly.
