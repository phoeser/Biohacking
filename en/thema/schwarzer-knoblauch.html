Black garlic

Herbs · black garlic, aged black garlic (ABG), fermented garlic

Black garlic is fresh garlic that has been matured for weeks under heat and high humidity. In a few small studies, blood pressure fell slightly; for blood lipids, the results contradict each other. No study has measured cardiovascular events. And it is not the same as aged garlic extract or garlic powder.

What black garlic is

To make black garlic, fresh garlic is kept for an extended period at 60 to 90 degrees and 80 to 90 percent humidity. The cloves turn black, the allicin content falls, and with it the pungent smell disappears. The process is often referred to as fermentation. The product is rich in S-allylcysteine.

Two other garlic products with their own body of studies must be distinguished from it: aged garlic extract (AGE), for which raw garlic is stored in aqueous ethanol for more than ten months, and garlic powder, which the EMA describes as a traditional herbal medicinal product. Results for these products do not automatically apply to black garlic, and vice versa. In the BK-Score, human evidence stands at 4 out of 10 points.

How it works

S-allylcysteine is considered the main candidate. That sulfur compounds from black garlic are absorbed has been shown in humans: after 20 g of black garlic, 33 sulfur-containing compounds were found in urine, including breakdown products of S-allylcysteine (Moreno-Ortega 2023). In a blood pressure study, nitric oxide in the blood and antioxidant capacity rose, and the activity of the blood-pressure-regulating enzyme ACE fell (Serrano 2023).

These are surrogate markers from a single study. How much of the small blood pressure effect they explain is open; most claims about further mechanisms come from laboratory and animal experiments.

What is well supported

  • A small reduction in blood pressure in two randomized trials. Diastolic −5.85 mmHg after 6 weeks in people with elevated LDL, mainly in men; in hypertension under medication, an additional −1.8 mmHg systolic and −1.5 mmHg diastolic after 12 weeks.
  • Higher HDL in a double-blind trial. After 12 weeks with 6 g daily, HDL rose and apolipoprotein B fell; LDL, total cholesterol and triglycerides remained unchanged.
  • No side effects in the cholesterol study. In the study by Jung 2014, no side effects were reported over 12 weeks. Black garlic is also eaten as an ordinary food.

What the studies show

Blood pressure: Valls 2022 and Serrano 2023

Valls 2022 gave 67 people with elevated LDL a standardized black garlic extract (250 mg with 1.25 mg S-allylcysteine) or placebo for 6 weeks each in a double-blind crossover study, along with dietary recommendations. Diastolic blood pressure fell by 5.85 mmHg (95 % interval −10.5 to −1.3), mainly in men with a diastolic value above 75 mmHg. Serrano 2023 tested a low-dose extract over 12 weeks in stage 1 hypertension under blood pressure medication, triple-blinded: an additional −1.8 mmHg systolic and −1.5 mmHg diastolic.

Blood lipids: Jung 2014 and Serrano 2026

In Jung 2014, 60 people with mildly elevated cholesterol took 6 g of black garlic daily or placebo for 12 weeks; 55 were analyzed. Triglycerides, LDL and total cholesterol did not differ, HDL rose and apolipoprotein B fell. Serrano 2026 gave 75 people with stage 1 hypertension 250 mg of extract (ABG10+) or placebo for 12 weeks. None of the usual blood lipid values changed; the authors themselves regard changes in lipoprotein subfractions as hypothesis-generating.

Heart failure: Liu 2018

In a study from China, 120 patients with heart failure due to coronary heart disease were divided evenly between black garlic and placebo and treated for 6 months. Ejection fraction, NT-proBNP and quality of life were better in the garlic group. It is a single study; a replication is lacking.

As a food: Villaño 2023

31 people each with and without elevated cholesterol ate 12 g of black garlic daily, about 4 cloves, for 12 weeks. Apolipoprotein A1 rose and several adhesion molecules in the blood fell. There was no placebo group; the comparison was before versus after.

Where the data stop

  • Cardiovascular events. What was measured was blood pressure, blood lipids and blood markers. No study has examined whether black garlic prevents heart attacks or strokes.
  • Cholesterol lowering. LDL and total cholesterol remained unchanged in the two placebo-controlled studies on blood lipids.
  • Independent replication. The blood pressure data come from a few small studies with standardized extracts. Whether the results can be transferred to bulbs or other preparations is open.
  • Transfer from other garlic products. Studies on aged garlic extract (AGE) or garlic powder describe other products with a different composition.

Status, approval and legal

Black garlic is a processed food made from ordinary garlic and is sold as bulbs, paste or in capsules. The EU list of authorized health claims under Regulation 432/2012 contains no claim for garlic.

In its 2017 monograph, the EMA describes garlic powder and dry garlic extracts as traditional herbal medicinal products, as an adjunct for the prevention of atherosclerosis and for cold symptoms, solely on the basis of long-standing use. Black garlic is not specifically covered in it.

Safety

In the study by Jung 2014, no side effects were reported; the longest study ran for 6 months. In the BK-Score, safety stands at 3 out of 10 points; this axis measures how well safety has been studied, not how safe the substance is.

For garlic preparations in general, the EMA names a bleeding risk: caution with anticoagulants and platelet aggregation inhibitors, and discontinuation 7 days before surgery. They are contraindicated during HIV therapy with saquinavir and ritonavir, because drug levels may fall. As side effects, the EMA describes mouth and body odor, gastrointestinal complaints, allergic reactions, headaches and bleeding. Whether this applies to the same extent to black garlic has not been specifically studied. This text is information and does not replace medical advice.

Frequently asked questions about black garlic

What is the difference between black garlic and aged garlic extract?

Black garlic is produced when fresh garlic matures for an extended period at 60 to 90 degrees and high humidity. For aged garlic extract (AGE), raw garlic is stored in aqueous ethanol for more than ten months. The two products have a different composition and their own studies.

Does black garlic lower blood pressure?

In two randomized trials with standardized extracts, blood pressure fell slightly: diastolic by 5.85 mmHg after 6 weeks, and in treated hypertension by an additional 1.8 and 1.5 mmHg after 12 weeks. The studies are small, and cardiovascular events were not measured.

Does black garlic lower cholesterol?

In two placebo-controlled studies, LDL and total cholesterol did not change. In one of them, HDL rose and apolipoprotein B fell. A study without a placebo group found a rise in apolipoprotein A1.

Do the warnings for garlic also apply to black garlic?

The EMA warnings on bleeding risk, anticoagulants, surgery and saquinavir refer to garlic preparations in general. They have not been specifically tested for black garlic. Anyone taking anticoagulants or facing surgery should raise garlic preparations with a physician.

Is black garlic a medicine?

No, black garlic is a food. There is no entry for garlic in the EU list of authorized health claims. The EMA describes garlic powder and dry garlic extracts as traditional herbal medicinal products, but not black garlic.
