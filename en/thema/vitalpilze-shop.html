Medicinal Mushrooms & Supplements

Equipment

Reishi, Cordyceps, lion’s mane and chaga are among the most popular mushrooms in the biohacking scene. Some of them do have interesting studies to show. Anyone buying them should know, however, what is supported, what is not, and how to recognize good products.

In short

Medicinal mushrooms are edible and healing mushrooms from the Asian tradition that are sold as powder, extract or capsule. Individual effects are supported by small randomized studies, for example less fatigue with a Reishi extract and better memory test scores with lion’s mane in mild cognitive impairment. For the big promises, such as effects against cancer or on the immune system, reliable data are lacking. The catch often lies in the products themselves: food supplements are only notified, not approved, and content and quality vary widely.

What lies behind medicinal mushrooms

Healing mushrooms such as the lacquered bracket, better known as Reishi, have been used in Asia for more than 2,000 years. The active compounds are considered to be mainly beta-glucans, that is, polysaccharides from the cell wall, and in some species triterpenes. What is sold is either the dried fruiting body, that is, the visible mushroom, or the mycelium, the fungal network, which is often grown on grain.

This makes a difference. Mycelium grown on grain contains a lot of starch from the growth medium, which with some measurement methods can distort beta-glucan values. According to the Verbraucherzentrale (German consumer advice centre), the fruiting bodies of the caterpillar fungus and the lacquered bracket have been used as food supplements in the EU for more than 20 years, but mycelium powder has not.

The four best-known mushrooms

Reishi has the most studies, with mixed results: for fatigue there is a positive signal, while for blood sugar, blood lipids and blood pressure Cochrane analyses show no effect. Lion’s mane is advertised for concentration and memory because of possible nerve growth factors; the human data are small but encouraging. Cordyceps is considered an endurance mushroom; pooled sports studies show slight improvements, while a controlled study in cyclists found no effect. Chaga is popular as a tea and antioxidant; no controlled studies in humans were found.

How to recognize good products

Reputable products state the mushroom species with its Latin name, say whether fruiting body or mycelium was used, and name the extraction method. Good manufacturers provide a certificate of analysis that lists beta-glucan and alpha-glucan, that is, starch, separately, plus tests for heavy metals, pesticides and mycotoxins. Healing claims against cancer, rheumatism or depression should make you skeptical, because they are not permitted for food supplements. Advice in a shop is good if it also says what a mushroom is not supported for.

What is well supported

The strongest individual findings exist for two mushrooms. In a double-blind study in 132 patients with neurasthenia, a fatigue syndrome, a polysaccharide extract from Reishi clearly improved symptoms after 8 weeks: 51.6 percent were more than minimally improved, compared with 24.6 percent on placebo. In a small Japanese study in people with mild cognitive impairment, lion’s mane improved test scores over 16 weeks; after stopping, they fell again.

For Cordyceps, a 2025 review of 14 randomized studies in 528 athletes found slightly better pooled endurance values. All three findings are real, but each rests on a narrow base.

What the studies show

Reishi for fatigue, 2005

Tang and colleagues gave 132 patients with neurasthenia a Reishi polysaccharide extract or placebo for 8 weeks. Severity and fatigue fell significantly more under the extract; 51.6 versus 24.6 percent were more than minimally improved. The extract was well tolerated.

Lion’s mane in mild cognitive impairment, 2009

Mori and colleagues divided 30 Japanese people aged 50 to 80 into two groups. After 8, 12 and 16 weeks, scores in the cognitive test under lion’s mane were significantly above placebo. 4 weeks after stopping, they fell markedly again, so the effect lasted only while intake continued.

Reishi for cardiovascular risk factors, Cochrane 2015

Klupp and colleagues found 5 studies with 398 participants. Reishi lowered neither long-term blood sugar (HbA1c) nor blood lipids or blood pressure to a statistically or clinically meaningful degree.

Where the data stop

The big promises are not supported. A 2016 Cochrane analysis found only 5 randomized studies on Reishi in cancer, and none of them recorded long-term survival. A 2025 systematic review on lion’s mane rests on 5 randomized studies, plus pilot and laboratory studies. Many studies come from Asia, are small and each uses a different preparation, so the results can hardly be transferred to any product off the shelf. A study in 22 cyclists found no effect of 5 weeks of Cordyceps on endurance or oxygen uptake.

Then there is the question of quality. According to the Verbraucherzentrale, medicinal mushroom products often do not contain the stated substances or amounts, some were contaminated with aflatoxins, and with the caterpillar fungus the identity of the material is fundamentally questionable. For chaga there are no controlled studies in humans, and for sleep there are no reliable human data for any of the mushrooms.

Status, approval and legal

In Germany, medicinal mushrooms are sold as food supplements. For this, a notification to the Federal Office of Consumer Protection and Food Safety (BVL) is sufficient; there is no approval or efficacy testing. No medicinal mushroom is approved as a medicine. In 2015, an expert commission of BVL and BfArM stated that products made from caterpillar fungus, turkey tail and lacquered bracket that are advertised with effects against cancer, rheumatism, impotence or depression are to be regarded as medicines and would then require approval. Healing claims are not permitted for food supplements.

Safety

Most medicinal mushrooms are well tolerated; stomach complaints, headaches and allergic reactions are possible. According to the Verbraucherzentrale, Reishi can lead to dangerous hypoglycemia in people with and without diabetes. Chaga is rich in oxalate; a 2022 case report describes a 69-year-old man who developed acute kidney failure after 3 months of chaga powder. Beta-glucan extracts from mushrooms should not be combined with anti-inflammatory medicines such as cortisone. Anyone taking blood sugar or blood-thinning medication, who has kidney disease or a weakened immune system, or who is pregnant or breastfeeding, should consult a physician beforehand.

Frequently asked questions about medicinal mushrooms & supplements

What are medicinal mushrooms?

Edible and healing mushrooms from the Asian tradition such as Reishi, Cordyceps, lion’s mane or chaga. They are sold as powder, extract or capsule. The main active compounds are considered to be beta-glucans and, in some species, triterpenes.

Do medicinal mushrooms really work?

Individual effects are supported in small studies, for example less fatigue with a Reishi extract and better memory test scores with lion’s mane. Reliable data on effects against cancer or on the immune system are lacking. Many studies are small and each uses a different preparation.

Which is better, fruiting body or mycelium?

The fruiting body is what is used traditionally and has long been used in the EU. Mycelium is often grown on grain and can contain a lot of starch, which can distort beta-glucan values. A good product says what is in it and backs this up with an analysis.

Is chaga dangerous?

Chaga contains a lot of oxalate. A case report describes acute kidney failure after months of taking chaga powder. Anyone with kidney disease or kidney stones should avoid it or consult a physician beforehand.

Are medicinal mushrooms medicines?

No, no medicinal mushroom is approved as a medicine in Germany. They are sold as food supplements, which only have to be notified. If they are advertised with healing effects, they count as medicines according to an expert commission of BVL and BfArM.

How do I recognize a good medicinal mushroom product?

By the Latin name of the mushroom species, by the statement of fruiting body or mycelium and by a certificate of analysis covering beta-glucan, starch and contaminant testing. Healing claims against diseases are a warning sign.
