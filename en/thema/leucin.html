Leucine

Amino acid · L-leucine, Leu, branched-chain amino acid, BCAA, HMB (metabolite)

Leucine is the amino acid that gives muscle the signal to build. No other protein building block has been studied as thoroughly as a trigger of muscle protein synthesis. Whether leucine as a single powder brings more muscle, however, is a different question from whether it kick-starts building.

In short

Leucine is an essential branched-chain amino acid and switches on muscle protein synthesis via the mTORC1 signaling pathway; this is well supported in humans by tracer studies. Especially in older adults, a higher proportion of leucine can raise a dampened building response again, and a small portion of protein with added leucine acts almost like a large one in the short term. Taken over months, however, isolated leucine brings neither more muscle mass nor more strength in the studies; measurable effects are shown mainly by combinations with protein and vitamin D. The BfR gives 4.0 g of isolated leucine per day in addition to food as a guidance value.

What it is

Together with isoleucine and valine, leucine belongs to the branched-chain amino acids, BCAA for short. The body cannot produce it itself; it has to come from food. The WHO sets the requirement for adults at 39 mg per kilogram of body weight per day. In fact, most people eat considerably more: in the US, the mean intake is 6.1 g of leucine per day, in the UK the median is 108 mg per kilogram. Leucine is found in all protein-containing foods, in meat, fish, dairy products and legumes. Whey protein is particularly rich in it; 25 g of whey provide 3.0 g of leucine.

As a preparation, leucine is available as a single powder, in BCAA blends, in blends of essential amino acids and in fortified oral nutritional supplements for older people. A close relative is HMB, a metabolite of leucine that the body forms itself and that is also sold as a supplement. How leucine fits into the overall picture of amino acids is described in more detail in the entry on amino acids.

How it is supposed to work

Leucine is not only a building block but also a signal. When its level rises after a meal, it activates mTORC1 in the muscle cell, a central switch for growth. This releases protein production. The BfR describes leucine as a strong stimulator of mTOR, and among the three branched-chain amino acids it is considered the most important for metabolism. For the actual building, however, the muscle then needs all the essential amino acids. Leucine is, so to speak, the ignition key; the building material has to come from the rest of the protein.

With age, muscle responds more weakly to the same amount of protein; experts speak of anabolic resistance. The idea behind leucine supplements is to amplify this signal so that even a smaller meal starts muscle building. HMB is additionally supposed to slow muscle breakdown.

What users are looking for

Strength athletes take leucine to upgrade a meal or a shake anabolically, especially with plant-based food with a lower leucine density. Older people and their relatives are looking for a simple remedy against age-related muscle loss. How you feel after training is a poor yardstick here. Anyone testing it is best off judging it by strength values and muscle mass over months, not by how they feel after a week.

What is well supported

The core is solid: leucine kick-starts muscle protein synthesis in humans. In a tracer study, 3.42 g of leucine increased synthesis by 110 %, and the mTOR signaling chain remained active longer than after HMB. A meta-analysis of 9 randomized studies in older people confirms the rise in the synthesis rate with a standardized difference of 1.08. In older adults, one study also showed that a small amount of amino acids with 26 % leucine no longer raised synthesis, but the same amount with 41 % leucine did.

Leucine also works as an amplifier. In 40 young men after resistance training, 6.25 g of whey, topped up with leucine to 5.0 g, acted almost as strongly as 25 g of whey over several hours. And in older adults with sarcopenia, leucine-rich protein preparations improve strength: a meta-analysis of 6 studies with 699 participants found a clear effect, and in a larger analysis, combinations with vitamin D increased grip strength by 2.17 kg. In the PROVIDE study with 380 sarcopenic older adults, an oral nutritional supplement of whey protein, leucine and vitamin D over 13 weeks brought 0.17 kg more muscle mass and a faster chair-stand test.

What the studies show

Meta-analysis of 17 studies in older adults

The analysis comprised 17 randomized studies with 1,418 older people. Isolated leucine changed neither fat-free mass nor grip strength nor leg press strength. Combinations with vitamin D and other nutrients, by contrast, improved grip strength by 2.17 kg and walking speed by 0.03 m per second.

Three months of leucine in healthy older men

30 men aged around 71 took 2.5 g of leucine or placebo with each main meal, 7.5 g per day in total, for 3 months. Muscle mass by CT and DXA, strength, insulin sensitivity and blood lipids did not change in either group. The study is considered the most important evidence that the acute synthesis effect does not automatically lead to more muscle.

PROVIDE: oral nutritional supplement in sarcopenia

380 older people with sarcopenia drank a preparation of whey protein, leucine and vitamin D or a control product with the same number of calories twice daily for 13 weeks. The primary outcomes, grip strength and a performance test, improved equally in both groups. On the secondary outcomes, the active group was ahead: 0.17 kg more muscle mass in arms and legs and a chair-stand test that was 1.01 seconds faster. What share is attributable to leucine cannot be separated out.

Where the data stop

The most important catch: with isolated leucine, the short-term rise in the synthesis rate does not translate into more muscle. The same meta-analysis that shows the synthesis effect in older adults found no difference in fat-free mass and leg muscle mass. It did not help during immobilization either: 24 young and 24 older adults wore a leg cast for 3 days and took 5 g of leucine 3 times daily or placebo; synthesis rate and muscle cross-sectional area fell equally in both groups. A meta-analysis specifically in sarcopenia did find advantages, but rates the evidence as low and considers the benefit of leucine alone to be limited.

The picture is similar for its relative HMB. In young people doing resistance training, it brought no additional fat-free mass and no strength in a meta-analysis of 11 studies. In sarcopenia, only grip strength improved, by 1.26 kg. Long-term data on isolated leucine intake are lacking, and whether a permanently high leucine intake influences aging via mTOR has not been studied in humans.

Status, approval and legal

Leucine is a nutrient and is sold in Germany as an ingredient of dietary supplements and sports nutrition. In 2013, the BVL permitted, by general administrative order, capsules with L-leucine, L-valine and L-isoleucine from other EU states at 600 mg each per day. There is no statutory maximum amount. In 2019 the BfR derived guidance values for additional isolated intake: 4.0 g of leucine per day, 8.2 g for all three BCAA together. Because sufficient human data were lacking, these values are based on animal studies. Leucine and HMB are not on the WADA Prohibited List 2026.

Safety

In usual amounts, leucine is a normal component of food; 7.5 g per day over 3 months changed neither insulin sensitivity nor blood lipids in older men. With very high single amounts, the ammonia level in the blood rises: in acute studies it was above normal values in young men from over 500 mg per kilogram per day, and in older adults from 550 mg per kilogram. The BfR advises children, adolescents, pregnant and breastfeeding women to avoid relevant amounts of isolated BCAA, and people with impaired kidney function or on a low-protein diet to consult a physician. It also recommends taking the branched-chain amino acids combined rather than individually. In the rare maple syrup urine disease, the breakdown of leucine is impaired; additional leucine there belongs only in a physician’s hands.

Frequently asked questions about leucine

Does leucine alone build muscle?

Not in studies lasting months. Isolated leucine does increase muscle protein synthesis in the short term, but in a meta-analysis of 17 studies, more muscle mass or strength appeared only with combinations with protein, vitamin D or other nutrients. Sufficient protein and training remain decisive.

What is the difference between leucine and BCAA?

BCAA is the umbrella term for the three branched-chain amino acids leucine, isoleucine and valine. Among them, leucine is the strongest signal for muscle building. The BfR recommends taking the three combined rather than individually and has given a separate guidance limit for each.

How much leucine is in whey?

In the best-known study on this, 25 g of whey provided 3.0 g of leucine. Just 6.25 g of whey, topped up with leucine to 5.0 g, had almost as strong a short-term effect on muscle protein synthesis. In the long term, this does not replace a sufficient total protein intake.

Is HMB better than leucine?

HMB is a breakdown product of leucine and in tracer studies additionally slows muscle breakdown, but leucine itself increases synthesis more strongly. In young people who train, HMB brought no additional muscle mass or strength in a meta-analysis. In older people with sarcopenia, grip strength improved slightly.

When does leucine become too much?

The BfR gives 4.0 g of isolated leucine per day in addition to food as a guidance value for adults. In acute studies, the ammonia level rose above normal values only at very high amounts. The value does not apply to children, pregnant and breastfeeding women, or people with kidney disease.

Does leucine help older people against muscle loss?

In older adults, a higher proportion of leucine raises the dampened building response to protein. Leucine-rich protein and vitamin D preparations improved strength and in part muscle mass in sarcopenia, whereas isolated leucine alone over three months did not. The combination of enough protein, strength training and good nutrient status does the most.
