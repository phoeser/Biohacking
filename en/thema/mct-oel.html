MCT oil

Fatty acid · Medium-chain triglycerides

MCT oil consists of medium-chain fats that the body processes differently from normal dietary fat: they go directly to the liver and are partly converted there into ketones. This makes MCT the simplest route into mild ketosis without completely changing your diet. The most interesting data concern memory problems in old age.

In short

MCT oil reliably raises ketone bodies in the blood, most strongly pure caprylic acid C8. In a 6-month study in people with mild cognitive impairment, several memory and language tests improved, and ketone uptake in the brain rose measurably. People who replace normal fat with MCT lose some weight in studies, on average 0.51 kilograms. For better endurance performance, by contrast, there is no evidence, and in healthy adults the cognition data are small and contradictory. The typical catch is the gut: at 30 grams a day, 75 percent of participants in one study developed diarrhea.

What it is

MCT stands for medium-chain triglycerides, that is, fats made from fatty acids with 6 to 10 carbon atoms. Commercially available are mainly caprylic acid (C8) and capric acid (C10). Classic MCT oil contains both, in one study in a ratio of 55 to 35 percent; pure C8 oil consists of more than 95 percent caprylic acid.

Coconut oil itself is not MCT oil. In a comparative study it contained only 3 percent C8 and 5 percent C10, and its ketone peak reached only 25 percent of that of pure C8.

How it works

Normal long-chain fats are distributed via the lymphatic system and need a transporter to be burned in the mitochondria. Medium-chain fatty acids, by contrast, go via the portal vein directly to the liver and are broken down there quickly. What the liver does not need immediately is turned into ketone bodies such as beta-hydroxybutyrate, which the brain can use as fuel.

This pathway is well measured in humans. In a crossover study with 9 adults, C8 was on average 295 micromoles per liter above control over the day, and the ketone area under the curve was 813 to 870 percent higher. In an 8-hour test with two doses of 10 grams of MCT, ketones rose by 19 percent, blood glucose fell by 12 percent, and insulin remained unchanged.

Interesting for anyone working with intermittent fasting: in the same 8-hour test, an early meal pattern with breakfast but without lunch amplified the ketone effect of MCT more than a late meal pattern without breakfast. The age of the participants and a low-carbohydrate meal, by contrast, did not change the ketone response. MCT can therefore metabolically complement a short fasting phase without the diet having to be fully ketogenic.

The idea behind it: in Alzheimer’s disease, glucose utilization in the brain falls early and in limited regions. Ketones are the brain’s alternative fuel and could bypass this energy deficit. That additional MCT actually brings more ketones into the brain has been shown with PET imaging.

Where MCT has been used for decades

In medicine, MCT is not a new trend. As early as 1967 it was described for the treatment of fat malabsorption, because it is absorbed without the usual fat digestion. In chylothorax, a lymphatic congestion in the chest cavity, an MCT diet is considered the first therapeutic measure. And the MCT ketogenic diet is an established variant of the ketogenic diet in hard-to-treat epilepsy; a 2026 review considers it similarly effective to the classic form, even though equivalence has not been proven.

What is well supported

Mild ketosis is well supported, and in mild cognitive impairment there are serious clinical data. A meta-analysis of 12 papers with 422 participants found higher ketone levels in MCI and Alzheimer’s disease and an improvement on a combined cognition measure (SMD -0.289). A second meta-analysis saw an effect on general cognition (SMD 0.64), but not on individual domains such as memory or attention. On weight, a meta-analysis of 13 randomized studies with 749 participants showed that MCT instead of long-chain fats lowers weight by 0.51 kilograms and waist circumference by 1.46 centimeters more. Another analysis found that people eat less after MCT, even though they do not feel fuller.

What the studies show

BENEFIC: 6 months in mild cognitive impairment

People with mild cognitive impairment drank a beverage with 15 grams of MCT twice daily for 6 months (39 participants) or a calorie-matched placebo (44 participants). In the MCT group, free and cued recall, verbal fluency, naming and the number of errors in the Trail Making Test improved significantly. Imaging showed 2.5- to 3.2-fold higher ketone uptake in 9 white matter tracts, and the improvement in processing speed was linked to it.

AC-1202 in Alzheimer’s dementia

152 patients with mild to moderate Alzheimer’s dementia received an MCT preparation or placebo for 90 days. On day 45, the MCT group scored 1.9 points better on the ADAS-Cog cognition test. In patients without the risk gene APOE4 the advantage was larger, 4.77 points on day 45 and 3.36 points on day 90. Side effects, mainly in the gastrointestinal tract, occurred more frequently under MCT.

Two studies in healthy people, two results

In 30 young healthy people, 12 or 18 grams of MCT per day improved performance on Trail Making and digit span after 2 to 3 weeks, but not attention and reaction time. In 80 healthy older people, by contrast, 30 grams per day over 14 days brought no effect at all on cognition or brain activity, even though ketones rose.

Where the data stop

The positive cognition data come almost exclusively from people with mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer’s disease. The studies are small and short, the authors of the largest meta-analysis see a relevant risk of bias, and the effect appears to be smaller in carriers of APOE4. Whether MCT slows progression to dementia has not been studied. In healthy people, a small positive and a larger negative study stand side by side; that does not support a claim of more focus in everyday life. For sport, a systematic review concludes that MCT oil mostly does not improve performance and does not change substrate utilization; the resulting ketones are apparently hardly used as the main fuel during endurance exercise. The weight effect is small, and the meta-analysis found indications of commercial influence. For an effect against Candida there are only laboratory experiments, no human studies.

Status, approval and legal

In Germany, MCT oil is a food and is sold as an edible fat and dietary supplement; there is no maximum amount. In medicine, MCT fats are used as part of nutritional therapy in fat malabsorption, chylothorax and in the MCT ketogenic diet for epilepsy, there with medical and dietetic supervision. MCT oil is not an approved medicine. It is not on the WADA Prohibited List.

Safety

The most common side effects concern the gut. In a study with 30 grams per day, 75 percent of participants developed diarrhea and 11 percent dropped out because of side effects. A review on sport names 30 grams as a practical upper limit to keep complaints during and after exercise low; in the BENEFIC study, the same amount was split into two servings over 6 months. Almost all cardiovascular and inflammatory markers remained unchanged; only the inflammatory messenger IL-8 rose by 147 percent, and its significance is unclear. In blood lipids, MCT is less conspicuous than often feared: a meta-analysis found no effect on total, LDL and HDL cholesterol, but a small rise in triglycerides of 0.14 millimoles per liter. Compared with oils consisting mainly of unsaturated fatty acids, however, total and LDL cholesterol rose. MCT oil is pure fat and correspondingly energy-dense. The clinical literature lists ketosis, acidosis, that is, overacidification of the blood, and liver cirrhosis as contraindications. Anyone who has diabetes and takes insulin or blood glucose-lowering drugs should discuss a ketosis strategy with a physician beforehand.

Frequently asked questions about MCT oil

Is coconut oil as good as MCT oil?

No. In a comparative study, coconut oil contained only 3 percent C8 and 5 percent C10. Its ketone peak was 25 percent of that of pure C8 oil. For ketosis, concentrated MCT oil is considerably more effective.

Is C8 better than regular MCT oil?

For ketone production, yes. In a crossover study with 9 adults, pure C8 was the strongest test oil, clearly ahead of classic MCT oil with C8 and C10 and ahead of pure C10. Whether this also makes a clinical difference has not been studied.

Does MCT oil help with weight loss?

A little. People who replaced long-chain fats with MCT lost on average 0.51 kilograms more in a meta-analysis of 13 studies. Another analysis found that people eat less after MCT. As a sole weight-loss strategy, that is too little.

Does MCT oil improve concentration?

In people with mild cognitive impairment there are positive studies over 6 months. In healthy people the data are contradictory: a small study in younger people was positive, a larger one in older people showed no effect.

Why does MCT oil give me diarrhea?

That is the most common side effect. In a study with 30 grams per day it affected 75 percent of participants. In the BENEFIC study, the daily amount was split into two servings as a drink; a review on sport names 30 grams as a practical upper limit.

Does MCT oil do anything for endurance?

According to the current data, no. A systematic review mostly found no performance improvement and no change in fat burning. Ketones do rise, but apparently are hardly used as the main fuel during exercise.
