Phenylpiracetam (Phenotropil)

Racetam with stimulating effects, prescription-only in Russia, not approved in Germany/EU · fonturacetam, carphedon, Phenotropil

Phenylpiracetam is piracetam with an additional phenyl ring, and this small difference turns a rather restrained nootropic into a stimulating agent. In Russia, as Phenotropil, it is a prescription-only medicine, used among other things for exhaustion and after brain damage. Russian studies show clear improvements; a study against placebo was not found. It has been banned in sport since 1998.

What phenylpiracetam is

Phenylpiracetam, also called fonturacetam, was described in the Soviet Union in the early 1980s as a derivative of piracetam. In Russia it is a prescription-only medicine as Phenotropil, and now also as Actitropil. The Russian summary of product characteristics lists, among other indications, impaired brain function after vascular disease or injury, neurotic conditions, learning disorders, overweight and alcohol dependence.

In the nootropics scene it is considered the strongest of the racetams and is used for concentration, drive and performance, often compared with Noopept or as part of nootropic combinations. What is rated here is the state of knowledge.

How it works

Even the first description in 1983 showed stronger activation in animals than with piracetam, a counter-effect to sedation by diazepam, protection against memory loss and an anticonvulsant effect that piracetam lacks. At high doses, by contrast, it had a sedating effect.

The most important target was described by a research group from Riga: the S-form selectively inhibits the dopamine transporter and thus increases the availability of dopamine, similar to modafinil, without affecting noradrenaline or serotonin receptors. Phenylpiracetam is a mixture of two mirror-image forms; in mice, both increased locomotor activity and had an antidepressant effect, while only the R-form improved memory. According to the Russian summary of product characteristics, the active ingredient is completely absorbed after intake, and the half-life is 3 to 5 hours. The effect on the dopamine transporter has not been measured in humans.

What is well supported

  • Less exhaustion in Russian studies. A meta-analysis of 11 papers with 549 patients found a reduction in exhaustion on the MFI-20 scale of 16.3 points on average after one month (95 percent confidence interval 8.85 to 23.85), plus improvements in mood, sleep and cognitive performance (Devlikamova 2025).
  • Better recovery after stroke. In 400 stroke patients, neurological function and everyday functioning recovered significantly better in the phenylpiracetam group than in the comparison group (Kovalchuk 2010).
  • A clear site of action in animals. The S-form is a selective inhibitor of the dopamine transporter (Zvejniece 2017).
  • A regular medicine in Russia. Phenotropil is approved there as a prescription-only medicine; the registration was renewed without time limit in 2025.

What the studies show

Devlikamova 2025: the meta-analysis in asthenia

The authors pooled 11 Russian papers with 549 patients, mean age 45.9 years. In all studies, exhaustion fell clearly after one month, by an average of 16.3 points on the MFI-20 scale, p less than 0.0001. Side effects occurred in an average of 5.5 percent and disappeared within a week with continued treatment. What was analyzed was the change from baseline, not the difference from a placebo group.

Kovalchuk 2010: rehabilitation after stroke

400 patients with ischemic stroke were followed for one year; 200 received three additional treatment courses with phenylpiracetam during rehabilitation. On several scales for neurological function and everyday functioning, this group recovered significantly better, p less than 0.0001. Whether the groups were randomly assigned and the assessments blinded is not evident from the abstract.

Zvejniece 2011 and 2017: two mirror-image forms

The research group separated the two forms and tested them in mice and rats. Both increased locomotor activity and had an antidepressant effect in the swim test; only the R-form showed the memory-enhancing effect, although both reached the brain equally well. The S-form selectively inhibited the dopamine transporter and, in overweight rats and mice, reduced weight gain, fat mass, blood sugar and leptin over 8 to 12 weeks, without making the animals more restless.

Where the data stop

  • No comparison with placebo. A placebo-controlled, blinded study was not found. The meta-analysis evaluates before-and-after changes, in which time, attention and expectation play a part.
  • Healthy people. There are no controlled studies on concentration, memory or performance in healthy people. The data concern patients with exhaustion or after brain damage.
  • Dependence and long-term use. Phenylpiracetam inhibits the dopamine transporter like a stimulant. How often tolerance or misuse occurs has not been studied.
  • Products outside Russia. European official medicines control laboratories seized phenylpiracetam in large quantities of raw material; what is sold online is not a tested medicine (Vanhee 2025). More on this under gray market.

Status, approval and legal

In Russia, phenylpiracetam is a prescription-only medicine as Phenotropil. In Germany and the EU it is not approved, neither as a medicine nor as a dietary supplement; European official medicines control laboratories have nevertheless seized it, including in large quantities of raw material. It is listed neither in the German Narcotics Act (BtMG) nor in the German Prescription Drug Ordinance. An official classification under the German New Psychoactive Substances Act (NpSG), which covers entire groups of substances, was not found.

In sport, fonturacetam was the first nootropic to be banned, in 1998. Today it is on the WADA Prohibited List 2026 as a non-specified stimulant (S6.A) and is prohibited in competition. The German Anti-Doping Act therefore prohibits its manufacture, trade, supply and prescription for doping purposes in sport; it is not listed in the annex of the Act that prohibits the acquisition and possession of non-small quantities.

Safety

In the Russian studies on exhaustion, side effects occurred in an average of 5.5 percent and subsided within a week. The Russian summary of product characteristics lists insomnia, especially when taken in the afternoon, psychomotor agitation and, in the first days, increased blood pressure, skin flushing and a feeling of warmth. Contraindications are age under 18, pregnancy and breastfeeding.

Data on use over months, on interactions with other stimulating agents and on dependence are missing. For people with high blood pressure or cardiovascular disease, the increase in blood pressure mentioned in the summary of product characteristics is particularly relevant.

Frequently asked questions about phenylpiracetam (Phenotropil)

What is phenylpiracetam?

A derivative of piracetam with an additional phenyl ring, also called fonturacetam. In Russia, as Phenotropil, it is a prescription-only medicine. It is considerably more stimulating than piracetam; in animals, the S-form inhibits the dopamine transporter.

Does phenylpiracetam work better than piracetam?

In animals it is more strongly activating and additionally anticonvulsant. A study directly comparing the two agents in humans was not found. The clinical data on phenylpiracetam come from Russian studies in exhaustion and after stroke.

Does phenylpiracetam help healthy people concentrate?

That has not been studied. The studies concern patients with states of exhaustion or after brain damage. There are no controlled studies in healthy people on concentration, memory or performance.

Is phenylpiracetam banned in sport?

Yes. As fonturacetam, it is on the WADA Prohibited List 2026 among the non-specified stimulants (S6.A) and is prohibited in competition. In 1998 it was the first nootropic to be banned.

What side effects does phenylpiracetam have?

The Russian summary of product characteristics lists insomnia, psychomotor agitation and, in the first days, increased blood pressure, skin flushing and a feeling of warmth. In the studies on exhaustion, side effects occurred in an average of 5.5 percent. Long-term data and data on dependence are missing.

Is phenylpiracetam legal in Germany?

In Germany it is approved neither as a medicine nor as a dietary supplement and is not listed in the Narcotics Act. The Anti-Doping Act prohibits its manufacture, trade and supply for doping purposes in sport.
