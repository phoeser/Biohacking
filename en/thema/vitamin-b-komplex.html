Vitamin B Complex

Vitamin · B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B7, B9, B12

A B complex bundles the eight B vitamins that work closely together in metabolism: thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, pantothenic acid, B6, biotin, folate and B12. Their functions are fully understood. The interesting question is what a supplement does when you are not deficient, and on that there are unusually large studies.

In short

B vitamins are indispensable helpers for energy metabolism, nerves and blood formation, and anyone who belongs to a risk group for deficiency clearly benefits. In people who are well supplied, the studies show a mixed picture: B vitamins reliably lower homocysteine, slightly reduce strokes and, in one study, slowed brain shrinkage in older people with memory problems. Heart attacks, mortality and cognitive aging did not change in large analyses. For stress there is a small effect. The most important catch is vitamin B6: in 2023, EFSA lowered the upper limit to 12 mg a day.

What it is

The B vitamins are water-soluble. Most act as coenzymes, that is, as tools of enzymes: thiamine, riboflavin, niacin and pantothenic acid in the breakdown of carbohydrates and fats, B6 in protein metabolism, folate and B12 in cell division and blood formation.

Folate, B12 and B6 share a special task: they break down homocysteine, an intermediate product of protein metabolism. High homocysteine is considered a risk factor for cardiovascular disease and is associated with more dementia in observational studies. This gave rise to the hope that these diseases could be prevented with B vitamins. Exactly that was tested in large studies.

A B complex supplement usually contains all eight vitamins in one capsule. The amounts vary widely. An EFSA market analysis of 2,210 supplements containing vitamin B6 found a median of 1.4 mg per serving; half were between 1.01 and 2.0 mg, only 1.4 percent above 20 mg.

Who really benefits

For some groups, the question of benefit is easy to answer. Between 3 and 43 percent of older people have a B12 deficiency, particularly with chronic inflammation of the stomach lining, atrophic gastritis. Anyone on a vegan diet gets B12 practically only from supplements or fortified foods. The diabetes drug metformin and acid blockers reduce B12 absorption, as do gastrointestinal surgery, celiac disease and Crohn’s disease.

For everyone who could become pregnant, there is a recommendation of 400 µg of folic acid per day in addition to food, because it lowers the risk of neural tube defects in the child. According to the US health agency CDC, the gene variant MTHFR C677T does not change this: folic acid is recommended for carriers too. Some of them might additionally benefit from the active form 5-MTHF; there is no evidence of a general advantage of methylated forms.

What users are looking for

Most people reach for a B complex against tiredness, in stressful phases, after alcohol or as nerve protection. Many report feeling more alert and resilient, others notice no difference. That fits the study situation. Those who were poorly supplied beforehand are most likely to feel something; with a good starting point, little is to be expected. If you notice persistent tiredness, a blood test including B12 and folate is the more sensible first step than a supplement on suspicion.

What is well supported

The best-supported effect is the lowering of homocysteine. In 11 large trials with a total of 22,000 participants, the level fell by 26 to 28 percent with B vitamins. The Cochrane review of 15 randomized trials with 71,422 people also found a small but significant decrease in strokes: 4.3 versus 5.1 percent (relative risk 0.90), with high-quality evidence.

In the brain, the VITACOG trial showed an impressive finding. In older people with mild cognitive impairment, the brain shrank by 0.76 percent per year over 2 years with B6, B12 and folic acid, compared with 1.08 percent on placebo. Those who started with a homocysteine above 13 µmol/l even had 53 percent less brain shrinkage. A meta-analysis with 2,015 participants also found a small effect of B complex supplements on stress (SMD 0.23), and the EU has authorized the claim for several B vitamins that they contribute to the reduction of tiredness.

What the studies show

Cochrane review on heart and stroke 2017

15 randomized trials with 71,422 participants, followed for 1 to 7.3 years, tested B6, B12 and folic acid against placebo. Heart attacks occurred in 7.1 versus 6.0 percent (relative risk 1.02), and 11.7 versus 12.3 percent died (1.01), both without a difference. Strokes were somewhat less frequent with the vitamins. The authors consider further trials on this question unlikely to be fruitful; the data are stable.

VITACOG: brain shrinkage in memory problems 2010

271 people over 70 with mild cognitive impairment took 0.8 mg of folic acid, 0.5 mg of B12 and 20 mg of B6 daily or placebo for 24 months. 168 completed both MRI measurements. Brain atrophy, the primary endpoint, was clearly slower with the vitamins, especially with high homocysteine. The B6 amount in this trial is above today’s EFSA upper limit of 12 mg.

Cognitive aging in 11 large trials 2014

An international analysis used the individual data of 22,000 participants from 11 trials. Although homocysteine fell by about a quarter, neither memory nor processing speed nor global cognitive performance changed. An effect of more than one month of brain aging gained per year of treatment could be ruled out.

B vitamins and mood 2019

The meta-analysis included 16 randomized trials with 2,015 participants that gave at least 3 B vitamins for at least 4 weeks. 11 of 18 papers reported a positive effect. Pooled, stress improved significantly, depressive symptoms narrowly missed significance (p = 0.07) and anxiety did not improve at all. People with poor supply or low mood benefited most.

Where the data stop

The big plan to prevent heart attacks and dementia with B vitamins has not worked out in well-supplied people. Homocysteine falls reliably, but heart attacks and mortality do not change. On cognitive aging, the largest analysis with 22,000 people found no effect. Another meta-analysis of 95 studies did see an advantage, but it amounted to only 0.14 points on the Mini-Mental test and appeared only with treatments lasting over 12 months. The often-cited link between folate and less dementia (hazard ratio 0.61) comes from observational studies on diet, not from trials with supplements.

For energy and nerves, the authorized health claims describe the function of a nutrient, not an additional benefit when supply is adequate. That stress or exercise substantially increases requirements could not be demonstrated with robust figures. For hair, skin and nails, the benefit of biotin without deficiency rests only on case reports and small studies.

Status, approval and legal

In Germany, B complex supplements are food supplements; B12 and folic acid are also available as medicines against deficiency states. Binding EU maximum levels for vitamins in food supplements do not yet exist. In 2024, the BfR proposed a maximum level for vitamin B6 of 0.9 mg per daily dose after EFSA had lowered the tolerable upper limit for adults from 25 to 12 mg in 2023. Both assessments continue to rely on a case-control study from 1987 in which about 100 mg a day were taken on average. Authorized claims include that riboflavin, niacin, pantothenic acid, B6, folate and B12 contribute to the reduction of tiredness and that biotin contributes to the maintenance of normal hair and skin. B vitamins are not relevant to doping.

Safety

The most important risk is vitamin B6. High long-term doses can damage nerves in the hands and feet, with tingling and numbness. EFSA sets the upper limit for adults at 12 mg per day, for children at 3.2 to 10.7 mg depending on age. Nobody reaches this through a normal diet; high-dose supplements or the combination of several products are what matter here. Folic acid should not exceed 1,000 µg per day, because it can mask the anemia of a B12 deficiency while nerve damage continues. Niacin as nicotinic acid often triggers a harmless but unpleasant flush from 30 to 50 mg; nicotinamide does not. Biotin distorts laboratory tests: as little as 10 mg interfered with thyroid values, and in 2019 the FDA warned of false heart attack tests. Let them know before blood draws. For B12 there is no upper limit because of its low toxicity.

Frequently asked questions about vitamin B complex

Does a vitamin B complex give you more energy?

People who are poorly supplied, for example with a B12 deficiency, often notice a clear improvement. With good supply, studies show small effects at most, most likely on perceived stress. With persistent tiredness, a blood test is more sensible than a supplement on suspicion.

How much vitamin B6 is too much?

In 2023, EFSA lowered the tolerable upper limit for adults from 25 to 12 mg per day. The BfR proposes at most 0.9 mg per daily dose for food supplements. Most supplements are below this, some high-dose ones well above it.

Do B vitamins protect against dementia?

In older people with memory problems, B6, B12 and folic acid slowed brain shrinkage in one study, especially with high homocysteine. In the largest analysis with 22,000 people, however, cognitive aging did not change. Protection against dementia has not been demonstrated.

Do I need methylated B vitamins because of MTHFR?

The US health agency CDC recommends normal folic acid for carriers of the common MTHFR variant too. Some might additionally benefit from the active form. The data do not show a general advantage of methylated forms in healthy people.

Who should supplement B12?

Above all people on a vegan diet, older people with atrophic gastritis and everyone taking metformin or acid blockers. Deficiency is also more common after gastrointestinal surgery and with celiac disease or Crohn’s disease. There is no upper limit for B12.

Why should I mention biotin before a blood draw?

Biotin interferes with some laboratory tests. Even a single dose of 10 mg distorted thyroid values, and falsely low heart attack values have contributed to a death. So let them know beforehand or pause after consulting a physician.
