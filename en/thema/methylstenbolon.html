Methylstenbolone

Designer steroid, 17α-methylated anabolic agent; never approved as a medicine, sold as a “prohormone” and “dietary supplement” · Methylstenbolone, MSTEN, 2α,17α-dimethyl-17β-hydroxy-5α-androst-1-en-3-one, contained for example in “Super DMZ Rx 2.0”

Methylstenbolone is an anabolic agent from the family of designer steroids: never developed as a medicine, but sold as a “prohormone” or dietary supplement for muscle building. Chemically it is a close relative of methasterone (Superdrol). The only thing studied in humans is how long the substance remains detectable in urine – on its effects there is no study, on its safety there are case reports.

In short

Methylstenbolone was marketed as a legal-sounding alternative to anabolic steroids, often in combination with dimethazine. How strongly it builds muscle has never been measured in humans; the only human studies come from doping laboratories and describe its excretion, with metabolites detectable up to 29 days after a single dose. What is documented is a case of jaundice due to bile stasis in a young man who had taken such a product according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and a cluster of complaints in New York in which vitamin products contained methylstenbolone and related steroids. In Germany the substance is listed by name in the annex of the Anti-Doping Act; in sport it is banned at all times.

What methylstenbolone is and how it works

Methylstenbolone has the chemical name 2α,17α-dimethyl-17β-hydroxy-5α-androst-1-en-3-one. It has the same two methyl groups as methasterone, plus a double bond between carbon 1 and 2. It was never developed as a medicine; a review lists it as a designer steroid that was never approved for medical use and is used as an illegal dietary supplement (Tauchen 2021). In 2013 a doping laboratory described a product bought online that contained methylstenbolone, as stated on the label (Cavalcanti 2013).

How it works can only be inferred from its structure: steroids of this kind bind to the androgen receptor, have anabolic and masculinizing effects and suppress the body’s own hormone production. None of these effects has been measured for methylstenbolone itself. Products that contained methasterone and dimethazine in addition to methylstenbolone had a strongly androgenic effect in cell experiments, lasting several days (Tran 2023). The methyl group in the 17α position makes the substance effective as a capsule and slows its breakdown in the liver. For an overview of the substance group, see Anabolic steroids (AAS).

What is well supported

It is established that methylstenbolone was contained in products sold as dietary supplements – sometimes as declared (Cavalcanti 2013, Agbenyefia 2014), sometimes together with methasterone (Geldof 2014) or hidden in vitamin products (Tran 2023). Its excretion in humans, the basis for detection in doping controls, has also been well measured. Nothing more is established in humans.

What the studies show

Excretion in 4 volunteers

Cavalcanti 2013 gave 4 volunteers a single dose and analyzed their urine. Unchanged methylstenbolone was detectable for only about 45 hours, two metabolites for more than a week. The study makes no statement about efficacy.

Long-term detection with a labeled substance

Piper 2019 used methylstenbolone labeled with heavy hydrogen so that even small amounts could be reliably attributed. After a single dose, urine was collected for 29 days. Up to 40 metabolites were found; three of them were still detectable on the last day of the study. The study served to extend the detection window in doping controls.

Case report: jaundice after 30 days

Agbenyefia 2014 describes a previously healthy 26-year-old man who had taken the product “Super DMZ Rx 2.0”, containing methylstenbolone and dimethazine, for 30 days according to the manufacturer’s instructions and developed jaundice due to bile stasis. The authors call it the first reported case involving this product.

Complaints caused by contaminated vitamin products

In New York in 2012 and 2013, around 16 people reported nonspecific complaints such as fatigue, hair loss and muscle pain. What they had in common was a vitamin B and mineral product from the same supplier. The analysis found methasterone, dimethazine and methylstenbolone. One patient was hospitalized with liver damage, and a child showed marked virilization (Tran 2023). What share of the complaints was due to methylstenbolone cannot be separated out.

Where the data stop

There is no study comparing methylstenbolone with placebo, neither on muscles and strength nor on the liver, blood lipids or hormones. Experts on designer steroids describe exactly this pattern: little is known about the effects and metabolism of unapproved steroids because clinical studies are lacking (Joseph and Parr 2015).

The case reports are limited as well. In both documented cases other steroids were involved, dimethazine or methasterone, and the amounts in the products were not controlled. How common liver damage is under methylstenbolone cannot be read from them.

Status, approval and legal

Methylstenbolone is not approved as a medicine in Germany, the EU or the US. In Germany it is listed by name in the annex of the Anti-Doping Act: manufacturing, trading and placing it on the market for doping purposes are prohibited, as are acquiring, possessing and bringing it into the country in non-small quantities for doping in sport (§ 2, punishable under § 4). It is not listed by name on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List, but falls under S1.1 as a substance with a similar chemical structure and is therefore banned at all times.

Safety

In humans, only individual cases are documented: jaundice due to bile stasis after a product containing methylstenbolone and dimethazine (Agbenyefia 2014), and a cluster of complaints with one case of liver damage and virilization in a child after contaminated vitamin products (Tran 2023). A review of designer steroids in supplements lists liver damage, bile stasis, kidney failure, testicular underfunction, breast growth in men and infertility for the group; some of this is reversible, but with aggressive use damage can persist (Rahnema 2015). That a product labeled methylstenbolone also contained methasterone (Geldof 2014) points to a further risk: with such goods, what is actually inside is an open question. For the class risks of anabolic steroids, see Anabolic steroids (AAS).

Frequently asked questions about methylstenbolone

What is methylstenbolone?

A designer steroid, closely related to methasterone. It was never developed as a medicine and was sold as a “prohormone” or dietary supplement for muscle building.

Is the effect on muscles established?

No. There is no study on its effects in humans. The only thing studied is how long the substance and its metabolites remain detectable in urine.

Is methylstenbolone a prohormone?

Not in the strict sense. It is itself an anabolic steroid with a 17α-methyl group and does not first have to be converted in the body. The term “prohormone” was above all a sales label.

Which side effects are documented?

One case of jaundice due to bile stasis after 30 days of use and a cluster of complaints including liver damage caused by contaminated vitamin products. In both cases other steroids were involved.

Is methylstenbolone legal in Germany?

No. It is not an approved medicine, it is listed by name in the annex of the Anti-Doping Act, and it is banned in sport at all times.

How long is methylstenbolone detectable?

In one study, individual metabolites were still detectable in urine 29 days after a single dose.
