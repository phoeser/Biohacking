VNS analysis (HRV)

Equipment

A healthy heart does not beat like a metronome. The fine fluctuations between two beats reveal how the autonomic nervous system is working at the moment. Measuring this heart rate variability is one of the best windows into stress and recovery that you can get without a needle.

In short

A VNS analysis measures heart rate variability, HRV for short, that is, the variation in the time intervals between heartbeats, and uses it to draw conclusions about the autonomic nervous system. It is well supported that HRV drops under stress and that a low HRV goes along with a higher cardiovascular risk. As a tracking measurement for training, stress management or biofeedback it makes sense. The catch: single values depend heavily on age, sex and measurement conditions, and the popular display of a sympathetic-parasympathetic balance is methodologically disputed.

What is measured

The heart is constantly controlled by two opposing players. The sympathetic nervous system speeds it up, the parasympathetic nervous system, mainly via the vagus nerve, slows it down. Because the vagus acts very quickly, for instance with every breath, the interval between beats fluctuates continuously. High variability at rest is considered a sign of an adaptable nervous system.

Measurement is done with ECG electrodes or a chest strap, briefly at rest or as a long-term ECG. The data yield time-domain measures such as SDNN or RMSSD and frequency-domain measures, in which the fluctuations are broken down into low and high frequency bands. For such short-term measurements, the occupational medicine guideline recommends at least 5 minutes, after a rest period of at least five, ideally 15 minutes.

How to recognize a good measurement

A sound analysis takes place under defined conditions: same time of day, same position, rest beforehand, no coffee and no training immediately before. It compares with age- and sex-specific values and above all assesses the course over time in the same person. Caution is warranted when a diagnosis such as burnout is derived from a single measurement or a treatment package is sold directly on that basis.

What is well supported

First, it is well supported that HRV responds to stress. A 2018 meta-analysis and review of 37 publications found decreasing parasympathetic activity under stress in most studies. The link with heart health is also well supported: in 8 cohort studies with 21,988 people without known heart disease, a low HRV went along with a 32 to 45 percent higher risk of a first cardiovascular event.

The measurement becomes useful when you work with it. HRV biofeedback, in which you influence your own values through slow breathing, showed small to medium effects in 58 randomized studies, most clearly for anxiety and depression. And endurance training guided by daily HRV improves vagally mediated HRV more than a fixed training plan.

What the studies show

HRV and first cardiovascular event, meta-analysis 2013

Hillebrand and colleagues pooled 8 studies with 21,988 participants without known cardiovascular disease. Those with the lowest HRV more often had a first cardiovascular event; depending on the measure, the risk was 32 to 45 percent higher. These are observational data.

HRV biofeedback, meta-analysis 2020

Lehrer and colleagues analyzed 58 randomized controlled trials. Overall, HRV biofeedback had a small to medium effect, comparable to other effective methods. It was largest for anxiety, depression, anger and athletic performance, and smallest for sleep and quality of life.

HRV-guided training, meta-analysis 2021

Manresa-Rocamora and colleagues compared endurance training guided by daily HRV with fixed training plans. Vagally mediated HRV improved significantly more; for oxygen uptake and performance there were only small, non-significant advantages.

Where the data stop

HRV is a sensitive but not an unambiguous measure. The 2018 review states that there is no accepted standard for measuring stress, and the German occupational medicine guideline stresses that reference values depend on age and sex and that values should only be interpreted in the overall context. A single measurement therefore says little, the course over time says more.

Particularly disputed is the display of a balance between the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems, usually calculated from the ratio of low to high frequency. A frequently cited 2013 paper bears the title that this ratio does not accurately measure sympathovagal balance. In addition: that a low HRV goes along with cardiac risk does not mean that deliberately raising HRV lowers the risk. That has not been studied. For diagnosing burnout, no studies were found that establish an HRV measurement as a reliable tool.

Status, approval and legal

HRV measurement is an established procedure in cardiology, sleep medicine and occupational medicine; occupational medicine has its own guideline for it. As a stress or recovery analysis in prevention and coaching, it is not covered by statutory health insurance and is paid for privately. Many sports watches and chest straps now also measure HRV; accuracy depends on the device and the measurement conditions.

Safety

The measurement is non-invasive and carries no risk. Its informative value is limited with heart rhythm disorders such as atrial fibrillation or frequent extra beats, with pacemakers and under medications that affect heart rate, such as beta blockers. Breathing, body position, caffeine, alcohol and lack of sleep also shift the values. Anyone with abnormal values together with heart symptoms belongs in a cardiological work-up, not in a stress program.

Frequently asked questions about VNS analysis (HRV)

What is a VNS analysis?

A measurement of heart rate variability, that is, the variation in the intervals between heartbeats. It is used to infer how active the autonomic nervous system is, above all the calming vagus nerve.

What is a good HRV value?

That depends heavily on age, sex, fitness and measurement conditions; there is no universally valid ideal value. Your own course over time under the same conditions is more meaningful than a single value.

Can HRV measure stress?

It responds reliably to stress and typically drops when parasympathetic activity decreases. However, there is no accepted standard for measuring stress through HRV alone.

Can an HRV measurement detect burnout?

No, there are no reliable studies for that. HRV can indicate strain and poor recovery, but it does not replace a diagnosis.

How can I improve my HRV?

The evidence supports regular endurance training, ideally guided by your daily form, and HRV biofeedback with slow breathing, which showed small to medium effects in 58 randomized studies.

Are HRV values from sports watches reliable?

For the trend under the same conditions they are often usable, for example at night or right after waking up. Accuracy depends on the device; for medical questions an ECG-based measurement is better.
