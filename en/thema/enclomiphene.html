Enclomiphene

Selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), TRT alternative · Enclomiphene citrate, isomer of clomiphene

Enclomiphene is the tablet meant to raise the body’s own testosterone instead of replacing it from outside. The mechanism is correct and has been demonstrated in phase 3 trials in humans. Even so, the substance is still not approved as a medicine anywhere, and the reason for this is instructive.

In brief

Enclomiphene is the anti-estrogenic half of clomiphene, known for decades, and acts as a selective estrogen receptor modulator. It blocks the feedback loop in the brain, so the control hormones LH and FSH rise and the testes produce more of their own testosterone – unlike classic testosterone therapy, sperm concentration is preserved; data on pregnancy rates are lacking. This fertility advantage is large and well supported: under enclomiphene, 2 to 5 percent of men fell below the critical sperm concentration, under testosterone gel 24 to 49 percent. In the testosterone level itself, by contrast, the difference from the gel is practically zero. The real question remains open: whether men feel better on it was assessed by only 2 of 10 studies at all – and that is exactly why the US regulatory agency said no on December 1, 2015.

The problem it is meant to solve

Anyone who supplies testosterone from outside switches off their own production. The brain measures the level in the blood, sees plenty available and shuts down the control hormones: LH, luteinizing hormone, and FSH, follicle-stimulating hormone. Without these two, two things stop happening in the testes. They no longer make their own testosterone, and they no longer make sperm.

Hence the reports of infertility and shrinking testes under testosterone therapy. Once family planning is complete, that is an acceptable price; for those who want children, it is a problem. This is exactly the gap enclomiphene targets.

How it works and where it comes from

The brain does not actually measure the testosterone level directly. It mainly measures estrogen: in the body, testosterone is partly converted into estradiol, and this estradiol presses on the feedback switch. Enclomiphene blocks this switch. The brain believes there is too little and turns up LH and FSH. The testes receive more signal and produce more – their own testosterone and sperm along with it. So what is replaced is not the hormone but the signal.

The substance itself is not new. Clomiphene has been approved for decades, though for inducing ovulation in women, and consists of two mirror-image forms. Zuclomiphene stays in the body for a very long time and acts more like an estrogen; enclomiphene is the anti-estrogenic half with a short half-life. Enclomiphene is thus purified clomiphene without the part that gets in the way.

Important for putting this in context: the principle requires an intact axis between hypothalamus, pituitary and testes. Where the testes themselves can no longer do the job, a stronger signal does not help.

What is well supported

Unusual for this database: the mechanism does not need to be qualified here; it works the way it is described. There are two parallel, randomized, double-blind phase 3 trials in which the comparison group also received a dummy preparation of the other dosage form. 256 overweight men aged 18 to 60 with secondary hypogonadism completed them over 16 weeks, split into four groups: two enclomiphene groups, a testosterone gel and placebo. After 16 weeks, testosterone was around 430 to 445 ng/dl under enclomiphene, 350 to 387 under the gel and 214 to 236 under placebo. What matters is what happened to the control hormones: under enclomiphene, LH and FSH rose markedly, p below 0.001; under the gel, both were suppressed. This very difference shows up where it counts – in the sperm.

What the studies show

Two phase 3 trials, 256 men, 16 weeks

Randomized, double-blind and double-dummy, with testosterone gel and placebo as comparators. Sperm concentration rose slightly under enclomiphene, by 11 to 15 percent, and fell under the gel by 33 to 57 percent. Under enclomiphene, 2 to 5 percent of men fell below the critical threshold of 15 million per milliliter; under the gel, 24 to 49 percent. Side effects were reported in 21 percent, none severe, with no difference between groups.

Review of 10 randomized trials, 819 men

Pools clomiphene and enclomiphene together. Compared with placebo, total testosterone rose by 274 ng/dl, confidence interval about 192 to 356. Compared with testosterone gel, the difference was 5.4 ng/dl with a p-value of 0.83, so practically zero: both approaches raise the level equally well. For sperm, by contrast, the gap is huge, namely 70.4 million per milliliter in favor of the tablet. The quality of evidence by GRADE is moderate for testosterone, LH and FSH versus placebo, downgraded because of wide confidence intervals and high heterogeneity.

TRAVERSE: the benchmark it has to be measured against

For classic testosterone therapy there is a trial with 5,246 men aged 45 to 80, with symptoms and cardiovascular risk, followed for about 33 months on average. For the combined cardiovascular endpoint, the figures were 7.0 percent under testosterone versus 7.3 percent under placebo, hazard ratio 0.96. There are signals there too: arrhythmias requiring treatment 5.2 versus 3.3 percent, acute kidney injury 2.3 versus 1.5 percent. The difference in scale remains: 5,246 men over 33 months versus 256 men over 16 weeks.

Where the data stop

On December 1, 2015, the US regulatory agency sent the manufacturer a rejection letter. The reasoning: the design of the phase 3 trials was no longer suitable for demonstrating a clinical benefit. Added to this were three objections concerning inclusion criteria, dose titration and the validation of the measurement method. The objection seems unfair at first, because the trials showed exactly what they were meant to show. But what is on the label is a lab value: testosterone rises, LH rises, FSH rises, sperm are preserved. Nobody takes a tablet to change a number on a lab report. What would need to be measured is whether the man has more energy, more libido, less depressed mood, more muscle mass, fewer bone fractures.

That is exactly what remains open to this day. In the review, 2 of 10 studies assessed symptom questionnaires at all. One found no improvement in the general sexual function index compared with placebo; another found better scores on a questionnaire on age-related complaints and in sexual desire. The authors themselves call their conclusions on symptoms preliminary, and on safety they write that the analysis is underpowered despite everything. What remains is a clean mechanism, a shifted intermediate value and an open question about the outcome.

Status, approval and legal

Enclomiphene is not approved as a medicine anywhere, not even in the US. What is offered there runs through compounding, because the substance is a component of the approved clomiphene. In Germany, clomiphene is approved, but for inducing ovulation in women; there is no approval for men with low testosterone. Online, the trade works as with peptides: declared as a research chemical, not for human consumption. The US Department of Defense lists both substances on its prohibited list because they keep turning up as testosterone boosters in food supplements, which is illegal. And clomiphene is on the World Anti-Doping Agency’s prohibited list under hormone and metabolic modulators. For anyone who is tested anywhere, this is not a gray area but a hit.

Safety

In the phase 3 trials, side effects were reported in 21 percent of participants, none severe and with no difference between groups. That sounds good but says little: observation lasted 16 weeks in 256 men, and the review considers its own safety analysis underpowered. Reported are visual disturbances as a rare event, mood swings and headaches. This leads to a point regularly twisted in forums: anyone who chooses enclomiphene because it is supposedly safer than testosterone is choosing the substance with the weaker safety data – not because it is more dangerous, but because nobody has looked long enough. Sourcing via the gray market adds to this, with unverified origin and unverified content. Low testosterone needs to be investigated, because it has causes that should be found, and that investigation is done by a physician, not by a provider who sells the remedy along with it.

Frequently asked questions about enclomiphene

Does enclomiphene raise testosterone more than a testosterone gel?

In the phase 3 trials, levels were somewhat higher under enclomiphene; in the review of 10 studies, the difference was practically zero at 5.4 ng/dl with a p-value of 0.83. So both approaches raise the level equally well. The difference lies not in the height but in what happens to the testes.

Is fertility really preserved?

What is preserved is sperm concentration, and that is the most robust advantage of the substance. Under enclomiphene it rose by 11 to 15 percent; under the gel it fell by 33 to 57 percent. 2 to 5 percent of men fell below the critical threshold of 15 million per milliliter, compared with 24 to 49 percent under gel. Whether pregnancies actually succeed as a result was not studied.

Why was approval rejected?

On December 1, 2015, the US agency decided that the design of the phase 3 trials was no longer suitable for demonstrating a clinical benefit, and additionally objected to inclusion criteria, dose titration and the validation of the measurement method. What was demonstrated were lab values. Whether the men are better off as a result was not the question the trials answered.

Do you feel better on it?

After more than ten years, that is still open. Of 10 randomized trials, only 2 assessed symptom questionnaires at all, and the results contradict each other. The authors of the review themselves describe their conclusions on symptoms as preliminary.

Is enclomiphene relevant to doping?

Yes. Clomiphene is on the World Anti-Doping Agency’s prohibited list under hormone and metabolic modulators, and the US Department of Defense lists both substances on its prohibited list. Anyone competing in a tested environment must expect a positive finding.

Does it work for every man with low testosterone?

No. The principle requires an intact axis between hypothalamus, pituitary and testes, that is, secondary hypogonadism. Where the testes themselves are the cause, a stronger control signal achieves nothing. Low testosterone should therefore first be investigated by a physician.
