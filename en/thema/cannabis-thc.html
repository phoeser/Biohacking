Cannabis / THC

Main psychoactive compound of the cannabis plant (cannabinoid, acts at the CB1 and CB2 receptors); in Germany no longer a narcotic since April 1, 2024 – prescription-only as medical cannabis (MedCanG), permitted to a limited extent for adults as recreational cannabis (KCanG) · Tetrahydrocannabinol, Delta-9-THC, Δ9-THC, medical cannabis, cannabis flowers, cannabis extract, dronabinol, nabilone, nabiximols, Sativex, Canemes, marijuana, hashish, recreational cannabis

11 min read · 10 sources, checked November 7, 2026

In the BK-Score: Supported, with caveats – human evidence 6 out of 10, safety data 7 out of 10. There are several studies in humans, but they are small, short or inconsistent.

THC, short for tetrahydrocannabinol, is the main psychoactive compound of the cannabis plant and the reason why cannabis has long been used as a medicine and a recreational drug. In dozens of randomized trials, cannabinoids measurably relieve chronic pain and spasticity, but usually only to a small extent. Since April 1, 2024, cannabis is no longer a narcotic in Germany: doctors prescribe it on a normal prescription, and adults may possess it within narrow limits. The risks are also established, above all dependence and a higher risk of psychosis with daily use of high-potency strains.

What cannabis and THC are

The cannabis plant contains many cannabinoids. THC is the one that intoxicates. CBD (cannabidiol) is not intoxicating and CBN (cannabinol) is marketed mainly as a sleep aid; both have their own entries, this one is about THC and the whole plant. As a medicine, THC is available in several forms: as the pure substance dronabinol, as the synthetic derivative nabilone, as an oral spray with THC and CBD (nabiximols), and in cannabis flowers and extracts of pharmaceutical quality, whose content differs depending on the strain.

What is rated is the state of knowledge. It is broad: numerous randomized trials on pain, spasticity and nausea, an accompanying survey by the BfArM with around 21,000 reported treatments, and large epidemiological studies on the risks. More on pain can be found under Chronic pain, on sleep under Sleep & sleep hygiene.

How it works

THC activates the cannabinoid receptors CB1 and CB2. CB1 is found mainly in the brain and spinal cord, CB2 mainly in the immune system. These are the same receptors through which the body’s own endocannabinoids such as anandamide act. CBD, by contrast, does not activate them.

Via CB1, THC dampens the transmission of pain signals and muscle tension, counteracts nausea and stimulates appetite. The same target in the brain explains the high and the impairments in memory, attention and reaction. Desired effects and side effects therefore cannot be cleanly separated.

What is well supported

  • Chronic pain, with a small effect. A systematic review in JAMA of 79 randomized trials with 6,462 participants found moderate-quality evidence: at least 30 percent less pain was achieved by 37 percent on cannabinoids and 31 percent on placebo. A later meta-analysis in the BMJ of 32 trials with 5,174 pain patients confirms a small advantage: 10 percent more of those treated felt meaningful relief than on placebo.
  • Spasticity in multiple sclerosis and paraplegia. In 7 trials, spasticity on the Ashworth scale fell more than on placebo, also with moderate-quality evidence.
  • Nausea during chemotherapy. A complete response was achieved by 47 versus 20 percent on placebo, though from only 3 trials and with low-quality evidence.
  • Established in care. Since 2017, doctors can prescribe cannabis at the expense of statutory health insurance if the insurer approves. In the BfArM’s accompanying survey, symptoms improved in just under three quarters of cases according to the doctors’ reports.

What the studies show

Whiting 2015: the big overview

The review evaluated 79 randomized trials on ten uses, from nausea through pain and spasticity to anxiety, sleep and psychosis. Only 4 trials had a low risk of bias. Most showed an improvement, but it was not statistically significant in all trials. On the pain scale from 0 to 10, scores fell on average by 0.46 points more than on placebo. For weight gain in HIV, sleep disorders and Tourette syndrome there was only low-quality evidence. Short-term side effects, including serious ones, occurred more often on cannabinoids.

Wang 2021: how large the benefit for pain is

The meta-analysis included 32 trials with 5,174 adults, 29 of them against placebo, with cannabis taken by mouth or applied to the skin and observation periods of 1 to 5.5 months. Pain fell on average by 0.5 cm on a 10 cm scale, with moderate certainty of evidence. Physical function and sleep quality improved very slightly to slightly, with high certainty; emotional and social functioning did not. In trials of three months or longer, dizziness was considerably more common than on placebo.

What users report: the BfArM’s accompanying survey

Between 2017 and 2022, doctors had to report anonymized data on statutorily insured patients who received cannabis. 16,809 complete data sets were evaluated. The main conditions treated were chronic pain (76.4 percent), then spasticity (9.6 percent) and loss of appetite and wasting (5.1 percent). In just under 75 percent of cases the symptoms improved, in 70 percent quality of life. One third stopped before a year was up, mostly because of lack of effect (38.5 percent), less often because of side effects (25.9 percent). Those who received flowers were on average 45.5 years old, more than two thirds male, rated the success higher and reported a euphoric effect three times as often. There was no control group; the BfArM stresses that the data do not replace clinical trials.

Where the data stop

  • Small effects on pain. The relief averages about half a point on the ten-point scale. For individuals it can be more, on average it is little.
  • Flowers without approval trials. The meta-analysis on pain covers only cannabis taken by mouth or applied to the skin, no inhaled flowers. According to the German federal government, cannabis flowers are prescribed without a marketing authorization under medicines law, without evidence tested in approval trials in patients. In the accompanying survey, the amounts of THC with flowers were far above all scientifically studied dosages.
  • Sleep and appetite. Here the evidence is of low quality. In pain patients, sleep quality improved only slightly.
  • Long-term data from therapy. The controlled trials ran for a few months at most. What years of medical treatment achieve is shown only by observational data without a comparison group.

Status, approval and legal

Since April 1, 2024, cannabis is no longer a narcotic in Germany. The Cannabis Act split the rules into two laws. The Medical Cannabis Act (MedCanG) covers flowers, extracts and THC including dronabinol for medical purposes. Only physicians may prescribe, not dentists or veterinarians; it is dispensed only in pharmacies on prescription, since 2024 on a normal prescription instead of a narcotics prescription. Statutory health insurance pays after approval by the insurer.

A tightening is planned. The government draft of December 3, 2025, justifies it by the fact that imports of medical flowers rose by 170 percent from the first to the second half of 2024, but prescriptions reimbursed by statutory insurers by only 9 percent, and that telemedicine platforms arrange flowers after an online questionnaire without personal contact with a doctor. In future, flowers are to be prescribed only after personal contact in the practice or on a home visit, and follow-up prescriptions without new contact only if such contact took place in the last four quarters. Mail-order delivery of flowers to consumers is to be banned. As of November 2026, the law has not been passed; the Medical Cannabis Act applies in the version of June 20, 2024.

For adults aged 18 and over, the Consumer Cannabis Act (KCanG) regulates handling without a prescription. It is permitted to carry up to 25 g, up to 50 g at one’s place of residence and up to three live plants. Advertising and sponsorship are banned. Use is banned in the presence of minors, public use in and within sight of schools, children’s playgrounds, children’s and youth facilities and publicly accessible sports facilities, with the line of sight ending after 100 m, and in pedestrian zones between 7 a.m. and 8 p.m.

In road traffic, a limit of 3.5 ng/ml THC in blood serum applies; anyone who reaches it commits an administrative offence, and in combination with alcohol a higher fine is imposed. THC from taking a prescribed medicine as directed is exempt. In sport, according to the WADA list 2026, all natural and synthetic cannabinoids are banned in competition, with the exception of CBD.

Safety

Dependence is the most common serious risk. According to a meta-analysis of 21 studies, 22 percent of people who use cannabis meet the criteria for a cannabis use disorder and 13 percent those for dependence. In young people who use weekly or daily, the risk of dependence rises to 33 percent.

The second major risk concerns mental health. In the European EU-GEI study with 901 people with first-episode psychosis and 1,237 controls, daily use was associated with 3.2-fold and daily use of high-potency strains with at least 10 percent THC with 4.8-fold higher odds of psychosis. Assuming a causal relationship, 12.2 percent of first-episode psychoses could be prevented without high-potency cannabis, 30.3 percent in London and 50.3 percent in Amsterdam.

In the therapy trials, dizziness, dry mouth, nausea, fatigue, euphoria, confusion and balance problems are common, and serious side effects also occur more often than on placebo. For flowers, the BfArM urges that the risk of misuse and dependence be considered already when planning therapy, because of the high amounts of THC, the high proportion of men and the younger age of those treated.

Frequently asked questions about cannabis / THC

Is cannabis legal in Germany?

Since April 2024, cannabis is no longer a narcotic. Adults may carry up to 25 g, up to 50 g at their place of residence and three live plants. Medical cannabis is available only on a doctor’s prescription from a pharmacy.

Does cannabis help with chronic pain?

In studies yes, but usually only a little. On average, pain falls by about half a point on a ten-point scale, and around 10 percent more of those treated feel clear relief than on placebo. Dizziness and fatigue, on the other hand, are more common.

Does THC help with sleep?

Studies specifically on sleep disorders are few and of low quality. In people with chronic pain, sleep quality improved only slightly in studies.

Can cannabis trigger psychosis?

Daily use is associated with just over 3-fold, daily use of high-potency strains with almost 5-fold higher odds of psychosis. A case-control study cannot establish the cause with certainty, but the differences between eleven study sites are consistent with the use of high-potency strains.

Is cannabis addictive?

Just over one in five people who use cannabis develops a cannabis use disorder, about one in eight a dependence. In young people with weekly or daily use, it is one in three.

Can I drive after cannabis?

From 3.5 ng/ml THC in blood serum, driving is an administrative offence, and in combination with alcohol the fine is higher. THC from a prescribed medicine taken as directed is exempt.
