Shilajit

Mineral · Mumijo, fulvic acid mineral resin

Shilajit, also called mumijo, is a tar-like resin from mountain rock that has long been regarded as a rejuvenating remedy in Ayurvedic medicine. Today it is taken mainly for testosterone, energy and strength. There are a few controlled human studies on it with interesting results, but no broad data base yet.

In brief

In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial in men aged 45 to 55, total testosterone rose by 20.45 % from baseline after 90 days and was significantly above placebo. A second controlled trial found less strength loss after fatigue at 500 mg daily, though only in a subgroup. Both trials were funded by the manufacturer of the tested extract; no independent replication could be found. In the short term, shilajit was well tolerated in the studies. The real catch lies with the product: composition and purity vary, and thallium, among other things, has been found in products.

What it is

In mountain regions, shilajit seeps out of the rock as a dark brown to black, sticky mass. It consists of humic and fulvic acids, other organic compounds and minerals. There is no clear-cut definition: an assessment for the EU notes that mumijo is clearly defined neither in its origin nor in its composition.

Studies therefore mostly use a purified, standardized extract. The product from the most important testosterone study was standardized to at least 60 % bioactive constituents, of which at least 50 % were fulvic acids. What is sold as resin, powder or capsule does not automatically correspond to this.

How it is supposed to work

In the Ayurvedic tradition, shilajit is considered a rasayana, a remedy for rejuvenation and strengthening, including of male fertility. The modern studies build directly on this and measure hormones, sperm parameters and muscle strength.

The mechanism has barely been studied in humans. In the testosterone study, the pituitary hormone LH remained stable while FSH rose. This points to an effect on the hormonal axis but does not explain it. Also advertised are support for the mitochondria, recycling of coenzyme Q10 and a supply of trace elements. For these claims, no human data could be found in the research for this page. Studies on tissue samples show that after several weeks of intake, certain genes in muscle and skin are read differently, for example genes for connective tissue and small blood vessels.

Tradition and use

Shilajit is not a new scene product. Besides the long Ayurvedic tradition, its use in food supplements in Europe is documented: the Czech Republic reported it for the period before 1997, which is why shilajit in this form is not considered a novel food in the EU. Experience reports from verifiable sources that go beyond this tradition are not available for this page.

What is well supported

Best supported is the effect on testosterone in healthy middle-aged men. In a randomized, double-blind trial, men aged 45 to 55 took 250 mg of purified shilajit or placebo twice daily for 90 days. On day 90, total testosterone was 20.45 % above baseline and significantly above the placebo group, and free testosterone was 19.14 % above baseline. Enrolled were men who showed signs of testosterone deficiency symptoms on the ADAM questionnaire, that is, precisely the group for which the topic is practically relevant.

In addition, there are indications regarding strength and fertility. In a second controlled trial with 63 young men, the stronger participants lost less strength after a fatigue exercise on 500 mg daily than on placebo. And in 28 men with too few sperm, sperm count, motility and testosterone rose markedly from baseline after 90 days. Tolerability was good in all studies; liver and kidney values remained unchanged.

What the studies show

Testosterone study 2016: 90 days, placebo-controlled

Pandit and colleagues randomized 96 healthy men aged 45 to 55 to 250 mg shilajit twice daily or placebo. The 75 who completed the 90 days were analyzed. Total testosterone rose by 20.45 % from day 0 and on day 90 was significantly above placebo; in the placebo group it fell slightly. LH remained stable, FSH and DHEAS rose. The study was funded by the manufacturer of the extract.

Strength study 2019: 63 men, 8 weeks

Keller and colleagues gave 21 recreationally active men each 250 mg, 500 mg or placebo. After 8 weeks, 100 maximal leg extensions followed. In the stronger half of participants, strength loss was 8.9 % on 500 mg and 16.0 % on placebo, and a marker of collagen breakdown was lower. The lower dose had no effect. This study, too, was funded by the manufacturer.

Fertility study 2010: without a placebo group

Biswas and colleagues gave 35 men with oligospermia 100 mg of processed shilajit twice daily for 90 days; 28 completed the study. Compared with baseline, total sperm count rose by 61.4 % and testosterone by 23.5 %. Without a comparison group, the contribution of the substance cannot be separated from other influences.

Skin study 2019: 14 weeks in women

Das and colleagues gave healthy middle-aged women 125 mg or 250 mg shilajit or placebo twice daily for 14 weeks. On the higher dose, skin blood flow improved compared with baseline and placebo, and in skin samples genes for new blood vessel formation and connective tissue were more active. No side effects were reported. The study shows that shilajit has measurable tissue effects in humans, but it says nothing yet about noticeable changes in the skin.

Where the data stop

The data base is narrow. The two randomized trials on testosterone and strength were each funded by the manufacturer of the tested extract; no independent replication could be found. The testosterone study analyzed only the men who stayed until the end, 75 of 96. Whether libido, energy or performance change noticeably was not measured as an outcome there. The strength effect appeared only at 500 mg and only in the stronger half of participants. The figure of 23.5 % more testosterone that also circulates comes from the fertility study without a placebo group, not from the controlled trial.

Then there is the product question. The studies used standardized extracts; the market offers very different resins and powders. Another randomized trial on shilajit and resistance training appeared in 2026, but so far only as a preprint without peer review. For the advertised effect on mitochondria and coenzyme Q10, there are no robust figures in humans.

Status, approval and legal

Shilajit is available in Germany as a food supplement. An EU consultation handled by Germany concluded in 2020 that mumijo as or in food supplements is not a novel food, because it was already used this way in the Czech Republic before May 15, 1997. In all other foods it is considered novel and would need its own authorization. For competitive athletes, the general NADA notice on contamination of food supplements applies.

Safety

In the controlled studies, shilajit was well tolerated over 8 to 14 weeks and in the testosterone study over 90 days; liver and kidney values remained unchanged in the fertility study. There are no long-term data. The most important risk is purity: a 2025 laboratory analysis found thallium in raw shilajit and in products, up to 0.5 µg per gram in products and at most 0.095 µg per tablet, in some cases more than in the raw material. For comparison, the authors cite an average dietary intake of under 2 µg per day; other heavy metals have also been described in the literature. There are no data on pregnancy, breastfeeding, children or interactions. Because shilajit changed hormone levels in studies, intake in hormone-dependent conditions should be discussed with a physician.

Frequently asked questions about shilajit

How much shilajit was taken in the studies?

The testosterone study used 250 mg of a purified extract twice daily for 90 days, the strength study 250 or 500 mg daily for 8 weeks. The fertility study used 100 mg twice daily. This is a description of the studies, not a personal recommendation.

Does shilajit really raise testosterone?

In a controlled trial in men aged 45 to 55, total testosterone rose by 20.45 % after 90 days and was significantly above placebo. The study was funded by the manufacturer; no independent replication could be found. Whether this has a noticeable effect in everyday life was not measured.

Is shilajit resin better than capsules?

There is no comparison on this. The studies used standardized extracts with a defined proportion of fulvic acids. For any form, what matters most is whether the supplier documents purity and content through independent laboratory analyses.

Can shilajit be contaminated with heavy metals?

Yes. A 2025 analysis found thallium in raw shilajit and in products, in some products even more than in the raw material. The highest amount measured was 0.095 µg per tablet; the consequences of long-term intake have not been studied.

Does shilajit help build muscle?

No peer-reviewed study has shown muscle building. In a study with 63 men, the stronger participants on 500 mg daily lost less strength after fatigue. A larger training study from 2026 has not yet been peer-reviewed.

Is shilajit legal in Germany?

Yes, as a food supplement it may be sold without novel food authorization. In other foods such as drinks or bars, it is considered novel and would need its own authorization.
