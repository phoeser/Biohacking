Minoxidil

Potassium channel opener and vasodilator; as a solution or foam up to 5 % available without prescription in pharmacies, as a tablet prescription-only · Regaine, Alopexy, Lonolox, oral minoxidil, LDOM

Minoxidil comes in two forms with very different legal status. As a solution or foam on the scalp, it has been used for decades against hereditary hair loss and is supported by blinded studies; up to 5 % it is available without a prescription in pharmacies. As a tablet, it is approved in Germany only for hard-to-treat high blood pressure; for hair loss it is prescribed off-label. In the only blinded head-to-head comparison to date, the tablet was not superior to the foam, and it brings with it the side effects of a blood pressure drug.

What minoxidil is

Minoxidil was originally a blood pressure drug; increased hair growth is one of the most common side effects of the tablets. In Germany there are two routes today: solutions and foams with up to 5 % for use on the scalp in hereditary hair loss (androgenetic alopecia), and tablets that are approved only for high blood pressure that does not respond adequately to other drugs.

Unlike finasteride, minoxidil does not lower the hormone DHT; according to the summary of product characteristics, the increased hair growth under the tablet is not associated with a hormonal disorder. What is rated is the state of knowledge. For use on the scalp it is good: blinded studies in men and women, decades of use. For the low-dose tablet against hair loss, called “LDOM” in the scene, there is one blinded comparative study, one network meta-analysis and large retrospective analyses.

How it works

As a tablet, minoxidil lowers blood pressure by dilating the small arteries. This is done by its metabolite minoxidil sulfate, which opens potassium channels in the vascular muscle. The body reacts in the opposite direction: heart rate rises, and salt and water are retained. That is why, according to the summary of product characteristics, the tablet for high blood pressure is given together with a diuretic and a beta blocker.

How minoxidil stimulates hair growth is only partly understood despite more than 30 years of use. In animal experiments it shortens the resting phase of hair follicles so that they enter the growth phase earlier, and probably likewise in humans. It presumably also prolongs the growth phase and enlarges the follicles. Whether the same potassium channels play a role in this has not been shown so far.

What is well supported

  • The solution works better than placebo. In a blinded 48-week study with 393 men aged 18 to 49, the 5 % solution was superior to the 2 % solution and placebo, in hair counts as well as in the assessment of participants and investigators. Compared with 2 %, 45 % more hair regrew.
  • In women too. In 113 women with hereditary hair loss, 5 % foam once daily worked no worse after 24 weeks than 2 % solution twice daily, with less itching and flaking.
  • The tablet also acts on hair. In a network meta-analysis of 23 studies in men, oral minoxidil showed the largest increase in terminal hairs after 24 weeks. This is an indirect comparison across different studies.
  • Long use. Minoxidil has been widely used for decades. The side effects of the tablet are documented in the summary of product characteristics from the treatment of high blood pressure.

What the studies show

Olsen 2002: 5 % versus 2 % and placebo

Olsen 2002 (J Am Acad Dermatol) divided 393 men aged 18 to 49 with hereditary hair loss into three groups: 5 % solution (157), 2 % solution (158) or vehicle solution without active ingredient (78), each twice daily for 48 weeks. The 5 % solution was superior to both comparison groups in the number of non-vellus hairs in the target area and in the assessment of participants and investigators, and the response began earlier. Itching and local irritation occurred more often under 5 %; there were no signs of effects elsewhere in the body.

Blume-Peytavi 2011: foam in women

Blume-Peytavi 2011 (J Am Acad Dermatol) compared 5 % foam once daily with 2 % solution twice daily in 113 women over 24 weeks. The foam was non-inferior in hair count and hair thickness, with non-significantly better values, and caused skin intolerance less often, mainly itching and flaking. Because of the different dosage forms, the participants were not blinded.

Penha 2024: tablet versus solution

In a double-blind study at a Brazilian specialist clinic (JAMA Dermatol 2024), 90 men aged 18 to 55 received for 24 weeks either low-dose oral minoxidil plus a solution without active ingredient, or 5 % solution plus a dummy tablet; 68 completed the study. In the density of terminal hairs at the frontal area and crown, the groups did not differ significantly. Only the photo assessment favored the tablet at the crown (24 %, p = 0.04), not at the frontal area. Under the tablet, 49 % developed increased body hair growth (hypertrichosis) and 14 % headaches.

Vañó-Galván 2021: 1,404 patients in retrospect

The largest safety analysis of the low-dose tablet (J Am Acad Dermatol 2021) looks back across several centers at 1,404 patients, two thirds of them women. The most common side effect was hypertrichosis (15.1 %). Lightheadedness (1.7 %), fluid retention (1.3 %), palpitations (0.9 %), headache (0.4 %) and swelling around the eyes (0.3 %) were less common; 1.7 % discontinued because of side effects, and no life-threatening events were observed. The study is retrospective and has no control group.

Where the data stop

  • Whether the tablet is better. The only blinded head-to-head comparison with the solution showed no superiority in hair density. The network meta-analysis that puts oral minoxidil ahead compares indirectly across different studies.
  • The cardiac safety of the low dose. Pericardial effusion, fluid retention and angina pectoris are known from the treatment of high blood pressure. For the low dose against hair loss there are retrospective analyses without a control group, but no large controlled long-term studies.
  • How minoxidil acts on hair. After more than 30 years, the pathway at the follicle is only partly clarified. That potassium channels in the hair follicle are involved is an assumption, not proof.
  • Duration. The controlled studies cited ran for 24 to 48 weeks. They do not directly say what happens over many years.

Status, approval and legal

For use on the scalp in hereditary hair loss, minoxidil up to a concentration of 5 % is exempt from the prescription requirement (German Ordinance on Prescription-Only Medicines). As a medicine it remains pharmacy-only, meaning it is available only in pharmacies.

Tablets containing minoxidil are prescription-only. In Germany they are approved only for high blood pressure that does not respond adequately to maximum doses of other blood pressure drugs in combination, and should then be given together with a diuretic and a beta blocker. For hair loss, the tablet is an unapproved use and belongs in the hands of a physician.

Following a European procedure in 2024, the product information for the solutions and foams contains a warning: infants who came into contact with treated skin areas of their parents developed increased body hair growth. The packaging also states that the product must not be taken orally.

Safety

On the scalp, itching and local irritation are the most common side effects, more frequent under 5 % than under 2 %. Children should not come into contact with treated areas; in infants, the increased body hair growth regressed within months after contact ended.

For the tablet, the summary of product characteristics lists pericardial effusion in 3 to 5 % of treated hypertensive patients not on dialysis, occasionally with cardiac tamponade, as well as salt and water retention with edema, reflex tachycardia and possible angina pectoris. Contraindications include pheochromocytoma and pulmonary hypertension due to mitral stenosis. Increased hair growth on the face and body occurs in most hypertensive patients and regresses one to six months after stopping.

These figures come from the treatment of high blood pressure. In the retrospective analysis of the low dose against hair loss, side effects elsewhere in the body were rare; a control group was missing. The safety value of 7 in the BK-Score rates the state of the data, not the danger.

Frequently asked questions about minoxidil

Does minoxidil work against hair loss?

For use on the scalp, this is supported by blinded studies. In 393 men, the 5 % solution was superior to the 2 % solution and placebo after 48 weeks; in 113 women, 5 % foam once daily worked no worse than 2 % solution twice daily.

Is minoxidil as a tablet better than the solution?

Not in the only blinded head-to-head comparison to date, with 90 men. After 24 weeks, hair density did not differ significantly; only the photo assessment at the crown favored the tablet. Almost every second person on the tablet developed increased body hair growth.

Is the minoxidil tablet approved for hair loss?

No. In Germany, minoxidil tablets are prescription-only and approved only for hard-to-treat high blood pressure. For hair loss, the tablet is an unapproved use.

Do you need a prescription for minoxidil solution or foam?

No. For use on the scalp in hereditary hair loss, minoxidil up to 5 % is exempt from the prescription requirement. It remains a medicine, however, and is available only in pharmacies.

What side effects does oral minoxidil have?

In a retrospective analysis of 1,404 patients, increased body hair growth was the most common (15.1 %); lightheadedness, fluid retention and palpitations were less common. The summary of product characteristics for the blood pressure tablet also lists pericardial effusion and angina pectoris, observed in the treatment of high blood pressure.
