Uridine (uridine monophosphate)

Stress & mind · uridine monophosphate, UMP

Uridine is a nucleoside from which the body forms RNA and building blocks for cell membranes. In the nootropics scene it is regarded as a synapse building block, usually combined with choline and omega-3 fatty acids. This idea comes from the research around Richard Wurtman and was tested as the nutrient combination Fortasyn Connect in Souvenaid in early Alzheimer’s disease, with mixed results. There are almost no human data on uridine on its own.

In short

Via several intermediate steps, uridine provides the building block with which choline and fatty acids such as DHA are assembled into phosphatidylcholine, the main component of cell membranes. In gerbils, the combination of uridine monophosphate, choline and DHA raised membrane building blocks and synaptic proteins in the brain after four weeks. In humans, the same idea has only been tested as a mixture of eleven nutrients: in mild Alzheimer’s disease, memory improved in two trials, in moderate disease it did not, and in early Alzheimer’s disease the two-year trial missed its primary goal. The Cochrane analysis sees probably little difference in cognitive performance.

What uridine is

Uridine is a nucleoside made of the base uracil and the sugar ribose. The body produces it itself; it is found in breast milk, according to the Danish food authority at 2.2 mg per kilogram, and in most foods mainly as a component of RNA. As a food supplement, uridine 5′-monophosphate, UMP for short, is usually sold, often as the disodium salt.

Uridine is also encountered indirectly: citicoline is split in the body into choline and cytidine, and the cytidine is converted into uridine in humans. In infant formula, uridine monophosphate is permitted as one of five authorized nucleotide additives.

How it works

To build phosphatidylcholine, the nerve cell needs three ingredients from the blood: choline, a pyrimidine source such as uridine, which becomes CTP via UTP, and a polyunsaturated fatty acid such as DHA. Wurtman’s group showed in gerbils, whose pyrimidine metabolism resembles that of humans, that more of these precursors allows more membrane to form: phosphatidylcholine in the brain rose by 13 to 22 percent with uridine and choline or with DHA alone, and by 45 percent with all three. The synaptic proteins synapsin-1 and PSD-95 increased by 41 and 38 percent. In addition, UTP activates so-called P2Y receptors.

This is the basis of the combination of uridine, choline and omega-3 fatty acids known in the scene as the synapse stack. Whether more synapses form in humans as a result has not been measured. What was measured were memory tests, EEG and brain volume, and only under the mixture Fortasyn Connect.

What is well supported

The biochemistry is well supported: uridine is a precursor in the Kennedy pathway of membrane formation, and in animals the combination with choline and DHA measurably increases membrane building blocks and synaptic proteins.

In humans, it has been shown that a mixture with 625 mg of uridine monophosphate per day was well tolerated over months. In two randomized trials in mild, untreated Alzheimer’s disease, it performed better on a memory test than a control drink with the same taste.

What the studies show

Souvenir I (Scheltens 2010) – mild Alzheimer’s disease, 12 weeks

225 patients without Alzheimer’s medication drank Souvenaid or a control drink daily. On delayed recall of a word list, one of the two primary endpoints, the Souvenaid group was ahead after 12 weeks (p = 0.021). The second primary endpoint, an Alzheimer’s cognition test, and all other measures such as daily functioning and quality of life remained unchanged.

Souvenir II (Scheltens 2012) – mild Alzheimer’s disease, 24 weeks

Confirmatory trial in untreated patients with mild Alzheimer’s disease. The memory score of a neuropsychological test battery rose more over 24 weeks than under control (p = 0.023; Cohen’s d = 0.21). The EEG showed differences in functional connectivity in favour of the active group; for the overall score of the test battery there was only a trend (p = 0.053).

S-Connect (Shah 2013) – moderate Alzheimer’s disease under medication

527 patients who were already taking Alzheimer’s medication received Souvenaid or a control drink for 24 weeks. Cognitive performance declined equally in both groups, a difference of 0.37 points on the cognition test (p = 0.513). Side effects and blood values did not differ. The trial describes the mixture: uridine monophosphate, choline, phospholipids, EPA, DHA, vitamins E, C, B12 and B6, folic acid and selenium.

LipiDiDiet (Soininen 2017 and 2021) – early Alzheimer’s disease, up to 36 months

311 people with prodromal Alzheimer’s disease, that is, memory impairment without dementia, were assigned to Fortasyn Connect or a control drink; the trial was publicly funded. After 24 months, the primary endpoint missed significance (difference 0.098; p = 0.166), because the control group also declined much more slowly than expected. After 36 months, decline in the test score was 60 percent lower (p = 0.014), and on the dementia severity measure CDR-SB 45 percent lower (p = 0.014); only 81 participants with 36-month data were still included in this analysis.

Cochrane 2020 – Souvenaid in Alzheimer’s disease

Three randomized trials with 1,097 participants, with low risk of bias, two of them funded by the manufacturer. In early Alzheimer’s disease, there were not fewer cases of dementia after 24 months (RR 1.09; 95 % CI 0.82 to 1.43). For cognitive performance there was probably little or no difference; for combined measures of thinking and daily functioning the results were contradictory. Side effects were rare; the data were not sufficient for a statement on long-term tolerability.

Kondo 2011 – uridine alone in depression

The only human study found with uridine as a single substance in the neuropsychiatric field: an open-label case series with seven depressed adolescents with bipolar disorder, six weeks. The depressive symptoms declined, and uridine was well tolerated. Without a control group, no effect can be inferred from this.

Where the data stop

The gap is fundamental: all controlled human studies tested a mixture of eleven nutrients. What share uridine has in the result has not been investigated, nor whether uridine with choline and omega-3 alone achieves the same. For healthy people who want to improve their memory or concentration, there is not a single study.

The Alzheimer’s data are not clear-cut either. Positive memory findings come from short trials in mild disease, the largest trial under medication was negative, and the favourable 36-month finding from LipiDiDiet rests on a greatly shrunken group. That more synaptic proteins form in animals is a surrogate, not proof of better brain performance.

Status, approval and legal

Uridine is not a medicine in Germany. Souvenaid is marketed as a medical food for people with early Alzheimer’s disease. In infant formula, uridine 5′-monophosphate is permitted up to 1.75 mg per 100 kcal (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/127).

For food supplements, there is no official maximum amount and no authorized health claim. In 2020 the Danish food authority had uridine monophosphate assessed as a new nutrient for food supplements; the National Food Institute of the Technical University of Denmark could not rule out a health risk at 300 mg per day, because safety studies are lacking.

Safety

Harmful effects of uridine have not been described in the literature, but there are also hardly any data that would have looked for them. The Danish food institute found only a limited acute toxicity study in rodents; studies in animals with repeated dosing or in healthy people were not available. The Souvenaid trials with 625 mg of uridine monophosphate daily over up to 24 weeks showed no accumulation of side effects, but they were not designed to detect safety problems and were conducted in older patients, not in the general population.

Anyone who has depression or bipolar disorder or takes psychiatric medication should discuss uridine with a physician; the only data in this area come from a case series with seven adolescents. There are no studies on pregnancy and breastfeeding.

Frequently asked questions about uridine

What does uridine do for the brain?

Uridine is a precursor for membrane building blocks. In animals, the combination with choline and DHA increased synaptic proteins in the brain. In humans, this has only been tested as a mixture of eleven nutrients in Alzheimer’s disease, with mixed results. There are no studies in healthy people.

What is Souvenaid?

A drinkable nutrition product with the nutrient combination Fortasyn Connect: uridine monophosphate, choline, phospholipids, EPA, DHA, several vitamins and selenium. It has been tested in several randomized trials in Alzheimer’s disease and is marketed as a medical food.

Does Souvenaid help with Alzheimer’s?

In mild Alzheimer’s disease without medication, a memory measure improved in two trials; in moderate disease under medication, it did not. In early Alzheimer’s disease, there were not fewer cases of dementia after two years. The Cochrane analysis sees probably little difference in cognitive performance.

How are uridine, citicoline and alpha-GPC related?

All three are discussed for building membrane components. Citicoline provides choline and cytidine, which becomes uridine in humans; alpha-GPC provides choline. Uridine provides the pyrimidine component. There are no comparative studies between the three substances.

Is uridine safe?

Harmful effects have not been described, but they have hardly been studied. In the Souvenaid trials, 625 mg of uridine monophosphate per day was well tolerated. Even so, the Danish food authority could not rule out a risk at 300 mg per day from food supplements in 2020, because safety studies are lacking.
